\textbf{Purpose and status.}
This note summarizes an integral refinement of the maps studied in
Kumar--Rogers, Part II \cite{KR}, and Kumar--Xie, Part III \cite{KX}.
It separates published statements, deductions whose arguments are recorded
and checked in the companion repository, and unproved conjectures.
Deductions are presented with their arguments in the companion notes;
external expert review and originality remain unestablished.

\section{The published starting point}
\published Part II gives the classical symplectic and odd-orthogonal
generator images and stable injectivity: Theorem 16 for C and Theorem 29
for B. Part III gives the even-orthogonal generator images, its stable
integral presentation, and stable injectivity, especially Theorems 3.6 and
3.8 and Proposition 3.7. The injectivity theorems are stated over \(\C\);
their proofs explicitly use chosen integral forms.

Use the normalized torus coordinates
\(\bar t_j=(t_j-t_j^{-1})/2\), corresponding to \(x_j\) under the
Springer--Borel map. Put \(c_i=c_i(Q)\), where \(Q\) is the tautological
quotient bundle on the relevant isotropic Grassmannian, with \(c_0=1\)
and \(c_i=0\) outside its rank. The squared-tail generator image is
\[
 e_i(\bar t_{\rm tail}^{\,2})
 \longmapsto c_i^2+
 2\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^j c_{i+j}c_{i-j}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The integral theta/eta presentations and B/C Schubert comparison used
below are existing Schubert calculus \cite{BKTIG,BKTD}.

\section{The additional integral question}
Write \(n=a+k\). On
\(X_B=\OG(a,2n+1)\) use
\(q_i=e_i(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2)\), \(1\le i\le k\).
On \(X_D=\OG(a,2n)\) use \(q_1,\ldots,q_{k-1}\) and
\(p=x_{a+1}\cdots x_n\), with \(p^2=q_k\).
Take \(a,k\ge1\) in B and \(a\ge1,k\ge2\) in D.

Polynomial degree \(d\) means cohomological degree \(2d\);
\(\deg q_i=2i\) and \(\deg p=k\).
Let \(L_d\subset H^{2d}(X;\Z)\) be the integer span of all degree-\(d\)
monomials in these fixed inputs after applying the Borel map. The object
to determine is
\[
 \sat(L_d)/L_d,\qquad
 \sat(L_d)=(L_d\otimes\Q)\cap H^{2d}(X;\Z).
 \tag{2}
\]
We retain every nonzero Smith factor of \(L_d\hookrightarrow\sat(L_d)\),
including \(1\): this means the finite group (2) together with \(\rank L_d\).
The index is in the selected rational image, whose rank can be smaller
than ambient cohomology. Injectivity alone does not imply saturation.

\section{Structural deductions and finite type-B formulas}
\deduction The arguments recorded in \cite{Repo} yield the following
refinements of the image problem, in the normalization of Section 2.
They use published integral presentations; these exact Smith conclusions
are not attributed to those sources as published theorems.

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.32\textwidth}p{0.29\textwidth}p{0.29\textwidth}@{}}

Stable saturation strengthens the injectivity established in the papers:
the normalized image contains every integral class in its rational span.
The finite ranges are sufficient, not claimed optimal.
In B, the full Smith multiset is also unchanged under \(a\leftrightarrow k\).

\paragraph{Why the B comparison controls the prime two.}
For the same squared-root Schur inputs, let \(C\) be the integral
symplectic coefficient matrix. Its selected image is primitive by the
quaternionic Grassmannian fibration and integral Schur basis
\cite{PR}. For signed Schubert indices \(w\),
\[
 B_{w,\lambda}=2^{\nu(w)}C_{w,\lambda},
 \qquad \nu(w)=\#\{\text{negative signed entries}\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
After block duality, \(\nu\le\ell:=\min(a,k)\). As the full C row lattice
is primitive, the B row lattice lies between \(2^\ell\Z^R\) and \(\Z^R\),
where \(R\) is the source rank in the degree under consideration.
This gives the B exponent bound. The stable B/D saturation arguments
instead use integral specialization of the theta/eta models.
In D, working over \(\Z[1/2]\) with a sign-averaging retraction rules out
odd-prime factors.
For later use, in the smaller-head presentation define the prefix lattice
\(P_t=\operatorname{span}_{\Z}\{C_{w,\bullet}:\nu(w)\le t\}\subseteq\Z^R\),
with \(P_{-1}=0\). If every prefix is primitive, the multiplicity of
\(2^t\) is \(\rank P_t-\rank P_{t-1}\).

\paragraph{A concrete closed type-B family.}
Set \(A=\max(a,k)\). For \(\ell=2\), put
\(u=\lceil A/2\rceil\), \(v=\lfloor A/2\rfloor\).
In polynomial degree \(2m\), the complete multiset is
\[
 \left\{2^{e(r,s)}:0\le s\le r\le A,\ r+s=m\right\},
 \qquad
 e(r,s)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&r\le u,\ s\le v,\\
 2,&s>u,\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
The proof uses two-variable plethysm, theta Giambelli, and an integral
change of basis with disjoint Schubert supports. For \(\ell=3\), all four
multiplicity polynomials likewise have closed formulas, derived through
primitive Schur-product prefixes \cite{Repo}. For example, \(a=k=2\)
in polynomial degree \(4\) has factors \((1,2)\): the image has index
\(2\) in its rank-two saturation.

\section{Unit factors, classical counting, and the largest factor}
For type B put
\[
 \ell=\min(a,k),\quad A=\max(a,k),\quad
 r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\quad c=\lceil n/2\rceil.
\]
Let \(M_e(m)\) count factors \(2^e\) in polynomial degree \(2m\),
equivalently cohomological degree \(4m\).
The variable \(q\) below counts \(m\); it is not a generator \(q_i\).
The Gaussian \(\qbinom{v}{u}\) counts partitions in the rectangle
\((v-u)^u\), and is zero outside \(0\le u\le v\). Set \(U_{0,0}=1\).

\subsection*{The polynomial is already known; its Smith interpretation is additional}
\published The polynomial
\[
 U_{n,\ell}(q)=
 \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor}
 q^{j(c-\ell+j)}
 \qbinom{r}{j}\qbinom{c-j}{\ell-2j}
 \tag{5}
\]
is classical Birkhoff/Hall-type enumeration \cite{Birkhoff,Sage}.
For ambient type \((2^r,1^{n\bmod2})\) and subgroup type
\((2^j,1^{\ell-2j})\), the standard subgroup formula gives its summand.
The same expression counts invariant subspaces of a maximal-rank
square-zero operator. It is not a new counting polynomial.

\deduction The integral comparison in the companion notes identifies
\[
 \sum_m M_0(m)q^m=U_{n,\ell}(q).
 \tag{6}
\]
This is the additional Smith assertion, rather than Birkhoff's theorem.
A brief account of the argument is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Modulo \(2\), only the \(\nu=0\) rows of (3) remain.
Littlewood's two-quotient identity identifies their row lattice with
a bounded Littlewood--Richardson product lattice.
\item Let \(W=\{U\in\Gr(\ell,n):NU\subset U\}\), with
\(N^2=0\) of rank \(r\). A bounded affine-lattice model realizes \(W\)
as a closed union of Iwahori Schubert cells; the rectangular case is
already established in \cite{HNS}.
\item Anderson's integral Chern-generation theorem \cite{Anderson}
makes \(H^*(\Gr(\ell,n);\Z)\to H^*(W;\Z)\) surjective.
A Grassmann-bundle resolution, with degree-one sections over cells,
has primitive cohomology pullback. Its Schur coproduct is the LR matrix
above. Thus that row lattice is primitive, and its rank is (5).
\end{enumerate}
The complete integral argument, including the odd-\(n\) lattice bounds,
is supplied in \cite{Repo}; this paragraph is a proof sketch.

\subsection*{A separate all-rank endpoint deduction}
An incidence resolution and integral Pieri straightening identify the
prefix \(P_{\ell-1}\) with the coordinates indexed by
\(\lambda_\ell\le c-1\). The complementary partitions have every part
at least \(c\), giving
\[
 \boxed{\ \sum_m M_\ell(m)q^m
       =q^{\ell c}\qbinom{r}{\ell}.\ }
 \tag{7}
\]
This argument does not require primitivity of every intermediate prefix.

\newpage
\section{Relation to the Euler generator in Part III}
\deduction The Euler sector on \(\OG(a,2n)\), generated by \(p f(q)\),
has the same nonzero Smith factors as the normalized B image on
\(\OG(a-1,2n-1)\), shifted by polynomial degree \(k\) for \(a\ge2\).
The proof uses the explicit difference basis in BKT Proposition 2
\cite{BKTD}; its rational Chern-linearity upgrades integrally because
the basis correspondence and the multiplication maps are integral.

Combining the square and Euler sectors requires further integral data.
A swapped pair of typed Schubert classes has sum and difference of
index \(2\). The companion two-flag lattice lemma describes this gluing:
a paired sector-exponent pair \((i,j)\) contributes exponents
\[
 \bigl(\min(i,j),\ \max(i,j)+1\bigr),
 \tag{8}
\]
with the pairing determined by relative flags over \(\F_2\), not by the
sector Smith lists alone. This is a lattice reconstruction, not a full
type-D partition classification.
The observed equality of the general D square sector with the B image
remains conjectural here, as does a complete type-D partition formula.

\paragraph{Normalization matters.}
These statements concern the chosen normalized integral forms appearing
in the proofs of Parts II and III. They need not equal the literal
intersection with the integral representation ring.
For the defining/vector SO/Sp character forms discussed in Part I
\cite{K}, a degree-\(d\) image is \(2^d\) times its normalized counterpart.
Consequently each nonzero Smith factor is multiplied by \(2^d\).
No such conclusion is asserted here for a Spin character lattice.

\section{The remaining type-B conjecture}
\conjectural Outside the justified cases, the proposed complete formula is
\[
 \boxed{\ \sum_m M_e(m)q^m
 \stackrel{?}{=} q^{ec}\qbinom{r}{e}\,
                 U_{n-2e,\ell-e}(q),
       \qquad 0\le e\le\ell.\ }
 \tag{9}
\]
Recompute the parameters of \(U\) at its displayed arguments.
This includes (6) and (7), agrees with all multiplicities for
\(\ell\le3\), and has one further justified layer at \(\ell=4,e=3\).

For a maximal-rank square-zero operator, define
\(\delta(U)=\rank(N:U\to V/U)\). The right side of (9) is the
compactly supported even Poincare polynomial of \(\delta(U)=e\),
by an elementary stratification in the companion notes.
Over finite fields this is the profile \((\ell,e)\) counting problem
already covered by Ram's general theorem \cite{Ram}.
The unresolved step is its identification with the intermediate
\emph{integral Smith} layers, including the required prefix-rank and
primitivity comparisons. Counting the strata alone does not prove (9).

\textbf{Evidence and limits.}
The formula matches 174 recorded exact Smith lists and 430 prefix
comparisons. A separate verifier checks 516 exact integer row-module
equalities. These finite checks support the conjecture but do not prove
it in arbitrary rank. No originality claim is made for any deduction.
The most useful points for further review are the integral comparison
behind (6) and the missing intermediate-layer comparison in (9).
