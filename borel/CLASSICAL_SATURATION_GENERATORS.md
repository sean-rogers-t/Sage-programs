# Geometric denominators and a proposed short presentation

**29 October 2026.** Sections 1--3 are deductions from the existing
integral prefix theorem. Section 4 is an unresolved stronger statement.
Finite exact ideal tests support it but do not prove it. The literature
comparisons in Section 6 do not establish priority.

## 1. Scope and the geometric denominator formula

Put A>=ell>=1, n=A+ell and c=ceil(n/2). Let

    R = Z[e_1,...,e_ell]/(h_(A+1),...,h_(A+ell)).

Its variables y are formal squared head roots. Weight m in R becomes
cohomological degree 4m under the normalized type-B characteristic map
phi on OG(ell,2n+1). The tail convention is recovered by inversion of
total squared Chern classes. Write I=phi(R) and

    S = H*(OG(ell,2n+1);Z) intersect (I tensor Q).

The map phi is injective on R, so it identifies S with a lattice in R_Q.
The same discussion applies to the D square sector through the earlier
integral row-lattice comparison. It is not a statement about the full
D Euler sector or every integral Levi-character source.

Let N be a maximal-rank square-zero endomorphism of C^n, and put

    W_t = {U in Gr(ell,n): rank(N:U -> C^n/U)<=t}.
    K_0=R,
    K_e=ker(R -> H*(W_(e-1);Z)),  1<=e<=ell,
    K_(ell+1)=0.

The degree in this restriction is ordinary degree 2m, not 4m.
The [existing geometric comparison](TYPE_B_DEFECT_COMPARISON.md) and
[general prefix proof](TYPE_B_GENERAL_SMITH.md) identify this kernel
with the coordinate annihilator of P_(e-1). In particular each K_e is
an integral ideal with torsion-free quotient.

**Denominator theorem.** Under phi_Q,

    S = sum_(e=0)^ell 2^(-e) K_e  inside R_Q.                 (1)

Proof: choose the simultaneous dual basis f_gamma of
[the adapted-basis note](CLASSICAL_ADAPTED_SMITH_BASIS.md), with entrance
exponents d_gamma. Exact coordinate duality gives

    K_e = direct_sum_(d_gamma>=e) Z f_gamma.

The right side of (1) therefore has basis 2^(-d_gamma) f_gamma.
These are precisely the saturation basis established there. This also
proves the equality integrally, not only after tensoring with Q.

Thus the old Smith multiplicities now have an intrinsic ring-level
interpretation: the allowable denominator 2^e is controlled by vanishing
on the lower defect locus W_(e-1). Formula (1) is an explicit constructive
corollary of the old theorem, not an independently new multiplicity result.
S is a subring because it is the intersection of an integral cohomology
ring with a rational subalgebra. We do NOT infer K_i K_j subset K_(i+j)
from this: denominator cancellation alone gives only a weighted condition.

## 2. A uniform family of elements in every K_e

For a partition nu, m_nu denotes the monomial symmetric polynomial,
with each distinct monomial occurring once. Define the ideal

    J_e = (m_nu : length(nu)>=e, all nu_i>=c) in R,
    J_0=R.

It suffices to use parts c<=nu_i<n and length(nu)<=ell. Indeed after
pullback to the full ordinary flag variety every root y_i satisfies
y_i^n=0: its characteristic polynomial is T^n. That pullback is
injective, so monomials involving an exponent >=n vanish in R.

**Inclusion theorem.** J_e subset K_e in every rank.

Use the symplectic incidence model Q_t of
[the prefix proof](TYPE_B_FILTRATION.md), with t=e-1 and b=ell-t.
The source roots there split as

    y = (x_1^2,...,x_b^2) + (u_1,...,u_t),

where the x_i are Chern roots from the ordinary base Gr(b,n), and
the u_i are the remaining quaternionic characteristic roots. Pull back
to flag bundles splitting both sets of roots. These pullbacks are
injective over Z by their integral Leray--Hirsch bases. On the flag
bundle over the ordinary base, x_i^n=0, by completing its tautological
flag to a full flag of the trivial rank-n bundle.

In any term of m_nu there are at least e distinct variables carrying
exponents >=c. Only t=e-1 of them can be u variables. Some x_i^2 must
therefore carry such an exponent, giving x_i^(2nu_j)=0 because 2c>=n.
Every term vanishes; no averaging or division by a factorial is used.
The source coefficient rows on Q_t are exactly P_t, so m_nu belongs
to K_e. Since K_e is an ideal, its R-multiples also belong to K_e.

Consequently

    phi(m_nu)/2^length(nu) is integral.                       (2)

The smaller range c<=nu_i<=A gives the primitive divided-power corner
already proved in the adapted-basis note. Allowing A<nu_i<n adds
integral classes but does not assert that these are basis vectors.

## 3. Integral algebra of the proposed generators

In the symmetric polynomial ring before imposing Grassmannian relations,
the ideal generated by high-only m_nu of length >=e is the Z-span of
all m_lambda having at least e parts >=c.

To prove this, products cannot remove high parts: overlapping factors
add nonnegative exponents. Conversely split lambda into its high and
low parts. The disjoint-variable term in m_high m_low is m_lambda with
coefficient one, because the two sets of exponents are disjoint. All
other terms have fewer low parts and still at least e high parts.
Induction on the number of low parts proves the assertion. This proof
retains integer coefficients and works in any fixed number of variables.

In R a stronger product rule holds for high-only partitions:

    m_lambda m_mu = product_j binom(a_j+b_j,a_j) m_(lambda union mu),

where a_j,b_j are the multiplicities of j. Overlapping-variable terms
vanish since their exponent is at least 2c>=n. The right side is zero
if the union has length >ell. In particular J_i J_j subset J_(i+j).

Over Q, J_e is the e-th power of J_1: factorials allow high monomials
to be expressed as products of power sums. Over Z this inference is
invalid; the divided monomials must be retained. This is exactly the
arithmetic visible in the proved divided-power corner.

There is nevertheless a uniform finite generating range independent of
the larger block A. Every J_e is generated over R by the m_nu with

    e<=length(nu)<=ell,   c<=nu_i<=c+ell-1.                 (3a)

For a proof, apply the monic characteristic polynomial
T^ell-e_1 T^(ell-1)+...+(-1)^ell e_ell to each selected root.
It expresses y_i^d, for every d>=c, as an R-linear combination of
y_i^c,...,y_i^(c+ell-1). Substitute these expressions in every term
of m_nu. The resulting expression is still symmetric, with integral
coefficients in the formal e_j, and all selected exponents lie in the
stated range. Grouping its distinct monomials into symmetric orbits
requires no division by a stabilizer or a factorial. This proves (3a).

It follows that T=R+sum_(e>=1) 2^(-e)J_e is an integral subring of S
generated as an R-module by at most binom(2ell,ell) divided monomials,
including 1. Their lengths range from zero to ell and their parts lie
in the ell-element interval (3a). The collision-free product rule proves
ring closure. Equality T=S remains equivalent to the required lattice
generation, and is proved below only in the specified cases.

## 4. The remaining presentation conjecture

**Conjecture:** J_e=K_e for all A>=ell, 1<=e<=ell.            (3)

The top case e=ell is already proved uniformly. The established basis
of K_ell is precisely the collection m_nu with ell parts in [c,A],
so it is contained in J_ell. Section 2 supplies the reverse inclusion.
More explicitly,

    K_ell=J_ell=(e_ell^c).

Indeed subtracting c from all ell parts factors every high full-length
monomial by (y_1...y_ell)^c. Thus the entire highest-denominator layer
is generated over R by the single integral class phi(e_ell^c)/2^ell.
This also resolves all e when ell=1.

The [two-head theorem](CLASSICAL_TWO_HEAD_GENERATORS.md) now proves
all e for ell=2 in every rank by a monic integral Groebner calculation.
Its saturation has R-module generators 1,p_c/2,p_(c+1)/2,e_2^c/4.
The remaining open part is ell>=3 and 1<=e<ell.

If (3) holds, the entire saturation S is generated as an R-module by

    m_nu/2^length(nu),   c<=nu_i<n,

including the empty partition. It is also generated as an R-algebra
by these elements, since (1) supplies the module equality and S is a
ring. This would replace adapted-basis inversion with short symmetric
function generators and identify the exact integral cohomology
presentation R/J_e of every defect variety W_(e-1).

What is missing is the reverse inclusion K_e subset J_e. The established
rank and primitivity of K_e do not prove that J_e is primitive. A rational
Hilbert-series match, if established, would still leave this issue.
An adequate proof could supply a monic integral straightening rule for
R/J_e, with the known defect-variety rank in every degree. It must handle
all primes, not merely invert 2 or the factorials of part multiplicities.

## 5. Exact tests and their limits

[The ideal test](classical-kernel-generator-test.json) checks all degrees
and every e for nine block pairs. It verifies actual Z-module equality
using generators m_nu times rectangular Schur classes; it does not merely
compare ranks or Smith counts. Every tested ideal equals K_e.
[The saturation test](classical-divided-generator-test.json) separately
checks the resulting R-module generation modulo 2. Since the tested
module contains the original image and its saturation quotient is
2-primary, full rank modulo 2 proves equality in each of those finite
instances. Neither report proves (3) in arbitrary rank.

## 6. Literature leads and boundaries

[Adem--Reichstein, *Bundles, Cohomology and Truncated Symmetric
Polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4799), and
[Galetto, *Generators of truncated symmetric polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6068)
study closely related symmetric truncation ideals. Their ambient
algebras are not automatically the Grassmannian quotient R used here;
the additional quotient and its torsion require a separate argument.

[Brundan--Ostrik, *Cohomology of Spaltenstein varieties*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.3426)
provides a presentation over C for flags satisfying N V_i subset V_(i-1).
This is not the stable-plane condition N U subset U defining our W_0,
and it is not an integral presentation theorem for all W_t. Its
recollection of the Tanisaki presentation suggests a connection with
two-column Springer fibers, but no integral contraction identity is
assumed here.

[Bianchi--Wilson, *Bases for local Weyl modules for the hyper and
truncated current sl2-algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00145)
gives characteristic-free local-Weyl bases in Section 5. Section 6's
reverse-order and truncated-module applications explicitly assume
characteristic zero; Section 7.2 explains the nonunit-leading-coefficient
obstacle. Thus the attractive Demazure/current-algebra analogy cannot
be cited as an integral proof of (3). An explicit compatible integral
identification would be additional work.

These are identified proof routes, not assertions that (3) is new or
that those papers leave it open. The next decisive advance is a uniform
integral straightening argument or a counterexample, not another table.

## 7. A sharp limit on a tempting generalization

Replacing squares by arbitrary powers is not a formal extension of (3).
For example, take R=H*(Gr(2,4);Z) and the root-cubing endomorphism psi_3.
The analogous threshold is ceil(4/3)=2. The ideal generated by all
high-only monomials has quotient

    Z[e_1,e_2]/(e_1^2-2e_2, e_1 e_2, e_2^2),

which is free with basis 1,e_1,e_2. But psi_3(e_2) has weight six,
above the top weight four of R, so e_2 is in the actual kernel.
Thus the analogous ideal is strictly smaller than ker(psi_3).
This elementary counterexample limits the proposed presentation to the
square-root setup underlying the B/C/D comparison; it is not a theorem
for arbitrary Adams powers or arbitrary representations.

## 8. An exact exterior-power reformulation of the open step

There is a uniform linear-algebra model that retains the integral issue.
Set V=Z[z]/(z^n), let T be multiplication by z, and identify R as a
free Z-module with exterior^ell(V) by the alternant map

    s_lambda -> z^(lambda_ell) wedge z^(lambda_(ell-1)+1)
                wedge ... wedge z^(lambda_1+ell-1).

Use zeros to pad lambda. The order fixes the otherwise irrelevant common
sign. Multiplication by a symmetric polynomial acts by multiplication
on the ell tensor variables before antisymmetrization. The map is
integral: rectangular Schur polynomials map bijectively to the standard
wedge basis; a Schur function outside the rectangle has an exponent
at least n, or has too many rows, and maps to zero. No division by ell!
is involved.

Introduce independent variables a_c,...,a_(n-1) and define

    A(a)=sum_(j=c)^(n-1) a_j T^j,
    U(a)=exterior^ell(1+A(a)).

Since 2c>=n, A(a)A(b)=0 for any two sets of coefficients. The coefficient
of product_j a_j^(m_j(nu)) in U(a) is exactly the operator of multiplication
by m_nu in R. This follows by expanding the wedge: each chosen slot
receives one T^j, and each distinct assignment occurs once. Consequently

    J_e = sum_(total a-degree>=e) image_Z([a^alpha] U(a)).    (4)

This is an unconditional equality for J_e, not an assertion of (3).
It reformulates the missing theorem as an integral image problem for
the exterior power of one explicit square-zero Toeplitz matrix family.
It uses a fixed construction in every rank, rather than separate Levi
calculations. Over Q these coefficient operators can be described by
ordinary powers of commuting derivations; over Z their divided-power
coefficients are essential.

Formula (4) gives a second proof of the settled top layer. The coefficient
of a_c^ell is exterior^ell(T^c), whose image is all of
exterior^ell(span(z^c,...,z^(n-1))) integrally. Every top-degree coefficient
has its image in that same direct summand. It has rank binom(n-c,ell),
the already established rank of K_ell. The inclusion J_ell subset K_ell
and primitivity of this direct summand imply equality.

For intermediate e, the unresolved statement is that the sum in (4)
is primitive with the known K_e rank, degree by degree. Replacing the
whole family by one generic matrix, replacing divided powers by ordinary
powers, or arguing only over Q would leave a real gap. This is the next
specific proof path proposed by the present work.
