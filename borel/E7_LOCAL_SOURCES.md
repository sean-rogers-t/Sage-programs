# Complete two-local sources for the D5+A1 and D4+A1 Levi subgroups

Let R=Z_(2), Q=Q(E7), and X=X(T), with Bourbaki's E7 numbering. All
degrees below are polynomial degrees. These are full invariant sources,
before applying the Borel map. Their images and the relative cokernels
are separate calculations. No literature-priority claim is made.

## Exact coefficient rings

Put h=omega_6, w=omega_7, z=omega_5, b=2z, p=w-z, and

\[
 (u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4,u_5)=
 (\omega_1,\omega_3-\omega_1,\omega_4-\omega_3,
 \omega_2+\omega_5-\omega_4,\omega_5-\omega_2).
\]

Then sum u_i=b. The characters h,p,u_1,...,u_5 form an R-basis of Q_R.
The D5 Weyl group permutes Y_i=2u_i-h and changes an even number of
their signs. The A1 reflection s_7 fixes h and the u_i, and sends
p to h-b-p. Thus

\[
 r=p(h-b-p),\qquad t=w(h-w)=r+hb/2-b^2/4.
\]

Taking S5 permutation invariants and then the commuting A1 invariants
gives the full polynomial test ring

\[
 P_5=R[h,b,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,r],\qquad c_j=e_j(u),
\]

with weights (1,1,2,3,4,5,2). The full D5+A1 invariant source A_5 is a
saturated graded submodule of P_5. Its characteristic-zero Hilbert series
is

\[
 H_5(q)=\frac1{(1-q)(1-q^2)^2(1-q^4)(1-q^5)(1-q^6)(1-q^8)}. \tag{1}
\]

In the D5 **root** certificate files, the variable printed as `z` means
b=2omega_5. In the weight coordinates above, z means omega_5. The D4
root files print b as `b`. These aliases matter when evaluating the
explicit coefficient polynomials.

For D4+A1, retain {2,3,4,5,7}, and put y=u_1=omega_1. Permute the
remaining four u_i and let c_j be their elementary symmetric functions.
Their sum is b-y. The analogous full test ring is

\[
 P_4=R[h,y,b,c_2,c_3,c_4,r],
\]

with weights (1,1,1,2,3,4,2), and the rational full-invariant Hilbert
series is

\[
 H_4(q)=\frac1{(1-q)^2(1-q^2)^2(1-q^4)^2(1-q^6)}. \tag{2}
\]

These test rings are exact integral rings. For example, over a coefficient
domain, R[p] is free on 1,p over R[p(a-p)]. Invariance under p -> a-p
forces the p coefficient to vanish, so its invariant ring is R[p(a-p)].
Apply this with a=h-b, together with the ordinary symmetric-polynomial
theorem. Fresh root/weight determinant and reflection checks appear in the
[D4+A1 generator audit](experiments/e7-d4a1-generators-audit.json) and the
preceding [D5+A1 lattice audit](experiments/e7-d5a1-moments-audit.json).

## A general leading-monomial certificate

Here is the source-completeness argument used in these calculations.
It applies more generally to a saturated graded R-submodule A of a
weighted polynomial ring P, when the ranks of A^d are known and the
chosen generators belong to A and form a subalgebra C.

Saturation implies that A/2A injects into P/2P: an invariant polynomial
whose coefficients are all even can be divided by two and remains an
integral invariant. Let D be the image of C in P/2P. Select homogeneous
polynomials of D and let S be the additive monoid generated by their
leading exponents, for a fixed monomial order. Then

\[
 H_{\mathbb F_2[S]}(q)\ \le\ H_D(q)\ \le\ H_{A\otimes\mathbb Q}(q)
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

coefficient by coefficient. The first inequality holds because the
initial algebra of D contains the monomial algebra F2[S]. If the first
and last Hilbert series are equal as rational functions, all dimensions
in (3) are equal in every degree. The chosen integral generators
therefore surject onto A^d/2A^d. Degreewise Nakayama gives C^d=A^d for
every d. This proves generation in all degrees.

The criterion works over Z_(p) for any prime p, replacing two and F2 by
p and F_p. It uses reductions of actual integral invariants. These need
not be all invariants of the modular representation P/pP when p divides
the group order; no equality with that larger modular invariant ring is
assumed. This general criterion is standard Hilbert and local algebra
applied to explicit product witnesses.

The leading order used here first compares weighted degree. Within a
degree, the smallest first-variable exponent wins, then the smallest
second-variable exponent, and so on. Equivalently, use weighted reverse
lexicographic order with the variable list reversed. Positive weights
make this a genuine monomial order.

The finite polynomial calculations provide witnesses for the required
leading exponents. Their degree bound only describes these witnesses;
the rational-function identity in (3) proves the unbounded conclusion.

## Computing the entire monoid Hilbert series

The certificate includes pure powers p_i e_i in S for every coordinate.
Let B=F2[x_1^(p_1),...,x_s^(p_s)]. For each residue vector
0<=a_i<p_i, define the monomial ideal I_a in B by

\[
 x^{a+p\nu}\in\mathbb F_2[S]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X^\nu\in I_a.
\]

Each I_a is given by its finite set of minimal exponent vectors. The
certificate supplies nonnegative monoid expressions for every minimum.
It also checks closure under every monoid generator and includes the
unit. These checks prove that the listed residue ideals describe exactly
S. In particular, the residue ideals retain the holes of the monoid.

Consequently

\[
 H_{\mathbb F_2[S]}(q)
 =\sum_a q^{\deg a}H_{I_a}(q),
 \qquad \deg X_i=p_i\deg x_i.                         \tag{4}
\]

The primary calculation uses lcm-lattice inclusion/exclusion for each
monomial ideal. The independent audit uses recursive slices in a
different variable order. Both verify the full integer polynomial
identity obtained by clearing the denominators in (3).

## D4+A1: a complete source theorem

The 18 actual integral generators have degrees

```
1,1,2,2,4,4,4,6,6,6,8,8,8,9,10,12,14,16.
```

They generate the full ring (Sym_R Q_R)^(W_D4 x W_A1).
Their explicit coefficient polynomials and rational invariant preimages
are in the [source data](experiments/e7-d4a1-sources.json).

The proof uses 18 leading-polynomial witnesses in degrees at most 16.
Their pure-coordinate periods are (1,1,2,2,2,2,8). The resulting
polynomial parameter weights are (1,1,2,4,6,8,16). Of the 128 possible
residue vectors, 16 occur; their monomial ideals have 21 minima in total.
Formula (4) agrees identically with (2). The
[independent all-degree source audit](experiments/e7-d4a1-initial-source-audit.json)
checks the actual product witnesses, their leading terms, monoid
expressions and closure, and the entire Hilbert series.

## D5+A1: the degree-23 correction and the orbit norm

The complete source through degree 22 had 23 algebra generators.
Their algebra misses one dimension in degree 23. The additional explicit
integral invariant is packaged with its exact construction and Borel
coordinates in the [degree-23 certificate](experiments/e7-d5a1-extra23-audit.json).
The fixed transcript starts at the independently verified degree-22
coefficient basis. Every subtraction and coefficientwise exact halving
is replayed. Its Borel image belongs to the previously constructed root
image: a necessary source generator can be redundant after Borel.

The other required generator is the degree-32 norm of the 32-character
D5+A1 orbit of the root w-z. It is monic in r^16. Its actual root orbit,
coefficient integrality and rational invariant expression were
[independently audited](experiments/e7-d5a1-norm-audit.json).

These 25 elements generate the full two-local root invariant source in
all degrees. The [primary initial-source calculation](experiments/e7-d5a1-initial-source.json)
and its [independent audit](experiments/e7-d5a1-initial-source-audit.json)
verify 25 leading-polynomial witnesses through degree 32. Their periods
are (1,2,2,2,2,2,16), with polynomial parameter weights
(1,2,4,6,8,10,32). There are 512 possible residue vectors, 32 nonempty
residue modules, and 43 module minima. Formula (4) agrees identically
with (1), so criterion (3) proves the unbounded source assertion.

Every source generator maps to the earlier constructed root image on
E7/P6. The complete relative formula therefore has exactly 366 nonunit
factors, all two, as recorded in the
[complete comparison](E7_D5_A1_COMPARISON.md). The proof uses the
degree-23 witness and the full Hilbert identity; the degrees of the
generators alone would not establish completeness.

## Known inputs and scope of the deductions

The characteristic-zero Weyl invariant degrees, the symmetric-polynomial
theorem, leading-algebra Hilbert inequalities, monomial-ideal Hilbert
series, and Nakayama's lemma are standard inputs. The exact two-local
embeddings, displayed generators, product witnesses and finite monoid
certificates are the repository calculations. The argument above explains
how those certificates establish source completeness. It does not
establish literature priority for these particular presentations.

For the Borel images, the weight source still uses the previously proved
D5 or D4 source with the common A1 variable t. The root sources proved
here justify using their complete generator algebras in all cohomological
degrees. Ambient cohomology/image cokernels remain a separate question.

## Reproducing the source completion

From borel/, with NumPy and python-flint available to native Python:

```sh
python experiments/audit_e7_d5a1_extra23.py
python experiments/e7_d5a1_initial_source.py
python experiments/audit_e7_initial_sources.py --levi d5a1
python experiments/summarize_e7_d5a1_complete.py
python experiments/audit_e7_d4a1_generators.py
python experiments/e7_d4a1_initial_source.py
python experiments/audit_e7_initial_sources.py --levi d4a1
```

The first command replays the compact degree-23 transcript from the
committed degree-22 source; it does not require the expanded exploratory
checkpoints. The initial-source producers use the committed full source
data and norm. The independent shared audit reconstructs products in a
different order and computes the whole monoid Hilbert series by a different
method. PYTHON_FLINT_PATH may point to an optional separate installation.
SymPy and gmpy2 are also used by the generator and degree-23 audits.
After regenerating reports, rerun their dependent image/summary and release
bindings, since the reports contain execution times as well as proof data.
