# F4: the literal integral character source and its Borel images

For the 26-dimensional representation V(omega4), with the normalized
Springer map theta, the full torus intersection is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_{26}
=\{f\in\mathbb Q[\mathfrak t]:\theta^*f\in\mathbb Z[X(T)]\}
=\mathbb Z[6\omega_1,6\omega_2,12\omega_3,12\omega_4]
=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(6Q_{\rm long}).}
\]

Here Q_long is the lattice generated by the long roots, not the whole F4
root lattice. This statement includes inhomogeneous polynomials and holds
in all degrees. Consequently, for every standard Levi L,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_{L,\mathbb Z}
=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(6Q_{\rm long})^{W_L}.}
\]

For all four maximal parabolics we compute the complete Borel image of
this source, in every cohomological degree, and compare it with both earlier
selected forms. These image calculations do not supply a finite integral
generator-and-relation presentation of the invariant source.

## Relation to the papers and conventions

We use Sage/Bourbaki numbering, fundamental weights omega_i, and P_i formed
by omitting node i. Nodes 1 and 2 are long-root nodes. Polynomial degree d
corresponds to cohomological degree 2d. F4 has trivial center, so its root
and weight lattices coincide; no simply-connected/adjoint ambiguity occurs.
An integral character here means a virtual character with integer
coefficients, not necessarily the character of an actual representation.

The Springer/Borel construction is the one in
[Kumar, Part I](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar76.pdf).
[Kumar--Xie, Part III, Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 6.2](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar86.pdf)
give the F4 Springer formulas and rational/complex Levi generators and
their Borel images. We use the repository's
[audited catalogue](EXCEPTIONAL.md), retaining its existing correction metadata.

The integral intersection and its image lattices below are deductions
recorded here, not results asserted to be stated in those papers.
The [general saturation criterion](CHARACTER_SOURCES.md) is an elementary
argument. No priority or originality claim is made for this application.

## Proof of the torus intersection

In fundamental-weight coordinates the Cartan matrix, whose columns are
the simple roots, is

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&0\\
0&-2&2&-1\\
0&0&-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The nonzero weights of V(omega4) are the 24 short roots, each with
multiplicity one; zero has multiplicity two. They can be generated from
omega4 by the four reflections

\[
s_i(\mu)=\mu-\mu_i C_{\bullet i}.
\]

Put t_i=e^(omega_i). In the simple-coroot basis the trace Gram matrix is

\[
M=\sum_\mu m_\mu\mu\mu^t
=\begin{pmatrix}
12&-6&0&0\\
-6&12&-12&0\\
0&-12&24&-12\\
0&0&-12&24
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

If b_i=sum_mu m_mu mu_i t^mu, then Theta=M^(-1)b gives
Theta_i=theta*(omega_i). This fixes the normalization: d(theta)_1=id.
Define x=(6omega1,6omega2,12omega3,12omega4) and B_i=theta*(x_i).
The B_i have integral Laurent coefficients. Their coefficient matrix has
a minor of determinant plus or minus one at the four exponents

\[
(-1,0,0,1),\quad(-1,0,1,-1),\quad
(-1,0,1,0),\quad(-1,1,-1,0).
\]

Thus every rational linear combination of the B_i with integral
coefficients has integer combination coefficients. This proves that the
degree-one lattice is exactly the span of x.

Now make the unimodular row change

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&-1\\
0&1&-1&1\\
1&-1&0&0\\
1&-2&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In descending lexicographic order, the four Laurent polynomials UB have
leading exponents

\[
(1,0,0,-1),\quad(1,0,-1,1),\quad
(1,0,-1,0),\quad(1,-1,1,0),
\]

all with coefficient 1. The exponent matrix is nonsingular over Q.
Distinct monomials in these four polynomials therefore have distinct
leading exponents, over every prime field. Hence the B_i are algebraically
independent modulo every prime.

To finish, suppose f in Q[x] has integral Laurent pullback. Choose the
least positive integer N for which Nf is in Z[x]. If p divides N,
the reduction of Nf modulo p is a nonzero polynomial, by minimality,
but its Laurent pullback is zero modulo p. This contradicts the
independence just proved. Thus N=1. No homogeneity assumption was used.

For the root-lattice description, the columns
alpha1, alpha2, 2alpha3, 2alpha4 span Q_long. For example, the last two
are sums of long roots, and every long root belongs to their span.
An integer unimodular change takes this basis to
omega1, omega2, 2omega3, 2omega4. Hence Lambda=6Q_long.

Taking W_L invariants gives the asserted Levi source: the Springer map
is equivariant and injective on polynomial functions, and integral virtual
Levi characters restrict to Z[X(T)]^(W_L). No averaging over Z is used.

## Computing the complete integral Levi source degree by degree

The distinct catalogue generators have the following degrees:

| Omitted node | Derived Levi type | Rational generator degrees | dim(G/P) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C3 | 1, 2, 4, 6 | 15 |
| 2 | A1 x A2 | 1, 2, 2, 3 | 20 |
| 3 | A2 x A1 | 1, 2, 2, 3 | 20 |
| 4 | B3 | 1, 2, 4, 6 | 15 |

The two middle catalogues repeat a linear generator; we remove that exact
duplicate only. All remaining source monomials are retained, including
those with zero Borel image. Invariance, a nonzero Jacobian, and the
standard basic degrees of these reflection groups check the rational
polynomial-algebra description independently of the image calculations.

Write K=Q_long with basis u=(omega1,omega2,2omega3,2omega4). For a fixed
degree d, express every rational invariant monomial m_j in the u variables,
and let M_d be the matrix of their coefficients. The lattice

\[
L_d=\{a\in\mathbb Q^{r_d}:M_da\in\mathbb Z^{N_d}\}
\]

is computed exactly by Hermite normal form. Its basis columns B_d then give
the actual character-source basis

\[
6^d\sum_j(B_d)_{jk}m_j.
\]

Each computed lattice has an independent completeness certificate:
M_d B_d is integral and selected maximal minors have greatest common
divisor 1. Thus its columns are primitive, so there are no additional
rational coefficient combinations satisfying the integrality conditions.
This checks completeness without trusting the Hermite algorithm.

As a small example for P1, write y=y4=omega1 and let e1 be the catalogue
quadratic. The degree-two source is

\[
\mathcal A_{1,\mathbb Z}^{\,2}
=\mathbb Z(36y^2)\oplus\mathbb Z\bigl(18(e_1-y^2)\bigr).
\]

This illustrates the role of combinations of generators. The nonzero
source element 18(e1+y^2) has zero Borel image; it must still participate
in the source calculation.

## Complete images and comparisons

Let h=beta(omega_i) in H^2(F4/P_i,Z), the primitive divisor class, and
J_i=beta(A_(i,Z)). The first two positive degrees give:

| Parabolic | J_i in H^2 | Smith factors in H^4 |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 6 Z h | 36 |
| P2 | 6 Z h | 36, 72 |
| P3 | 12 Z h | 72, 144 |
| P4 | 12 Z h | 144 |

All image lattices have full rational rank. Their Smith factors describe
the finite quotient H^(2d)(F4/P_i,Z)/J_i^d, with unit factors retained.
The [complete tables](F4_CHARACTER_TABLES.md) give all 74 degree rows;
the [machine-readable certificate](experiments/f4-character-form-results.json)
also stores source bases, embedded images, sums, intersections, and quotients.

The earlier [selected forms](EXCEPTIONAL_INTEGRAL.md) are the catalogue
generators over Z and their individual primitive-image rescalings.
Containment is tested in the common Schubert basis, not inferred from
Smith factors. In every positive nonzero degree, the character image is
properly contained in both earlier forms; in degree zero they agree.

## API and reproducible verification

```python
from sage_borel import f4_character_source

source = f4_character_source(2)
degree = source.degree(2)
assert len(degree.source_basis) == 3
image = degree.image()
assert image.smith_factors == (36, 72)
old = source.rational_algebra.integral_degree(2)
assert image.compare(old).left_in_right
```

`source_basis` consists of polynomials in the named **formal rational
generators**. Use `source.rational_algebra.substitute(f)` for a polynomial
in fundamental weights. `basis_matrix` gives the same basis in the ordered
`monomials`. The matrices are immutable. `image()` returns an
`IntegralImageLattice`, supporting membership, sums, intersections,
containments and quotient groups.

The general `IntegralCharacterSource(springer_map, rational_algebra,
basis_change=...)` computes the same intersection inside any specified
algebraically independent homogeneous rational invariant algebra, provided
the all-primes torus certificate succeeds. For arbitrary inputs it does
not assert that the specified rational algebra contains every Levi invariant.
It accepts genuine subalgebras as well. The F4 factory supplies the complete
published rational source.

Degree computations default to at most 500 rational source monomials and
2,000,000 possible coefficient-matrix entries. Larger requests require
explicit budget arguments. These are size guards, not runtime guarantees.
Source lattices in degrees above dim(G/P) still exist; their Borel images
vanish. There is no finite integral presentation API in this release.

In a recorded SageMath 9.5 run, the public API computed the 192-dimensional
source lattice for P2 in degree 20 in about 38 seconds, then its Borel image
in about 51 seconds. Its top Smith factor agreed with the independent table.
These are measured times, not general performance guarantees. The complete
table experiment uses localization plus Chevalley multiplication and takes
about 287 seconds for all four parabolics.

Run from `borel/`:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src sage -python experiments/f4_character_form.py
python experiments/audit_f4_character_form.py
PYTHONPATH=src sage -python tools/verify_character_release.py
```

The Sage experiment compares every rational source monomial's entire
Borel expansion using fixed-point localization and independent
root/coroot Chevalley multiplication. It also directly checks both
homogeneous and filtered Laurent intersections through degree three.
The [standard-library Python audit](experiments/audit_f4_character_form.py)
reconstructs the torus and source coefficient lattices, checks integral
combinations and maximal-minor certificates, and verifies image Smith
factors and lattice comparisons independently of Sage.
Its saved output is [f4-character-form-audit.json](experiments/f4-character-form-audit.json).
The [release regression record](character-verification.json) covers the API.

Historical G2/E6/E7 verification reports retain their historical dependency
hashes. They are not presented as hashes of this release.

## What this contributes to the general direction

Together with the existing proofs, the four minimal representations give

| Representation | Full torus integral source |
|---|---|
| G2, V(omega1), dimension 7 | Sym_Z(2 Q_long) |
| F4, V(omega4), dimension 26 | Sym_Z(6 Q_long) |
| E6, V(omega1), dimension 27 | Sym_Z(6 Q) |
| E7, V(omega7), dimension 56 | Sym_Z(12 Q) |

For simply laced types Q_long=Q. These are four established cases, not
a classification of representations. They support studying how trace
normalization and the lattice spanned by weights determine the linear
source, and when that linear source generates the full intersection.
The [weight-lattice formula](CHARACTER_SOURCES.md#the-linear-lattice-directly-from-weights)
explains the linear calculation as Lambda_rho=M_rho D_rho^#, where D_rho
is generated by the weights multiplied by their multiplicities.
The leading-coefficient proof is the extra ingredient: a linear-lattice
calculation alone does not establish the all-degree assertion.
