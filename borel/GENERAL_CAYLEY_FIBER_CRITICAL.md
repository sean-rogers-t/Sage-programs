# General Cayley fibers from characters and critical-point algebras

Date: 2026-10-23. Written characteristic-zero deductions with independent
finite checks. The construction is assembled from known trace-map,
adjoint-quotient and singularity-theory ingredients. No novelty claim.

## 1. Scope and the general statement

Let G be a connected complex reductive algebraic group, rho an actual
finite-dimensional rational representation, and assume its trace form

    B(X,Y)=tr(d rho(X) d rho(Y))

is nondegenerate. Write chi=tr rho and normalize the Springer map by

    B(theta(g),X)=tr(rho(g) d rho(X)).                     (1)

For semisimple G a faithful differential suffices for nondegeneracy.
Retain the character lattice of the specified group. No parabolic,
cohomology ring, integral image, or Springer resolution is involved.

Fix a semisimple target h in Lie(G) and put

    K=Z_G(h), k=Lie(K), r=rank K=rank G,
    F_h=theta^(-1)(h), pi:K -> Q=K//K.

K is connected reductive, h is central in k, and B restricted to k is
nondegenerate. Let eta_1,...,eta_c be a basis of the algebraic character
lattice of K. There are unique complex numbers c_j such that

    B(h,X)=sum_j c_j d eta_j(X),       X in k.             (2)

Here d eta_j is the logarithmic differential at identity. Such numbers
exist because the left side annihilates [k,k], and character differentials
span the dual of k/[k,k]. No integrality of c_j is asserted.

Both chi restricted to K and the eta_j descend to Q; denote their
descents by bar chi and bar eta_j. For q_0 in Q define the formal function

    W_h(q)=bar chi(q)-bar chi(q_0)
           -sum_j c_j log(bar eta_j(q)/bar eta_j(q_0)).    (3)

The logarithms are their formal series at 1. They define a local potential,
not a single-valued global regular function. Their differentials are
regular algebraic one-forms, since the eta_j are invertible.

**Theorem A (critical presentation and regular-quotient reduction).**

(a) F_h is a closed subscheme of K. At every g_0 in F_h, with q_0=pi(g_0),
its formal germ is the critical scheme of the pulled-back function:

    formal F_h at g_0 = Crit(W_h composed with pi).        (4)

This part does not assume g_0 regular or Q smooth.

(b) If Q is smooth at q_0 and pi is smooth at g_0, choose local parameters
q_1,...,q_r on Q. Then dW_h(q_0)=0 and, noncanonically,

    completed O_(F_h,g_0)
      = (C[[q_1,...,q_r]] / (partial W_h/partial q_i))
          [[u_1,...,u_d]],        d=dim K-r.              (5)

Thus the transverse algebra is the Jacobian, or Milnor, algebra of W_h.
This is an equality of schemes/formal local rings, not only point sets.
In particular

    dim T_(g_0) F_h = dim K-rank Hess(W_h)(q_0),
    rank d theta_(g_0)
      = dim G-dim K+rank Hess(W_h)(q_0).                  (6)

For connected simply connected semisimple G, the hypotheses of (b)
hold whenever g_0 is regular in K: dim Z_K(g_0)=r. This includes
exceptional groups and all actual representations satisfying (1).
At a point of F_h, equivariance gives Z_G(g_0) subset K, hence
Z_G(g_0)=Z_K(g_0). Since K has full rank, regularity in K and G is
equivalent there. Smoothness of the quotient map is the essential condition.

## 2. Proof of the critical presentation

Equivariance gives Ad(g)theta(g)=theta(g). Consequently

    (Ad(g)-1)h=-(Ad(g)-1)(theta(g)-h).

All defining equations of the stabilizer K therefore lie in the fiber
ideal. This proves the scheme-theoretic containment F_h subset K.
Also theta(K) lies in k: each element of K commutes with exp(s h), so
equivariance implies that its image commutes with h.

For completeness, B pairs eigenspaces of ad h only when their eigenvalues
sum to zero. Its zero eigenspace k is orthogonal to every nonzero
eigenspace. Nondegeneracy of B on Lie(G) then implies nondegeneracy on k.

For a tangent vector gX with X in k, the defining trace formula gives

    d chi_g(gX)=B(theta(g),X).

The left Maurer--Cartan form is g^(-1)dg. Equation (2) yields the global
identity of algebraic one-forms on K

    d chi - sum_j c_j d log eta_j
       = B(theta-h, g^(-1)dg).                            (7)

The right side expresses the entries of theta-h in an invertible
cotangent frame. Thus its coefficient ideal is exactly the fiber ideal
inside K. The left side is d(W_h composed with pi) in the formal
neighborhood under consideration. This proves (4), even at nonregular
points. In particular the logarithmic target correction in (3) cannot
generally be omitted.

If pi and Q are smooth at the indicated points, formal smoothness gives
parameters q_1,...,q_r,u_1,...,u_d on K with pi the projection onto q.
The pulled-back function depends only on q. Its gradient ideal is
therefore generated by the r partial derivatives in (5). Differentiating
that ideal at the critical point gives (6).

When G is simply connected semisimple, K is a Levi subgroup with simply
connected derived subgroup S. The multiplication cover Z(K)^0 x S -> K
is a finite central etale isogeny. The corresponding action on the central
torus factor is free. The quotient is locally etale-covered by
Z(K)^0 x (S//S). Steinberg's regular-element criterion makes S -> S//S
smooth at regular elements and S//S smooth. This proves the automatic
hypotheses stated after (6), including the torus directions.

## 3. Components, collapse and multiplicity

Assume here that G is simply connected semisimple. Let t be semisimple,
h=theta(t), H=Z_G(t), K=Z_G(h), and q_0=pi(t). The adjoint-quotient
fiber has underlying variety

    C_t=pi^(-1)(q_0)=K . (t U(H)),                        (8)

where U(H) denotes all unipotent elements of H. This variety is closed,
irreducible, of dimension dim K-r, and has a dense regular K-orbit.
These are the standard conjugacy-class/adjoint-quotient facts. H lies
in K by equivariance and is also the centralizer of t in K.

**Corollary B.** The following are equivalent:

* theta(tu)=h for every u in U(H);
* theta(tu)=h for one regular unipotent u of H;
* q_0 is a critical point of W_h on Q.

Indeed t u for regular u is regular in K. Apply Theorem A there, and
use the density of its K-orbit in (8). Conversely a zero of dW_h makes
the pullback differential vanish on the entire quotient fiber.

When these conditions hold, C_t is a component of (F_h)_red if and only
if q_0 is isolated in Crit(W_h). Its generic scheme multiplicity is then

    mu=dim_C C[[q_1,...,q_r]]/(partial W_h/partial q_i).   (9)

To see the converse as well as sufficiency, use (5) at a regular point
of C_t. A closed point is a component of the reduced critical germ
exactly when it is isolated. The formal product then computes both
the component and its generic length.

The Artinian algebra in (9) is a complete intersection and is Gorenstein:
an m-primary ideal generated by r elements in a regular local ring of
dimension r is a parameter ideal and a regular sequence. In particular:

* mu=1 exactly when Hess(W_h)(q_0) is nonsingular.
* If the Hessian has corank one and the point is isolated, the transverse
  algebra is C[[s]]/(s^mu) for some mu>=2.

For the second assertion, formal completion of squares splits off the
nondegenerate quadratic variables. The remaining one-variable function
has first nonzero term of degree mu+1; its derivative is a unit times
s^mu. These are standard singularity-theory facts applied to (5).

These statements do not classify components supported entirely in the
non-smooth locus of pi, nor do they determine singular points of C_t.

## 4. A useful criterion: characters linear in invariant coordinates

**Lemma C (elimination).** Suppose a critical germ has coordinates
x=(x_1,...,x_p), y=(y_1,...,y_q), q>=p, and potential

    W(x,y)=sum_i x_i F_i(y)+G(y),
    F(0)=0,       rank dF_0=p.                            (10)

Choose formal coordinates (v,z) on the y-space with v=F(y). Then

    Jac(W) = Jac(G(0,z)).                                (11)

Here Jac means the completed local Jacobian algebra; it may have
positive dimension. The r=p+q variables are reduced to q-p variables.

Proof: the equations partial W/partial x set v=0. The equations
partial W/partial v eliminate x=-G_v(0,z). The remaining equations
are G_z(0,z)=0. These are exact ideal eliminations, without radicals.
Shifting the base x-coordinate only adds a linear combination of v to G
and does not change G(0,z).

Consequences are uniform: p=q forces a reduced isolated critical point;
q=p+1 leaves just one derivative whose vanishing order gives multiplicity.
The criterion concerns the actual restricted character and its target
logarithm, so it can be tested without constructing representation matrices.
No assertion is made that every character admits coordinates (10).

### Type C revisited

In the [primitive middle-exterior model](SYMPLECTIC_FIBER_COMPONENT.md),
x is the m first-block coefficients and y the m external spectral
coefficients. The mixed matrix is L=E E^T with det L=1. The target
logarithm involves only the external coordinates. After translation,
the potential is of form (10) with F=L(y-y_0), p=q=m.
Lemma C proves mu=1 in every rank, independently of external target
collisions. On an etale labeled-eigenvalue chart of K//K the external
coefficient change is invertible, so possible collisions do not change
the argument. This reproves the generic component conclusion.

### Type A revisited

In the [SL_(2m) middle-exterior model](MIDDLE_EXTERIOR_FIBER_COMPONENT.md),
put alpha=i, beta_j=alpha gamma_j with gamma_j^m=-1, and
K_j=(alpha-beta_j)^m. Use first-block invariants a_1,...,a_(m-1) as x,
and labeled external eigenvalues b_1,...,b_m as y. The determinant
constraint sets a_m=1/product b_j. The normalized trace index is
D=binomial(2m-2,m-1), and the external-minus-first target eigenvalue
difference satisfies D(h_j-h_A)=-K_j. Thus (3) is, up to a constant,

    W=sum_(j=1)^(m-1) a_j e_(m-j)(b)
      +(product b)^(-1)+product b+sum_j K_j log(b_j/beta_j).

The map F=(e_1(b),...,e_(m-1)(b)) has rank m-1 at beta. Its zero locus
locally is b_j=s beta_j. Here p=m-1, q=m; Lemma C leaves

    w(s)=alpha^m(s^(-m)+(-1)^m s^m)
         +m alpha^m(1-(-1)^m) log s,
    w'(s)=-m alpha^m s^(-m-1) R_m(s),
    R_m(s)=1-s^(2m)           if m is even,
           (s^m-1)^2         if m is odd.                (12)

The product of the beta_j is (-1)^m alpha^m, whose reciprocal is alpha^m,
and sum K_j=m alpha^m(1-(-1)^m), proving the displayed coefficients.
Hence the transverse multiplicities are one and two respectively.
The target-centralizer dimensions remain those of the preceding proof.
This explains the parity result through one general character criterion.

## 5. Why the hypotheses matter

At a nonregular point, (4) remains true, but (5) need not:

    Crit(W composed with pi) need not equal pi^(-1)(Crit(W)).

The chain rule gives the exact fiber ideal

    entries of (d pi)^T (gradient W).                     (13)

One cannot cancel d pi where it drops rank. For the Sp_4 primitive
representation, K=Sp_2 x C*, a=tr A and q=b+b^(-1). At h with external
entries i,-i, the potential is

    W=1+a(b+b^(-1))-4i log(b/i).

Its quotient critical point is nondegenerate. Nevertheless at A=I the
quotient differential loses rank, and (13) gives the already audited ring

    C[[x,y,z,w]]/(wx,wy,wz,x^2+yz-w^2).

This explains how a perfect transverse pairing can coexist with an
embedded singular thickening. The reduced-support conjecture for that
family remains unresolved in higher rank; Theorem A does not prove it.

Even SL_2 in its defining representation illustrates the restriction:
at h=0, K=SL_2 and W(a)=a has no quotient critical point. The actual fiber
is the two reduced points +I,-I, both nonregular in K. They are critical
points of tr on the group, where pi is not smooth.

The isogeny qualification also matters. For G=PGL_2 and its adjoint
representation, lift locally to A in SL_2 and put a=tr A. The global
adjoint-quotient coordinate is q=a^2 and chi_Ad=q-1. At a=0 the element
is regular semisimple in PGL_2, but pi is ramified: dq=2a da. The
potential q-1 has no quotient critical point, while its pullback a^2-1
has a reduced critical divisor. Thus merely saying 'regular in K'
without the simply connected/smooth-quotient qualification is false.
The local critical presentation (4) still holds.

## 6. General multiplicity corollaries and published baselines

Multiplicity larger than two is already present in elementary published
examples. In the defining representation of SL_n, Kostant--Michor give

    theta(g)=g-tr(g)I/n,
    theta^(-1)(h) = Spec C[c]/(det(h+cI)-1).              (14)

Choose h with eigenvalues the roots of x^n-(-1)^(n+1). Then h is regular
semisimple, traceless, det h=1, and det(h+cI)=1+c^n. The fiber is a
single point of length n. In Theorem A its quotient potential has
Hessian corank one and Jacobian algebra C[[c]]/(c^n). This is a direct
corollary of KM Section 6.2, not a new unbounded-multiplicity example.

There is also a fixed-group corollary of their tensor identity.
For connected semisimple G, a representation V of dimension N with
nondegenerate trace form, and d>=1,

    theta_(V tensor^d)=(chi_V/N)^(d-1) theta_V.            (15)

KM Theorem 2.8(3) states this for simple G; the same proof works for
semisimple G, because tr(d rho(X))=0 and
B_(V tensor^d)=d N^(d-1) B_V. More generally, if P(chi_V) is an actual
representation character and P'(N)!=0,

    theta_P=P'(chi_V)/P'(N) theta_V.                      (16)

This character chain rule was already used in our polynomial-spin note.
We do not claim (15) or (16) as new constructions.

**Corollary D (scheme multiplicity of a character level).** If P'(z)
has a zero of order mu at c, and chi_V^(-1)(c) is nonempty and smooth,
then every irreducible component D of that character level is a component
of theta_P^(-1)(0) with generic multiplicity mu. In fact locally along
the smooth level the fiber ideal is exactly ((chi_V-c)^mu).

Proof: smoothness means d chi_V!=0, hence theta_V!=0 by (1). Some entry
of theta_V is a unit locally. Equation (16) therefore identifies the full
fiber ideal with (P'(chi_V)); the other factors are units there.

Consequently, on every fixed nontrivial connected semisimple G, actual
representations realize arbitrarily large generic multiplicities of
positive-dimensional zero-fiber components. To prove existence, start
with an infinitesimally faithful V. The nonconstant character chi_V has
only finitely many critical values: on each irreducible component of
the locus d chi_V=0 it is constant in characteristic zero. Its image
also omits only finitely many values. Choose a nonnegative integer a
such that -a is a nonempty regular value. Put V'=V direct-sum 1^a.
Then chi_(V')=chi_V+a has a nonempty smooth zero level, and
(V') tensor^(mu+1) realizes multiplicity mu there by (15).
These representations are generally reducible. The component dimension
is dim G-1. This is an elementary scheme-theoretic corollary of a
published identity, not a new construction of Cayley maps.

For SL_2 no shift is needed: chi_V=tr A and the smooth irreducible level
tr A=0 has dimension two. V tensor^(mu+1) gives it multiplicity mu.

## 7. What this contributes and what remains

The general reduction identifies the exact local algebra to compute;
Lemma C supplies an effective criterion that explains both earlier
families. It applies across group types when its explicit character
hypotheses can be verified. It does not classify all characters or all
fibers, and generic degree is a separate problem. Nonsemisimple targets
are outside the present theorem; their centralizers need not be reductive.

Known inputs are the trace and tensor identities, regular adjoint
quotients, formal smoothness, and critical-point/complete-intersection
algebra. The logarithm-corrected critical presentation and the applications
above are deductions written here; literature priority is unverified.
The multiplicity corollaries should not be advertised as solving a
named open problem.

The next general question is which Jacobian algebras arise from actual
irreducible representation characters, and which admit the linearity
criterion (10). A useful first milestone is a character-level criterion
for that form, or an obstruction forcing more than one residual variable.
Unbounded multiplicity by tensor powers is already explained and is
not a reason to generate another case table. At nonregular points the
separate problem is the interaction of (13) with quotient ramification.
