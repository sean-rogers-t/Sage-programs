# Integral Springer-generator images: literature audit

Audit date: 2026-10-19. This note concerns the integral lattices generated by
the **fixed, normalized input polynomials** in this repository. It separates
published statements, deductions from them, and questions for which no exact
answer was located. The bounded calculations are recorded separately in
[experiments/integral-springer-results.json](experiments/integral-springer-results.json).
Those computations are not proofs of a uniform theorem or evidence of novelty.

## What lattice is being measured?

For fixed homogeneous inputs `f_1,...,f_m` whose Borel images are integral,
let

$$
L_d=\sum_{\sum_j a_j\deg(f_j)=d}
\mathbb Z\,\beta(f_1^{a_1}\cdots f_m^{a_m})
\subset H^{2d}(G/P;\mathbb Z).
$$

The integral Schubert basis identifies the target with a free abelian group.
If `M_d` is the monomial-image matrix, its nonzero Smith factors
`s_1 | ... | s_r` describe

$$
\operatorname{sat}(L_d)/L_d\cong
\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb Z/s_i\mathbb Z,\qquad
\operatorname{sat}(L_d)=(L_d\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap H^{2d}(G/P;\mathbb Z).
$$

The saturation index is `product(s_i)`. It is an index in all degree-`2d`
cohomology only when the rational image has full rank. Rational injectivity,
or equality of rational image algebras, does not establish saturation.

## The integral form must be specified

The notation `Rep_poly^Z` in the proof of Part II is a **chosen polynomial
Z-form** of a complex algebra. It need not be the intersection of that complex
algebra with the ordinary integral representation ring.

For the standard symplectic and odd orthogonal Springer morphisms, put

$$
\bar t_i=\frac{t_i-t_i^{-1}}2,\qquad z_i=\bar t_i^2.
$$

[Kumar--Rogers, Part II](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar81.pdf),
Corollary 6, Proposition 7 and the proof of Theorem 16, defines the relevant
complex algebra using the `z_i`, and explicitly chooses `Z[e_1(z),...,e_k(z)]`
in the proof of the theorem, journal p.353. These generators have integral
cohomology images by equation (14). Their Laurent-character expressions can
have denominators. For example `z_i=(t_i^2+t_i^-2-2)/4`.

By contrast, [Kumar, Part I](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar76.pdf),
Lemma 15(a) and equation (15), journal p.406, identifies the literal
representation-ring intersection for `Sp(2k)` and `SO(2k+1)` as
`Z_sym[u_1,...,u_k]`, where `u_i=t_i^2+t_i^-2`. Its relation to the selected
normalization is `u_i=4*z_i+2`.

**Normalization deduction.** The elementary-function identity

$$
e_j(4z_1+2,\ldots,4z_k+2)
=\sum_{i=0}^j4^i2^{j-i}\binom{k-i}{j-i}e_i(z)
$$

and induction on `j` give

$$
\mathbb Z_{\rm sym}[u]
=\mathbb Z[4e_1(z),4^2e_2(z),\ldots,4^ke_k(z)].
$$

Thus the two Z-forms agree after rationalization but generally have different
integral images. In cohomological degree `4m`, the image lattice of the literal
intersection is `4^m` times the lattice for the normalized `e_i(z)` inputs.
This statement follows from the displayed translation; it is not a Smith-table
theorem stated in Part I or Part II. The equality concerns the graded form
obtained by including constants and making the triangular changes above.

In type D the selected source in Part III, equation (29), additionally contains
the Euler generator `p=product(bar t_i)`, with `p^2=e_k(z)`.

**Type-D normalization deduction, for SO rather than Spin.** Put
`v_i=t_i-t_i^-1` and `V=product(v_i)`. Part I, Proposition 12(b), identifies
the complex polynomial subalgebra with expressions

$$
P(v_1^2,\ldots,v_k^2)+VQ(v_1^2,\ldots,v_k^2),
$$

where `P,Q` are symmetric. The first summand has all Laurent exponents even,
and the second all odd, so fractional coefficients cannot cancel between them.
The substitution of a monomial in the `v_i^2` has leading Laurent monomial
with coefficient one; multiplication by `V` retains that property. Subtracting
leading terms inductively proves that an integral Laurent expression forces
`P,Q` to have integer coefficients. The fundamental theorem of symmetric
polynomials over Z then gives the literal intersection

$$
\mathbb Z[e_1(v^2),\ldots,e_k(v^2),V],\qquad V^2=e_k(v^2).
$$

In normalized coordinates these generators are `4^i*e_i(z)` and `2^k*p`.
Every generator is therefore scaled by `2^(its polynomial degree)`. In
polynomial degree `d`, the literal SO image lattice is `2^d` times the
normalized selected image lattice. This is our normalization deduction from
the published complex-subalgebra description and the SO character lattice;
it is not a Smith theorem stated in Part I or Part III. A Spin character
lattice has extra weights and requires a separate analysis.

For all these families the integral audit must distinguish the chosen
normalized Z-form from the representation-ring intersection. The dataset
records the inputs and any explicit alternative form separately.

| [Part II](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar81.pdf), Proposition 11 and Theorem 16, pp.350, 352--354; equations (13)--(18) | The natural symplectic tail generators map to `c_i^2+2*sum((-1)^j*c_(i+j)*c_(i-j))`. | [Part II](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar81.pdf), Theorem 29 and equations (26)--(30), pp.360--361 | Stable injectivity for odd orthogonal tail generators. The modulo-two argument is made in the Chern-class subring `bar H`, which has the same complexification as full cohomology. |
| [Kumar--Xie, Part III](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar86.pdf), Theorem 3.6, Proposition 3.7, Theorem 3.8 and equations (28)--(33), article pp.11--15 | Finite generator images, a stable integral presentation, and injectivity of the chosen type-D Z-form with its Euler generator. The proof first treats a Chern/Euler subring and then uses `p^2=e_k`. |
| [Part III](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar86.pdf), Theorems 5.2, 6.2, 7.2 and 8.2 | Explicit complex-algebra generator images for the listed exceptional maximal parabolics. The stated coefficient normalization must be retained in an integral audit. |

These results supply the maps and integral forms rather than a general
degreewise Smith-normal-form classification for every chosen family.

There is a useful additional consequence of the **symplectic** modulo-two
proof. In each degree the stable source and target are finite free Z-modules,
and the source-to-target reduction modulo two is injective. Hence none of the
nonzero Smith factors is even. This is a statement about the cokernel, stronger
than integral injectivity alone. That argument by itself gives no information
about odd prime factors.

The corresponding conclusion cannot be transferred to the full orthogonal
Schubert lattice from those proofs: their intermediate Chern-class subrings
may themselves be nonprimitive in full cohomology.

## Natural symplectic tails are saturated: a classical geometric deduction

Here there is substantially more than an absence of a counterexample. Let
`n=a+k`, with `a,k >= 1`, and take the normalized tail inputs

$$
q_i=e_i(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2),\qquad 1\le i\le k,
$$

on `IG(a,2n)`. In compact-group notation there is a projection

$$
\pi:\frac{Sp(n)}{U(a)\times Sp(k)}
\longrightarrow\frac{Sp(n)}{Sp(a)\times Sp(k)}
\cong\operatorname{HGr}(k,n),
$$

with fiber `Sp(a)/U(a)=LG(a,2a)`.

**Published ingredients.** Pragacz--Ratajski explicitly describe this fibration
in [Appendix A, §10.5, journal pp.81--82](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1000182205320).
Their §10.1--10.3, pp.80--81, describes integral quaternionic Schubert
cohomology and its cells. Their map uses the quaternionic span of the
isotropic `a`-plane; taking its quaternionic orthogonal complement gives the
displayed `k`-plane version. The fiber is an ordinary complex Lagrangian
Grassmannian and has a free integral Schubert basis in even degrees.

**Saturation deduction.** The base is simply connected. The integral Serre
spectral sequence has free groups only in even bidegrees, so every differential
vanishes. In total degree `j`, the image of the edge pullback is the top
filtration subgroup `F^j H^j`. Its quotient has a finite filtration with free
abelian successive quotients, and is therefore free. Thus `pi*` is injective
and its image is a primitive integral subgroup in every degree.

The tautological quaternionic bundle has symplectic Chern/Borel classes whose
pullbacks are `q_i`, up to sign conventions that are units. Its integral ring
is generated by these classes, with presentation

$$
H^*(\operatorname{HGr}(k,n);\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z[q_1,\ldots,q_k]/(h_{a+1},\ldots,h_n),
\qquad\deg(q_i)=4i.
$$

Here `h_j` denotes the complete symmetric polynomial expressed in the
elementary generators `q_i` of the squared-root alphabet.

One explicit broader presentation is
[Panin--Walter, Theorem 11.2, equation (11.7), pp.33--34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.0649),
with its Schur basis in equation (11.8). Their algebraic quaternionic
Grassmannian has the homotopy type of the compact one over C, as noted in
their introduction, p.2. The integral topological case gives the presentation
used here.

It follows that

$$
\mathbb Z[\beta(q_1),\ldots,\beta(q_k)]
=\pi^*H^*(\operatorname{HGr}(k,n);\mathbb Z)
$$

is saturated in `H*(IG(a,2n);Z)`. Therefore **all nonzero Smith factors for
these normalized inputs are one**, at finite rank as well as degreewise in
the stable limit. In polynomial degree `2m` their number is the number of
partitions of `m` contained in the `k`-by-`a` rectangle. Odd polynomial
degrees have zero image. Stable degreewise saturation follows by taking a
finite rank in which the degree has stabilized.

Combining this result with the normalization deduction above, the literal
symplectic polynomial-character intersection instead has all its nonzero
Smith factors equal to `4^m` in cohomological degree `4m`. This arithmetic
depends on the Z-form; it does not contradict saturation for normalized
`q_i`. These conclusions should be described as deductions from established
geometry and normalizations, not claimed as new standalone theorems.

## Theta, eta and Schur-Q literature supplies integral structure

The following sources give much stronger ambient information than rational
injectivity, although they do not state the exact Smith tables of our
selected tail-generator matrices.

- [Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, *A Giambelli formula for isotropic Grassmannians*](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/isogiamfinal.pdf),
  Theorem 2 and Proposition 5.2(a), author PDF pp.3--4 and 32, gives integral
  theta and Chern-monomial bases for stable symplectic cohomology. The proof
  of Proposition 5.2 explicitly uses dominance straightening. These bases
  are useful coordinates for comparing the normalized `q_i` with integral
  Schubert classes; the primitivity conclusion here uses the geometric proof
  above.
- [Tamvakis--Wilson, *Double theta polynomials and equivariant Giambelli formulas*](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/dbltheta.pdf),
  Proposition 1, author PDF p.8, gives three integral bases over `Z[t]`,
  including `c_lambda` and theta bases. Setting equivariant parameters to zero
  recovers the ordinary integral basis statement.
- [Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, *Quantum Pieri rules for isotropic Grassmannians*](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/qprig3.pdf),
  Theorems 1.2, 2.2 and 3.2, gives finite integral presentations for
  symplectic, odd orthogonal and even orthogonal Grassmannians. Part III's
  Theorem 3.3 quotes the type-D presentation from this source. Theorem 3.2(b)
  gives an integral special-Schubert-monomial basis.
- [Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, *A Giambelli formula for even orthogonal Grassmannians*](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/oggiam.pdf),
  equation (7), author PDF p.3, records `c_p=tau_p` for `p<k`,
  `c_k=tau_k+tau_k'`, and `c_p=2*tau_p` for `p>k`. Theorem 4, p.5, gives an
  integral eta basis and finite specialization. These formulas explain why
  Chern and Schubert lattices must be distinguished at the prime two.

The last observation is relevant to the measured B/D factors of two. It is
background for deriving them, not by itself a formula for every selected
Pontryagin/Euler image. No closed finite-rank partition classification for
the exact B/D selected families was located in the sources reviewed here.
Since their full rings and products are already known, a new claim would
require identifying what additional arithmetic statement is obtained from
those presentations.

The subsequent [orthogonal proof notes](ORTHOGONAL_SMITH.md) give deductions
from this background: stable primitivity, finite powers-of-two restrictions,
a type B exponent bound and block symmetry, complete quadric formulas,
and exact matrix and gluing formulas. They explicitly distinguish these
proved statements from a closed finite partition classification and make
no novelty claim.

## Ambient torsion-index theory has a different scope

[Calmes--Petrov--Zainoulline](https://arxiv.org/html/0905.1341v2) generalize
the characteristic map using formal group rings and oriented theories.
Theorem 6.4 assumes the torsion index is invertible to prove surjectivity;
Theorem 6.9 also assumes no 2-torsion for the invariant-kernel description.
The geometric comparison is made under the additional hypotheses in §13.
These are not unconditional integral invariant-quotient presentations.

Their results concern the ambient full-flag characteristic map and its
generalizations. They do not identify the lattice generated by a chosen
subset of normalized Springer generators. Even when the ambient torsion index
is one, replacing a primitive input `h` by `2*h` creates image index `2^d` in
degree `d` on projective space. An integral image index is also not torsion
in the integral cohomology of a complex flag variety: the latter is free.

## Exceptional groups: ambient rings are known; normalization remains essential

[Duan--Zhao, *The Chow rings of generalized Grassmannians*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511332),
Theorems 1--7, gives explicit integral presentations for seven exceptional
maximal-parabolic Grassmannians, including `F4/C3*S1`, `F4/B3*S1`,
`E6/D5*S1`, and `E7/E6*S1`. Their Dynkin numbering must be translated before
matching a catalogue case. Their
[full-flag paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.2444), Theorems 1.2--1.3 and
formulas (5.1)--(5.5), supplies integral Schubert presentations more generally.
These are references for the target lattices and products, rather than
published Smith classifications of the Part III normalized inputs.

The exceptional catalogue consists of rational Cartan-polynomial inputs from
the complex-algebra calculations. Each input must pass an integral Schubert
coefficient check before it defines a Z-subalgebra of integral cohomology.
For example, the recorded `G2/P1` quadratic normalization has image
`-3/4*sigma_(2,1)` and fails that check. Multiplying it by four gives an
explicitly different integral input; its index describes that alternative
Z-form, not the original normalization or an automatically identified
representation-ring intersection.

The F4/E6/E7 calculations in the accompanying dataset are likewise arithmetic
of the exact named catalogue polynomials. Several low-degree indices can be
read directly from the already published generator coefficients. A Smith
computation organizes those coefficients and their products; its output
alone is not a new structural result.

## Assessment and remaining research questions

**Established or direct consequences:** the maps and chosen integral forms;
stable injectivity; the integral theta/eta and finite Schubert presentations;
normalized type-C tail saturation at finite and stable rank; and the scaling
between the normalized and literal representation forms for Sp and SO
described above. The latter comparisons depend on the stated character
lattices and do not automatically apply to Spin.

**Not located in this targeted audit:** a closed partition formula for all
finite Smith multiplicities of the selected B/D Pontryagin-and-Euler images, or a
classification for the exact exceptional catalogue Z-forms. This means a
matching theorem was not found in the reviewed primary sources, not that
these statements are new. The reviewed scope includes Parts I--III, the
theta/eta and quaternionic references above, ambient torsion-index theory,
exceptional integral presentations, and searches combining the Springer-map
and selected-image terminology with integral/saturation/Smith descriptions.

A remaining mathematical question is whether the exact orthogonal matrix
and gluing formulas in the proof notes can be simplified to partition
statistics. A second is to identify and compare natural integral
forms of the exceptional polynomial representation subalgebras. Any claim
about representation rings must specify that form explicitly.
