# Irreducible Springer character lattices: a closed classification

Date: 2026-10-20. The classification below is a proved deduction under
the displayed hypotheses, not a conjectural fit to the survey. Its
published ingredients are identified below; no literature-priority claim
is made. Direct sums are explicitly excluded from the closed classification.
The [detailed proof and literature audit](IRREDUCIBLE_LATTICE_AUDIT.md)
found no gap under these hypotheses. It identifies established ordinary
weight-lattice results separately from the multiplicity-weighted step;
the exact integral-source formulation has unresolved literature priority.

## Definitions and result

Let G be simply connected and simple over C, with maximal torus T,
root lattice Q and weight lattice P=X(T). Fix a nonzero dominant integral
weight lambda and the irreducible representation V(lambda). Write
rho_W for the half-sum of positive roots, to distinguish it from a
representation homomorphism. Normalize the invariant inner product on
the real weight space so long roots have squared length two. Put

\[
I_\lambda=\frac{\dim V(\lambda)}{\dim\mathfrak g}
               (\lambda,\lambda+2\rho_W),\qquad
D_\lambda=\sum_\mu\mathbb Z(m_\mu\mu),\qquad
L^*=\{v:(v,L)\subset\mathbb Z\}.
\]

The index is the Dynkin index in the convention of
[Panyushev, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.0398).
In particular I_ad=2h^vee. Sources using the convention I_ad=h^vee
have indices half as large; their values cannot be substituted unchanged.

For the normalized trace Springer map theta_lambda, define the full
linear lattice

\[
\Lambda_\lambda=\{\ell\in P\otimes\mathbb Q:
                    \theta_\lambda^*\ell\in\mathbb Z[P]\}.
\]

**Theorem.** For every nontrivial irreducible highest-weight representation
of a finite simple type,

\[
\boxed{D_\lambda=Q+\mathbb Z\lambda,\qquad
       \Lambda_\lambda=I_\lambda(Q+\mathbb Z\lambda)^*.}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular the answer for all five exceptional types is:

| Type | Condition | Full linear lattice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G2, F4 | Any nonzero dominant lambda | I_lambda Q_long |
| E6, E7 | lambda in Q, equivalently trivial central character | I_lambda P |
| E6, E7 | lambda not in Q | I_lambda Q |
| E8 | Any nonzero dominant lambda | I_lambda P=I_lambda Q |

Here Q_long is the lattice generated by the long roots, inside the
original weight space. In types G2/F4 it is also the lattice generated
by the coroots after identifying the Cartan and its dual using this
normalized inner product. This identification, and its scale, matter.

The exceptional table follows because P/Q is trivial for G2/F4/E8,
cyclic of order three for E6, and cyclic of order two for E7.
For the standard Bourbaki/Sage numbering, lambda=sum a_i omega_i belongs
to Q exactly when

\[
a_1-a_3+a_5-a_6\equiv0\pmod3\quad(E_6),\qquad
a_2+a_5+a_7\equiv0\pmod2\quad(E_7).
\]

These congruences can also be obtained directly by requiring that the
coordinates of C^(-1)lambda are integral, avoiding a numbering assumption.

## The multiplicity-one argument

The distinction between the lattice generated by weights and the lattice
generated by multiplicity-weighted weights is essential. Formula (1)
requires a proof for D_lambda, not just for the ordinary weight span.

Choose a highest-weight vector v. Fix a target simple root alpha_j.
Choose a shortest Dynkin path from the support {i:lambda_i>0} to j,
written i_0,...,i_k=j. Every node after i_0 has highest-weight label zero.
The finite Dynkin diagram is a tree, so there are no chords in this path.
For every prefix define

\[
v_s=f_{i_s}\cdots f_{i_0}v,\qquad
\nu_s=\lambda-\sum_{t=0}^s\alpha_{i_t},\qquad \nu_{-1}=\lambda.
\]

Every v_s is nonzero. At s=0 this is the ordinary sl2 string through
lambda, since lambda_(i_0)>0. At a later step e_(i_s) commutes with all
previous lowering generators and annihilates v, so it annihilates v_(s-1).
The h_(i_s)-weight of v_(s-1) is
-<alpha_(i_(s-1)),alpha_(i_s)^vee>, a positive integer. Finite-dimensional
sl2 theory therefore makes f_(i_s)v_(s-1) nonzero. This works across
double and triple edges as well as single edges.

Each weight nu_s has multiplicity one in the full G-module. Indeed a
highest-weight module is spanned by words in the simple lowering
generators. A word reaching nu_s must use each path generator exactly
once and every other generator zero times, since simple roots are a
basis. The first generator applied must be i_0: all other path nodes
have zero highest-weight label. After any initial segment, every unused
node other than the next one commutes with all generators already
applied and kills v. Hence the only potentially nonzero order is the
path order. It is nonzero by the preceding paragraph, so the weight
space is exactly one-dimensional. The same reasoning applies to all
prefixes and to the original highest weight.

Consequently both nu_(k-1) and nu_k enter D_lambda with coefficient one,
and their difference is alpha_j. This also covers k=0, comparing lambda
with lambda-alpha_j. Thus Q is contained in D_lambda. The highest weight
itself has multiplicity one, giving lambda in D_lambda.
Conversely every weight is congruent to lambda modulo Q, so every
m_mu mu belongs to Q+Z lambda. These two inclusions prove
D_lambda=Q+Z lambda. No assumption that the representation is
multiplicity-free is needed, and no cancellation of multiplicities is used.

## Trace duality and a finite matrix formula

In fundamental-weight coordinates let C_ij=<alpha_j,alpha_i^vee> and
d_i=(alpha_i,alpha_i)/2. Thus C has the simple-root columns, and the
simple-coroot Gram matrix is

\[
H=C\operatorname{diag}(d_i^{-1}),\qquad
M_\lambda=\sum_\mu m_\mu\mu\mu^t=I_\lambda H.
\]

The standard trace-index identity supplies the second equality. Your
[weight-moment formula](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01538v1) gives

\[
\theta_\lambda^*(\omega)=M_\lambda^{-1}
                         \sum_\mu m_\mu\mu e^\mu.
\]

For a linear coefficient column ell, Laurent integrality says exactly
ell^t M_lambda^(-1)D_lambda is integral. Therefore
Lambda_lambda=M_lambda D_lambda^#, where # denotes ordinary coordinate
duality. Since the weight inner-product matrix is H^(-1), this is exactly
I_lambda D_lambda^* in (1).

For a usable matrix formula take any column basis B_D of Q+Z lambda,
computed from the r+1 columns [C | lambda]. Then

\[
\boxed{B_\Lambda=I_\lambda H B_D^{-t}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Weyl's dimension product computes dim V(lambda) from the positive roots;
the index formula then computes I_lambda. Neither calculation enumerates
the weights of V(lambda). Thus even very large highest weights require
only root-system data, rational arithmetic and matrices of rank r.
The basis in (2) is integral, as also follows by differentiating the
integral Laurent pullback at the identity, where d(theta_lambda)=id.

## Consequences for Springer and Borel maps

The [trace-source theorem](TRACE_SOURCE_THEOREM.md), whose
[audit](TRACE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) specifies its hypotheses, now gives

\[
\mathcal C_\lambda=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(\Lambda_\lambda),\qquad
\mathcal A_{\lambda,L,\mathbb Z}
        =\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(\Lambda_\lambda)^{W_L}.
\]

The polynomial-character map from the Kumar papers is the inverse
Springer pullback followed by beta^P. Thus (1) determines the integral
polynomial input form for every exceptional irreducible representation,
before computing its Levi invariants or Borel image.

If two representations have the same unscaled lattice in the table,
their degree-d Levi sources and Borel images differ by the scalar
(I_lambda/I_eta)^d. This holds for every standard Levi and all degrees.
In particular it applies to every pair of irreducibles in G2/F4/E8,
and within either of the two displayed E6/E7 classes. The image statement
is an equality of scaled lattices in rational cohomology; both original
images are integral. It does not assert image saturation or surjectivity.

The earlier results are recovered exactly:

| Representation | Index | Linear lattice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G2, dimension 7 / adjoint 14 | 2 / 8 | 2 Q_long / 8 Q_long |
| F4, dimension 26 / adjoint 52 | 6 / 18 | 6 Q_long / 18 Q_long |
| E6, dimension 27 / adjoint 78 | 6 / 24 | 6 Q / 24 P |
| E7, dimension 56 / adjoint 133 | 12 / 36 | 12 Q / 36 P |
| E8, adjoint 248 | 60 | 60 P=60 Q |

This classifies the torus input forms; explicit invariant presentations
and complete Borel-image tables for all exceptional parabolics are
separate tasks. It does not calculate new E8 cohomology tables.

## Why irreducibility cannot be omitted

Three copies of the F4 module V(omega4) have trace index 18, equal to the
adjoint module V(omega1). All their central characters are trivial.
However repetition leaves the normalized Springer morphism unchanged:
both its trace matrix and its moments are multiplied by three. Hence

\[
\Lambda_{3V(\omega_4)}=6Q_{\rm long},\qquad
\Lambda_{V(\omega_1)}=18Q_{\rm long}.
\]

Their index and central character agree but their linear lattices differ.
For an arbitrary direct sum the exact formula M_rho D_rho^# still applies
with the actual combined multiplicities. Replacing D_rho by a sum of the
irreducible D_lambdas is not justified when weight spaces overlap.
The new API accepts a single highest weight; the existing
SpringerCharacterMap continues to handle sums by actual coefficients.

## API and independent verification

Version 0.16 adds a highest-weight-only calculation:

```python
from sage_borel import irreducible_character_lattice

answer = irreducible_character_lattice("E6", (1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0))
assert answer.dimension == 27
assert answer.trace_index == 6
assert answer.central_character_order == 3
assert answer.lattice_type == "Q"  # the full lattice is index * Q
assert answer.linear_basis_matrix.ncols() == 6

huge = irreducible_character_lattice("E8", (100, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0))
assert huge.dimension > 10**30
assert huge.lattice_type == "P"
```

The result exposes `trace_gram`, `moment_lattice_basis`,
`linear_basis_matrix`, `linear_generators`, and `multiplicity_one_paths`.
This constructor does not compute `theta`; use `springer_character_map`
when explicit Laurent formulas are wanted. That existing constructor
retains its full weight/coefficient calculation as a separate check.

The [Sage survey](experiments/irreducible_lattice_survey.py) tests bounded
highest-weight families in all exceptional types, classical controls,
and huge highest weights. Cases of dimension at most 10,000 are also
compared with the full Laurent/Hermite construction. The
[saved data](experiments/irreducible-lattice-survey.json) record each case
and which full comparisons were performed.

The [Python-only audit](experiments/audit_irreducible_lattices.py) imports
no Sage or runtime library. It reuses the earlier independent auditor's
root/reflection and root-string/Freudenthal utilities, reconstructing
complete weights for the dimension-bounded cases. A separate truncated
Kostant character calculation checks every multiplicity-one path prefix,
including those for the huge highest weights. It verifies the index,
central congruences, coefficient integrality and lattice determinants
with exact rational arithmetic, without using Sage's Hermite computation.
Its [report](experiments/irreducible-lattice-audit.json) distinguishes full
weight reconstruction from root-only checks. Finite tests support the
implementation; the all-representation statement rests on the proof above.

## Published inputs and audit limits

- Ordinary weight-span and weight-difference lattice facts are established
  background; see [Larsen--Pink, Section 3, author-hosted version](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~pink/ftp/LP1.pdf).
  Their ordinary weight statement alone does not establish the equality
  for the scalar-weighted generators m_mu mu; the path argument supplies it.
- The normalized trace Springer morphism and its normalization come from
  [Kostant--Michor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109066).
- The explicit weight-moment formula is from
  [Rogers, Theorem 1 and Section 3, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01538v1).
- The index formula and integrality are classical Dynkin results, recorded
  with our precise normalization in
  [Panyushev, Theorem 1.1 and Example 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.0398).
- The highest-weight generation and simple-root relations used in the
  path proof are standard; see
  [Etingof, Sections 27.8-27.9](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/lnlg.pdf).
- The polynomial-character/inverse-Springer/Borel construction is from
  [Kumar, Part I, Definition 4 and Theorem 5](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar76.pdf).

The weighted-lattice argument and its Springer-source interpretation are
written out here as deductions from these inputs. Focused searches for
multiplicity-one weights generating lattices, weighted weight lattices,
and Springer/Dynkin-index integral forms did not establish priority for
this formulation. It may be known in equivalent language. The proof
audit here is not external peer review or formal verification.
