# Proof and literature audit of the irreducible lattice formula

Audit date: 2026-10-20. Scope: the theorem and exceptional table in
[Irreducible Springer character lattices](IRREDUCIBLE_CHARACTER_LATTICES.md).
No gap was found under the stated hypotheses, and no numerical formula
or runtime change was needed. This is a mathematical argument and source
audit, not external peer review or formal verification.

The formula is best described here as an explicit integral-source
corollary of trace projection, Dynkin's index and standard highest-weight
theory. The exact weighted-lattice/Springer formulation was not located
in the reviewed sources. That does not establish originality or absence
from the literature; priority remains unresolved.

## Statement being checked

G is a simply connected simple complex group, P=X(T) its weight lattice,
Q its root lattice, and V(lambda) a nontrivial irreducible representation.
The Springer map is normalized trace projection, with differential the
identity at 1. Long roots have squared length two. For distinct weights
mu and their multiplicities m_mu, define

\[
D_\lambda=\sum_\mu\mathbb Z(m_\mu\mu),\qquad
L^*=\{v:(v,L)\subset\mathbb Z\},\qquad
I_\lambda=\frac{\dim V(\lambda)}{\dim\mathfrak g}
                         (\lambda,\lambda+2\rho_W).
\]

The claim is

\[
D_\lambda=Q+\mathbb Z\lambda,\qquad
\Lambda_\lambda=I_\lambda(Q+\mathbb Z\lambda)^*.
\]

**Multiplicity weighting is a scalar multiplication here.** Repeating
mu a total of m_mu times in a list of lattice generators would instead
give the ordinary weight span. That different definition cannot prove
the displayed statement. The trivial module, arbitrary direct sums,
positive-characteristic simple modules, affine types and unspecified
normalizations are not covered by this classification.

Faithfulness of the group representation is not required: a nontrivial
representation of a simple complex Lie algebra has faithful differential,
and a finite central kernel is allowed. All polynomial/cohomology
comparisons below use fixed common fundamental-weight coordinates.

## Step-by-step proof audit

### Selecting paths and producing nonzero vectors

For target node j choose a shortest path from the support of lambda to j.
Its initial node has positive highest-weight label and all later nodes
have label zero: otherwise a shorter path starts at a later supported
node. The path has distinct vertices and no chords. These facts include
the case j already lies in the support, when the path has one vertex.

At every new node i, e_i commutes with all earlier f-generators because
their indices differ. It consequently kills the preceding vector. The
current h_i-weight is the initial label at the first step, or minus the
off-diagonal Cartan entry for the preceding edge at a later step. Both
are positive. Finite-dimensional sl2 theory therefore proves that the
next lowering operation is nonzero. Double/triple edges cause a positive
integer larger than one, not a failure of the argument.

Verdict: valid in characteristic zero. In characteristic p the same
numerical positivity need not imply the asserted action on a simple
module, which is one reason that case is outside the theorem.

### Why the full weight space has dimension one

By highest-weight theory and the Chevalley--Serre presentation, the full
module is spanned by words in simple negative generators applied to its
highest vector. Passing to a fixed weight component is legitimate: each
word is homogeneous in simple-root degree. For a path-prefix degree,
every selected simple generator must occur once and all other generators
must occur zero times. Other negative-root generators give no extra
vectors, since they themselves are generated by the simple ones.

The first applied generator must be the initial supported node; all
other selected nodes kill the highest vector. Suppose an initial path
segment has been applied. Any remaining node except the next node is
nonadjacent to that segment. Its lowering generator commutes past all
the previous ones and kills the highest vector. Thus a nonzero word
must follow the path order. There is exactly one such candidate, and
the preceding sl2 argument proves it is nonzero. This gives both the
upper and lower bounds on the dimension of the full weight space.

Verdict: valid. Exhibiting one nonzero word alone would not prove
multiplicity one; the word-order upper bound is essential.

### Recovering the weighted lattice

For every target root alpha_j, the two successive path-prefix weights
both have multiplicity one, so their difference alpha_j belongs to D.
For a one-node path these are lambda and lambda-alpha_j. If the latter
weight is zero its contribution to D is zero, which does not invalidate
the difference argument. Also lambda itself has multiplicity one.
These give Q+Z lambda contained in D. Conversely every weight lies in
lambda+Q, so every m_mu mu lies in Q+Z lambda. This proves equality.

Verdict: valid. There is no division by m_mu. Extremal Weyl-orbit weights
alone would not suffice: for A1 with highest weight 4 omega they span
4 Z omega, whereas Q+Z lambda=2 Z omega. The nonextremal path weights
are needed.

### Coordinate duality, metric duality and the index

Put C_ij=<alpha_j,alpha_i^vee>, d_i=(alpha_i,alpha_i)/2 and
H=C diag(d_i^(-1)). The columns of C are roots in fundamental weights;
H is symmetric and is the simple-coroot Gram matrix. The weight
inner-product matrix is H^(-1). The trace identity with our specified
Dynkin normalization is M=I_lambda H.

The weight-moment Springer formula implies that a linear column ell
has integral pullback exactly when ell^t M^(-1)D is integral. Since M
is symmetric, this means ell belongs to M D^#, where # is ordinary
coordinate duality. Metric duality is instead D^*=H D^#. Consequently
M D^#=I_lambda D^*, with the claimed scalar and direction of duality.

Verdict: valid. In particular I_ad=2h^vee, not h^vee. The latter convention
would produce a factor-of-two error if inserted without changing the
formula. The rank-one control independently compares all coefficients
of the normalized character derivative with the closed index formula.

### Exceptional specializations

For simply laced types, Q^*=P and P^*=Q with the chosen scale. Since P/Q
has prime order three for E6 and two for E7, Q+Z lambda is Q for trivial
central character and P otherwise. Duality reverses those two choices.
For E8 the two lattices coincide.

For G2/F4, P=Q, so the dual is the embedded coroot lattice. In the scale
where long roots have squared length two, this is Q_long, not Q.
The independent root check exhibits long-root bases and verifies its
index in P: three for G2 and four for F4. The E6/E7 charge congruences
are checked by charge*C=0 modulo the prime, a nonzero charge, and
det(C) equal to that prime; this proves equality of their kernels with Q.

Verdict: valid. The two-case E6/E7 table cannot be transferred unchanged
to composite/noncyclic centers such as A3 or D4. The uniform dual-lattice
formula and the API retain Q+Z lambda in those cases.

### Higher degrees and Borel comparison

The linear classification is independent of the proof that the full
polynomial source is Sym_Z(Lambda). The latter result is used separately,
with its hypotheses checked in the [trace-source audit](TRACE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md).
Taking W_L invariants does not imply generation by linear invariant
elements. If Lambda_lambda=I_lambda L_0 and Lambda_eta=I_eta L_0,
homogeneous symmetric powers and invariants scale by
(I_lambda/I_eta)^d. Applying the same linear Borel map preserves this
equality of image lattices in rational cohomology.

Verdict: valid. This compares inverse-Springer inputs and their Borel
images in common polynomial coordinates; it does not identify the two
Springer morphisms, or directly rescale their character functions. It
does not prove saturation, surjectivity, or a complete invariant-ring
presentation.

## Boundary checks and independent verification

The existing survey checks 490 cases, including 90 full Laurent
comparisons, and its separate audit reconstructs those 90 weight systems
and checks 6,711 Kostant path prefixes. They remain finite implementation
checks, not substitutes for the proof.

The new [boundary-check script](experiments/audit_irreducible_lattice_edges.py)
uses no Sage or runtime-library imports. It reuses the explicitly
documented independent root/arithmetic and Kostant utilities. It exhausts
every nonempty support mask in each of the five fixed exceptional
diagrams, enumerates the orders of distinct path generators, and checks
the associated Kostant character coefficients. Support masks suffice for
this word-order test: at an unused node the current label is the initial
nonnegative label plus positive contributions from used adjacent nodes,
so its positivity depends only on the support and previous nodes.

It also checks:

- Nonshortest paths: A2 at (1,1) gives multiplicity two at path degree
  (1,1), and A3 at (1,0,1) gives multiplicity three at (1,1,1).
- Rank-one coefficients and dual normalization for highest weights 1-32;
  an extremal-only lattice counterexample at highest weight 4.
- The long-root/coroot scale and E6/E7 central-charge kernels.
- A full independent F4 direct-sum counterexample. A unit determinant of
  actual multiplicity-one minimal weights proves D_min=P, so D_triple=3P.
  The adjoint contains the simple roots, giving D_adj=P. Their moment
  matrices agree, but their source covolumes differ by 3^4.

The [saved boundary report](experiments/irreducible-lattice-edge-audit.json)
records exact counts and source hashes. Full 133-test and isolated-wheel
17-test validation from the preceding release remains applicable to the
unchanged runtime. This follow-up changes proof exposition, attribution
and research verification only.

For a simply connected simple group, the ordinary weight-span statement
has the familiar form span_Z{mu}=Q+Z lambda: all weights lie in lambda+Q,
the highest weight occurs, and the root lattice is generated by weight
differences. This established statement helps explain why the center
appears. The weighted equality D_lambda=Q+Z lambda still needs the
multiplicity-one argument; it is not justified merely by citing the
ordinary result.

## Search record and conclusion

Searches on 2026-10-20 included equivalent language involving
multiplicity-one weight spans, weight multiplicities and generated
lattices, formal-character derivatives, Weyl-character derivatives in
characteristic p, trace-dual lattices, Dynkin index and central character,
Springer integral sources, and generalized Cayley maps. Representative
queries were `"weights of multiplicity one" "lattice"`,
`"lattice generated" "weight multiplicities"`,
`"Springer morphism" "central character"`,
`"generalized Cayley map" "Dynkin index"`, and
`"weighted weight lattice" representation`.
Primary papers were checked rather than treating secondary search
summaries as evidence for an exact theorem. The search did not include
unavailable/private sources or prove that every alternative terminology
has been exhausted.

**Conclusion:** the classification proof passes this audit under its
stated hypotheses. Its ordinary weight-lattice and index ingredients
are known. The exact multiplicity-weighted lattice and integral Springer
formulation were not located in the reviewed sources, so their priority
remains unresolved. Present this as a proved integral-source deduction
from established theory; do not describe the search as establishing a
new theorem, an open problem posed by those authors, or an absence result
about the literature.
