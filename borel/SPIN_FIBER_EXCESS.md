# A uniform counterexample to isolated quotient-critical Cayley fibers

Date: 2026-10-23. Characteristic zero. This note **refutes** the proposed
generic-Morse assertion for all fundamental representations of simple groups.
It does not refute generic reducedness. The proof uses standard spin branching,
Steinberg's quotient theorem and the repository's general critical-potential
reduction. The deduction is written here; independent novelty is not established.

## 1. The assertion being tested

Let G be simply connected and complex simple, rho a nontrivial fundamental
representation, and theta its trace-normalized Springer/generalized Cayley map:

    B(X,Y)=tr(d rho(X) d rho(Y)),
    B(theta(g),X)=tr(rho(g) d rho(X)).

For a fixed Levi subgroup K, consider semisimple h in the center of Lie(K)
with Z_G(h)=K. Write F_h=theta^(-1)(h), r=rank G, and G_reg for the elements
whose centralizers have dimension r. The candidate assertion was:

> For generic such h, F_h intersect G_reg is empty or smooth of dimension
> dim K-r; equivalently, its corrected quotient potential has isolated
> nondegenerate critical points.

Here generic is within the specified centralizer stratum, not generic in
the entire Lie algebra. The candidate is false, even for a minuscule
fundamental representation and even when the regular fiber is everywhere smooth.

## 2. The all-rank counterexample

**Theorem.** Let n>=2, G=Spin_(2n+1), and rho=V_(omega_n) its irreducible
spin representation of dimension D=2^n. Choose a central Lie generator Z
whose spin eigenvalues are +1 and -1, each D/2 times, and whose defining
vector eigenvalues are +2,-2 and 2n-1 zeros. For every lambda!=0 set
h=lambda Z. Then

    K=Z_G(h) = (Spin_(2n-1) x G_m)/mu_2,
    dim K=(n-1)(2n-1)+1,       rank K=n.

The regular part F_h intersect G_reg is nonempty and smooth, everywhere of
dimension

    dim K-n+(n-2) = n(2n-3).

Its quotient-critical locus has dimension n-2 and has nondegenerate Hessian
in the two normal directions. In particular, for n>=3 the proposed dimension
dim K-n=2(n-1)^2 is too small by n-2, for **every** target in this stratum.

For Spin_7 with its eight-dimensional spin representation, the proposed
dimension is 8 and the actual regular fiber dimension is 9. For n=2 the
excess is zero, so this endpoint is a consistency check, not a counterexample.

### 2.1 Branching, with the isogeny retained

Let S=Spin_(2n-1). On the central cover S x G_m -> K, write z for the
G_m coordinate. The kernel is generated by (-1,-1). The spin restriction is

    rho|_(S x G_m) = (S_spin tensor z) direct_sum (S_spin tensor z^(-1)),
    chi(s,z) = (z+z^(-1)) y(s),       y=chi_(S_spin).

This follows directly from the usual spin weights (+/-1/2,...,+/-1/2):
the first sign gives z or z^(-1), while the remaining signs give S_spin.
The Lie generator Z corresponds to z d/dz, so B(Z,Z)=D. The pair of
summands also shows B(Z,Lie(S))=0. These observations fix the normalization.

By Steinberg's theorem S//S is affine (n-1)-space with fundamental
characters as coordinates. Take y to be its spin coordinate and write
u_1,...,u_(n-2) for the remaining fundamental characters. Thus the quotient
of the cover has coordinates (z,y,u), with z invertible.

The deck involution acts as (z,y,u) -> (-z,-y,u): the other fundamental
representations of S descend to its orthogonal quotient. This action is free
because z is invertible. Consequently the cover is etale also on adjoint
quotients. For n=2 the list u is empty and S=Spin_3=SL_2.

### 2.2 Exact critical equations and reduced structure

Put c=D lambda. Locally, up to an additive constant, the corrected potential is

    W_h(z,y,u) = (z+z^(-1))y - c log z.

The logarithm denotes a local formal primitive; its differential is algebraic.
Although z need not descend as a character of K, this calculation is on the
specified etale cover and its differential descends. Multiplying the derivative
in z by the unit z gives the exact critical equations

    z+z^(-1)=0,
    (z-z^(-1))y=c,
    partial W_h / partial u_j=0 identically.

Scheme-theoretically, in C[z,z^(-1),y,u] their ideal is

    (z^2+1, (z^2-1)y-cz) = (z^2+1, 2y+cz).

It is reduced and is the disjoint union

    z=i,  y=c/(2i),   u arbitrary;
    z=-i, y=c/(-2i),  u arbitrary.

Each piece is affine (n-2)-space; the involution interchanges them and fixes u.
Thus the quotient-critical locus downstairs is affine (n-2)-space as well.
This statement counts quotient-critical pieces, not components of the entire
possibly nonregular Springer fiber.

The Hessian in the two coordinates (y,z) has determinant

    -(1-z^(-2))^2 = -4

on the critical locus. Its mixed entry is 2; the other u rows and columns
are zero. The critical scheme is therefore smooth with a nondegenerate normal
Hessian (a Morse-Bott critical locus). There is no transverse nilpotent
thickening at these quotient points.

### 2.3 From the quotient to the entire group

Every point of K//K has a regular K lift: use a regular representative for
S//S and the central torus coordinate, then descend the central isogeny.
At such a lift the quotient map is smooth. The trace identity is

    d chi - B(h,g^(-1)dg) = B(theta(g)-h,g^(-1)dg).

Consequently every regular lift of the above critical locus lies in F_h.
For a point of F_h, equivariance gives Z_G(g) subset Z_G(h)=K. Thus
Z_G(g)=Z_K(g), and a regular K lift is also G-regular. This proves
nonemptiness for every lambda!=0 without assuming the existence of a
regular semisimple lift.

Conversely, the whole fiber lies scheme-theoretically in K. At every regular
fiber point the [general critical-potential theorem](GENERAL_CAYLEY_FIBER_CRITICAL.md)
identifies its completed local ring with the quotient-critical local ring
adjoined dim K-n formal smooth variables. The local ring here is therefore
a power series ring in

    (n-2)+(dim K-n)=n(2n-3)

variables. This proves the smoothness and dimension assertions for the regular
part of the full Springer fiber. The full differential rank there is 4n,
since dim G=n(2n+1). It is not merely a calculation on the maximal torus.

## 3. What generalizes: branching can force excess dimension

**Proposition (unused quotient directions).** In the setting of the general
critical-potential theorem, assume the quotient is smooth and the quotient
map is smooth at the fiber point. Suppose local quotient coordinates can be
chosen as (v_1,...,v_(r-e),u_1,...,u_e), and the corrected potential is
independent of all u_j:

    W_h(v,u)=W_0(v).

Then its critical scheme is locally Crit(W_0) times a smooth e-dimensional
factor. If W_0 has an isolated nondegenerate critical point, the regular
Springer fiber is smooth of dimension dim K-r+e. More generally its completed
local ring is the Jacobian algebra of W_0 with e+dim K-r formal variables
adjoined. In particular, any nonempty such germ has dimension at least
dim K-r+e, whether or not the residual critical point is nondegenerate.

**Proof.** The u derivatives vanish identically. The remaining gradient ideal
is extended from the v power series ring. Apply the smooth-quotient product
formula; a nonsingular Hessian makes the residual Jacobian algebra C.

This is an elementary consequence of the existing general theorem, not a new
singularity-theory theorem. Its useful representation-theoretic input is
finding the factorization of the corrected character potential from Levi
branching. Independence must hold in actual independent local quotient
coordinates, not merely in a redundant list of torus expressions. The target
logarithms must also factor through v; absence from chi alone is insufficient.

In the spin family v=(y,z) and e=n-2. Thus the counterexample gives a general
obstruction to the candidate, not just a low-rank exception. Adding the
hypothesis that rho is irreducible, fundamental or minuscule does not remove it.

## 4. Status, checks and the next question

**Published inputs.** The spin weights and branching are standard; see
[Etingof, Lecture 31.2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec05.pdf).
Fundamental-character quotient coordinates and regular lifts come from
[Steinberg, Regular elements of semisimple algebraic groups](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1965__25__49_0.pdf).
The trace-projection map and the spin Cayley formulas are in
[Kostant-Michor, especially Section 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109066).
For odd-dimensional Clifford modules their multiplicity convention does not
change the trace-normalized map: multiplying a representation by a common
multiplicity scales both sides of its defining identity equally.

**Written deduction here.** The exact critical ideal, regular-fiber dimension
and counterexample follow from those inputs and the general reduction. No
claim is made that this fiber description is unpublished. This turn checked
the spin-character and Kostant-Michor sources, not an exhaustive literature
search for this particular boundary stratum.

**Relation to prior work.** Here chi=0. The existing
[Spin boundary calculation](SPIN_CAYLEY_GEOMETRY.md) places such images in
defining-vector rank at most two, consistent with the targets above. The
open-chart classical Cayley formula cannot be used at chi=0. Neither the
published generic degree nor the existing boundary rank bound alone gives
the dimension and reducedness proved here. Previous type A and C theorems
on their specified strata are unchanged.

**Independent checks.** [The verifier](tools/verify_spin_fiber_excess.py)
uses original Clifford matrices, not the quotient equations, in ranks 2..5
at two exact points per rank. It checks all Lie algebra trace equations,
the complete differential, and both source and target centralizer dimensions.
Separate highest-weight calculations check spin characters through rank 7.
A symbolic ideal calculation checks reducedness and normal Hessian determinant
over QQ(c). Finite computations corroborate the proof; they do not replace
the all-rank or every-target argument. See the
[verification record](spin-fiber-excess-verification.json).

**Not concluded.** Components or embedded structure wholly in the nonregular
locus are not classified. Generic degrees, minimum Cayley degrees and integral
Borel images are not computed here. Generic reducedness has not been refuted:
these regular fibers are reduced despite their excess dimension.

**Next target.** Determine a structural criterion on Levi branching for the
critical scheme of the corrected potential to be generically smooth, allowing
positive-dimensional critical loci. First identify smooth factorizations that
force unused quotient directions, then decide whether the residual potential
is generically Morse. The measurable goal is a criterion with checkable
hypotheses, or a fundamental-representation counterexample with persistent
residual degeneracy. A universal Morse-Bott assertion is a question to test,
not a replacement theorem asserted on this evidence.
