# Even/odd presentation of the symplectic projective K-cokernel

22 October 2026. Repository deduction with a complete argument below.
The ambient projective-bundle theorem, characteristic map and prior
literal-image identification are inputs. This is not external expert
review and no originality claim is made. Targeted searches for the
specific even/odd cokernel and Fitting formulation have not established
its priority; absence of a located statement is not evidence of novelty.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and statement

Let G=Sp_(2n)(C), rho its defining representation, P=P1, n>=1.
Use its full literal integral source, not the rationally normalized
epsilon generator. Let

\[
 R=\mathbb Z[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_n^{\pm1}],\quad s_i=t_i+t_i^{-1},
 \quad A=R[L]/\prod_i(L^2-s_iL+1),\quad b=L-L^{-1}.
\]

The [previous source proof](OTHER_THEORIES_EQUIVARIANT_K.md#7-an-integral-symplectic-projective-family)
identifies A with K_T(P^(2n-1)) and the image with R[b].
Put C=A/R[b], a quotient of modules, not a quotient ring.
The subring R[b] acts on C. Define polynomials by

\[
 P(s)=\prod_i(s-s_i)=E(s^2)+sO(s^2),\qquad
 \widetilde E(z)=E(z^2+4),\quad \widetilde O(z)=O(z^2+4),
 \quad G(z)=\widetilde E(z)+z\widetilde O(z).
\]

**Theorem.** As an R[z]-module, with z acting by b,

\[
 \boxed{C\simeq R[z]/(G(z),\,2\widetilde O(z)).} \tag{1}
\]

G is monic of degree n. If N is multiplication by O-tilde on
B=R[z]/G, then an n-by-n presentation over R is

\[
 R^n\xrightarrow{2N}R^n\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow0. \tag{2}
\]

The same construction holds over the universal spectral ring
R0=Z[s_1,...,s_n]. Its base change to R is the actual torus result.
Indeed each t_i satisfies the monic relation t_i^2-s_it_i+1=0,
with invertible constant term. R is finite free of rank 2^n over R0.

## 2. Proof of the reduction

Introduce z=b. The identity L^-1=L-z makes the projective ring

\[
 A\simeq R[z,L]/(L^2-zL-1,\ P(2L-z)).
\]

The first relation already makes L invertible. Set s=2L-z.
Then s^2=z^2+4, and

\[
 P(2L-z)=F(z)+2L\widetilde O(z),\qquad
 F=\widetilde E-z\widetilde O.
\]

Before imposing P, the quadratic algebra is free on 1,L over R[z].
Its ideal generated by P is spanned over R[z] by P and LP, since
every multiplier reduces to a+cL. Their coefficient vectors are

\[
 (F,\,2\widetilde O),\qquad
 (2\widetilde O,\,F+2z\widetilde O).
\]

Now quotient by the image of R[z], which removes the first coordinate.
The remaining generator, the class of L, has precisely the two
relations 2 O-tilde and F+2z O-tilde=G. This proves (1), without
dividing by two or claiming that C is a quotient algebra of A.

If n is even the leading term of G comes from E-tilde; if n is odd
it comes from z O-tilde. In either case it is z^n. Thus B is free
on 1,z,...,z^(n-1), and quotienting it by multiplication by
2 O-tilde gives (2). This is an all-rank proof, not an inference
from a finite list of Smith computations.

## 3. All Fitting ideals and a closed parity formula

Write I_k(N) for the ideal of k-minors. Then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)=2^{n-i}I_{n-i}(N)\ (0\le i<n),
 \qquad \operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)=R\ (i\ge n).} \tag{3}
\]

This is a reduced determinantal formula for every ideal. It does not
classify minimal generators of each I_k in closed form. The existing
determinant result gives, with F0=prod_(i<j)(s_i+s_j),

\[
 \operatorname{Fitt}_0(C)=(2^n F_0^2).
\]

There is a useful new closed formula at the other end:

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Fitt}_{n-1}(C)=
 \begin{cases}
 (2),&n\text{ odd},\\
 2(e_1(s),e_3(s),\ldots,e_{n-1}(s)),&n\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}} \tag{4}
\]

The entries of a multiplication matrix on a monic quotient generate
exactly the coefficient ideal of its multiplier: the first column
contains those coefficients, and reduction of every other column is
an integral linear combination of them. O-tilde has degree <=n-1.
Translation of its argument by four is an invertible triangular
change of coefficients over Z. Consequently its coefficient ideal is
that of O. If n is odd O is monic, so that ideal is the whole ring.
If n is even its coefficients, up to signs, are exactly the odd
elementary symmetric functions in the s_i. This proves (4).

At the height-one prime (2) of the torus ring, F0 is a unit:
its reduction is a nonzero Laurent polynomial over F2. The old
determinant and (2) therefore make N invertible there. The local
Smith factors of the full 2n inclusion are n units and n copies
of two. Larger two-powers after t_i=1 arise at the intersection
with the other character divisors; they are not the generic
two-primary local pattern.

## 4. Complete global decompositions in ranks one, two and three

For n=1, O=1 and G=z-s_1, so C=R/(2).
For n=2, O=-e_1(s) is constant, and

\[
 C\simeq(R/(2(s_1+s_2)))^2. \tag{5}
\]

For n=3 put e_j=e_j(s), d=e_2+4, c=4e_1+e_3 and
q=e_3-e_1e_2=-F0. Then G=z^3-e_1z^2+dz-c, and
the matrix N in the monomial basis is

\[
 N=\begin{pmatrix}d&c&e_1c\\0&0&q\\1&e_1&e_1^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Move its unit entry to the upper left. Subtract e_1 and e_1^2
times the first column from the other columns and clear the first
column. The remaining block is [[0,q],[q,e_1q]]. Swap its columns
and subtract e_1 times its first row from the second. These are
unimodular integral operations, giving diag(1,q,q). Hence

\[
 \boxed{C\simeq R/(2)\ \oplus\ (R/(2F0))^2\quad(n=3).} \tag{6}
\]

In rank three, all ideals are therefore
Fitt_0=(8F0^2), Fitt_1=(4F0), Fitt_2=(2), and Fitt_i=R for i>=3.
At t_i=1, n=2 yields the nonunit factors (8,8), and n=3
yields (2,128,128), with respectively two and three unit factors
added for the full ambient inclusion.

No global cyclic decomposition is asserted for n>=4. For example,
rank four has last ideal 2(e_1,e_3), so it cannot be replaced
without proof by a single product of root quotients.

## 5. Uniform doubled half-size presentation

The following strengthens the rank-n presentation. All isomorphisms in
this section are over R, not assertions about the action of z.
Write n=2r+epsilon, epsilon in {0,1}, and define an r-by-r matrix

\[
 H=\begin{cases}
 m_O\text{ on }R[u]/E(u),&n=2r,\\
 m_E\text{ on }R[u]/O(u),&n=2r+1.
 \end{cases}
\]

The modulus is monic of degree r in either case. Empty matrices are
allowed when n=1. Set D=coker(2H). Then

\[
 \boxed{C\simeq (R/(2))^\epsilon\oplus D\oplus D.} \tag{7}
\]

More strongly, 2N is integrally unimodularly equivalent to
diag(2H,2H) in even rank and diag(2,2H,2H) in odd rank.
Thus the claim remains valid under every coefficient-ring base change,
including singular fibers and characteristic two. It does not divide
by two, and it does not assert that D is globally cyclic.

**Even-rank proof.** Put p=E-tilde and q=O-tilde, so G=p+zq,
p is monic of degree n and deg(q)<=n-2. For a polynomial f of
degree less than n, let K(f) be the quotient on monic division of
qf by p. If r(f) is the remainder, then

\[
 qf=pK(f)+r(f)=G K(f)-zqK(f)+r(f).
\]

Consequently N_G T=N_p, where N_G and N_p multiply by q in the
respective monic quotients, and T(f)=f+zK(f). The correction zK(f)
has degree at most deg(f)-1. T is therefore triangular with diagonal
one over R, hence unimodular. Both p and q are even polynomials.
The even and odd monomial subspaces of R[z]/p are preserved by q;
in bases 1,z^2,... and z,z^3,... the two multiplication blocks
are identical. Each is multiplication by O(v+4) modulo E(v+4),
where v=z^2. The change u=v+4 is another integral triangular
basis change with diagonal one. This proves N_G equivalent to H+H.

**Odd-rank proof.** Here q is monic of degree n-1 and G is
monic of degree n. In the target use the basis
q,1,z,...,z^(n-2). This is a unimodular change from the monomial
basis. The first domain vector 1 maps to q and hence gives a unit
pivot. For j=1,...,n-1 divide

\[
 qz^j=G k_j+r_j,\qquad \deg r_j<n.
\]

The polynomial k_j is monic of degree j-1. Modulo q the target
remainder is -(G mod q)k_j. Thus, after removing the unit pivot,
the remaining block is minus multiplication by G mod q on R[z]/q,
followed by the basis change with columns k_1,...,k_(n-1).
That basis change is triangular with diagonal one. Clearing the
pivot row uses only its unit entry. Since G mod q=E-tilde mod q,
both defining polynomials are again even; parity and translation
give two copies of H. We obtain N equivalent to diag(1,-H,-H).
Changing row signs and multiplying the entire equivalence by two
proves (7). For n=1 the remaining block is empty and C=R/(2).

This also explains paired Smith factors: after a base change to Z,
or to a DVR, every invariant factor of 2H occurs twice, together
with the single factor 2 in odd rank. The full projective inclusion
has n additional unit factors. This is a statement about these
specializations, not a Smith form over the multivariable ring R.

**All-rank Fitting convolution.** Put I_0(H)=R, and put I_j(H)=0
outside 0<=j<=r. Write

\[
 J_\ell=\sum_{a+b=\ell}I_a(H)I_b(H).
\]

The minors of a block diagonal matrix give this convolution. Since
I_j of any matrix is contained in I_(j-1), the optional unit block
in N lowers the required minor size by one. Therefore, for k=n-i>=1,

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)=
 \begin{cases}
 2^k J_k,&n\text{ even},\\
 2^k J_{k-1},&n\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}} \tag{8}
\]

The API now uses only minors of the half-size block for its general
fallback, followed by products of their generators. Formula (8)
does not classify closed minimal generators for every rank.

## 6. Complete closed rank-four Fitting formulas

Let n=4, a=e_1(s), c=e_2(s), d=e_3(s), e=e_4(s),
J=(a,d) and F0=product_(i<j)(s_i+s_j). Up to an irrelevant overall
sign, the half block before translation is

\[
 H_0=\begin{pmatrix}d&-ae\\a&d-ac\end{pmatrix},
 \quad \det H_0=d^2-acd+a^2e=-F_0.
\]

Equivalently after translation, with y=d+4a, v=c+8, w=e+4c+16,
one may use H_1=[[y,-aw],[a,y-av]]. It has the same determinant
and entry ideal (a,y)=(a,d)=J. Thus C is the direct sum of two
cokernels of 2H_0. Its complete Fitting list is

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Fitt}_0(C)&=(16F_0^2),\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_1(C)&=8F_0J,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_2(C)&=4J^2,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_3(C)&=2J,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)&=R\quad(i\ge4).
 \end{aligned}} \tag{9}
\]

Indeed I_1(H_0)=J and I_2(H_0)=(F0). For H_0+H_0, the
minor ideals are J, J^2+(F0), F0 J, (F0^2).
The displayed determinant belongs to J^2, proving (9).
This is an integral ideal identity, including at two; checking
only generic points or rational ideals would not suffice.

At t_i=1 the half block 2H_0 is [[64,-256],[16,-320]],
with integer Smith factors (16,1024). The full eight-dimensional
inclusion therefore has factors
(1,1,1,1,16,16,1024,1024), of total index 2^28.
The translated block gives the same factors.

## 7. Checks, known inputs and the remaining problem

The reduction and doubling use elementary quadratic algebras, monic
polynomial division, parity, and standard Fitting ideals, applied to
the previously identified literal image.
The ambient ring and characteristic-map theory are published:
[Griffeth-Ram](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405333).
The [prior determinant note](OTHER_THEORIES_EQUIVARIANT_K.md)
records the source/image argument and its independent projective checks.

The determinant's product of pairwise root sums is classical resultant
algebra: see [Holtz-Tyaglov, section 1.2 and Theorem 1.17 (Orlando)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.4703).
Their paper is not cited as establishing the present integral Springer
image or its full module splitting. The specific application (7)-(9)
is a proved deduction here; this targeted literature check does not
establish whether it has appeared elsewhere.

[The implementation and checks](CLASSICAL_EQUIVARIANT.md) compare the
doubled presentation with the original 2n inclusion by independent
projective matrices at ordinary, nonzero-resultant and singular integer
points through rank eight, with a separate rank-nine ordinary check.
The audit constructs exact change-of-basis certificates over independent
elementary coefficient variables in ranks 1-8, not just specialized
root values. It checks all rank-four minor ideals symbolically in
independent a,y,v,w, and tests the half-minor convolution against
original-inclusion integer Smith factors in ranks five and six.
Torus base change is also checked. These are finite corroborations;
the arbitrary-rank proof is the monic-division argument above.

Subsequent progress is in [the Schur-minor theorem](SYMPLECTIC_K_MINORS.md).
It proves that the minors using only the first j columns generate the
entire ideal I_j(H), over every coefficient ring. Integral Schur cyclicity
is the published input. Consequently all Fitting ideals have uniform
partition-indexed Schur-content generators, and explicit closed lists
were first proved through n=7. The subsequent [Hankel balancing theorem](SYMPLECTIC_K_BALANCING.md)
collapses the convolution to one product in every rank and gives explicit
quartic lists for n=8,9, including at two. Further generator simplification,
primary structure after moment substitution,
complete ordinary Smith multiplicities and larger isotropic flags
remain unresolved here. The initial-column formula is exact; it is not
a claim that every resulting list is minimal.
