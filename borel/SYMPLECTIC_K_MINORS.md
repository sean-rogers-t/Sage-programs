# Schur generators for integral multiplication-matrix ideals

22 October 2026. This is a deduction from established integral
exterior-power/Schubert calculus, applied to the defining symplectic
Springer image. It is not a claim of originality. The source, normalization
and doubled-module theorem are in [the preceding proof](SYMPLECTIC_K_FITTING.md).
The PDF and LaTeX exposition have not been changed.

## 1. General theorem over an arbitrary coefficient ring

Let A be any commutative ring, p(u) a monic polynomial of degree r>=1,
V=A[u]/p with ordered basis 1,u,...,u^(r-1), and q(u) any polynomial.
Let M be multiplication by q on V. For 1<=j<=r, let K_j be the
r-by-j matrix consisting of the first j columns of M.

**Theorem.**

\[
 \boxed{I_j(M)=I_j(K_j).} \tag{1}
\]

Here the left side means all j-by-j minors of the square matrix; the
right side means only the maximal minors of the rectangular matrix.
Thus binomial(r,j) explicit polynomials generate the entire ideal,
instead of binomial(r,j)^2. The statement is about ideals, including
their integral and nonreduced structure, not just their radicals or
their zero sets. It commutes with every coefficient-ring base change.

An equivalent partition-indexed generator family is

\[
 g_\lambda(p,q)=
 \det\left(\operatorname{coeff}_{u^{i_a}}
       \bigl(u^{b-1}q\bmod p\bigr)\right)_{a,b=1}^j,\qquad
 i_a=a-1+\lambda_{j+1-a},
 \quad \lambda\subseteq(r-j)^j. \tag{2}
\]

All divisions here are monic polynomial divisions. The ideal in (1)
is generated by these g_lambda. No inversion of a leading coefficient
of q, discriminant, resultant, integer prime, or factorial is used.

## 2. Integral proof and its published input

Let U be multiplication by u on V, and put
w0=1 wedge u wedge ... wedge u^(j-1) in exterior^j(V).
There are commuting integral elementary symmetric operators E_l on this
exterior power: E_l is the coefficient of t^l in exterior^j(1+tU).
Complete symmetric operators h_m are defined by

\[
 h_0=1,\quad h_m=0\ (m<0),\qquad
 \sum_{m\ge0}h_mt^m=
 \left(\sum_{\ell=0}^j(-1)^\ell E_\ell t^\ell\right)^{-1}. \tag{3}
\]

Their Giambelli operators are

\[
 S_\lambda=\det(h_{\lambda_a-a+b})_{a,b=1}^j.
\]

For an increasing index set I={i_1<...<i_j}, let
lambda=(i_j-j+1,...,i_2-1,i_1). Integral Schur calculus gives

\[
 S_\lambda w_0=u^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u^{i_j}. \tag{4}
\]

One elementary way to see why this identity is integral is the
alternant formula
det(x_b^(i_a))=s_lambda(x_1,...,x_j) det(x_b^(a-1)) over Z.
The quotient defining the Schur polynomial is an integral polynomial
identity before any coefficient specialization. Reduction modulo p in
each tensor factor preserves it. Equivalently one uses the operators
(3), which are defined directly on exterior powers and require no
antisymmetrization division. This avoids any assumption that j! or
two is invertible.

The integral symmetric action, its cyclic generator w0, and (4)
are published inputs: [Laksov-Thorup, Theorem 0.6 and Corollary 0.7](https://web.math.ku.dk/~thorup/temp/lt2b.pdf);
see also [Gatto-Santiago, sections 2-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702759).
The former treats arbitrary base rings and the monic factorization
algebra. These results are not being claimed as new here.

Since M=q(U), it commutes with U. Functoriality of exterior powers gives

\[
 (\wedge^j M)\wedge^j(1+tU)
 =\wedge^j(1+tU)(\wedge^j M).
\]

Consequently wedge^j(M) commutes with every E_l, h_m and S_lambda.
Combining with (4),

\[
 (\wedge^jM)(u^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u^{i_j})
 =S_\lambda(\wedge^jM)w_0. \tag{5}
\]

The coordinates of wedge^j(M) on the left are the minors for the
column set I. The coordinates of (wedge^j M)w0 are exactly the
initial-column minors (2). Every S_lambda is an integral A-linear
operator, so (5) writes every full minor as an A-linear combination
of the initial-column minors. The reverse inclusion is immediate,
since those minors were included in the full family. This proves (1).

Thus the minor theorem is a direct application of known Schur cyclicity,
not a new construction of Schubert calculus. Its computational use for
our specified Springer cokernel is the present extension.

## 3. A genuine Schubert-coordinate formulation

Let Fact_j(p) be the universal coefficient algebra of a monic
factorization p=p_j p_(r-j), with factors of degrees j and r-j.
It is finite free over A, with Schur basis indexed by
lambda contained in (r-j)^j. Under the established identification
of this algebra with the symmetric action on exterior^j(V), form

\[
 Q_j=\prod_{\ell=1}^j q(\xi_\ell)\in\operatorname{Fact}_j(p).
\]

The expression is symmetric in the formal roots of the chosen degree-j
factor, so it belongs to the factorization algebra. Then

\[
 Q_j=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq(r-j)^j}g_\lambda(p,q)s_\lambda,\qquad
 \boxed{I_j(M)=\operatorname{content}_A(Q_j).} \tag{6}
\]

The content means the ideal of all Schur-coordinate coefficients.
Equation (6) follows by applying both sides to w0 and comparing the
wedge basis in (4). It gives a uniform partition-indexed formula for
every ideal. It is not a classification of its minimal generators.
The auxiliary Grassmann/factorization algebra is a tool for this
linear algebra; it is not an identification of the original Sp/P1
variety with an auxiliary Grassmannian.

This also explains the connection with resultants and subresultants:
at j=r the sole coefficient is the usual resultant/norm, while smaller
j retain families of coordinates. Keeping just one principal
subresultant coefficient need not preserve an integral ideal.
For example p=u^2 and q=a*u+y give

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\a&y\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_1(M)=(a,y).
\]

The leading coefficient a and the resultant y^2 instead generate
(a,y^2), a strictly smaller ideal in Z[a,y]. They have the same
radical, so a field-valued vanishing test would miss the distinction.
This is a counterexample to that particular principal-coefficient
shortcut, not to subresultant methods that retain full determinantal data.

## 4. Application to the symplectic Springer image in every rank

Keep G=Sp_(2n)(C), its defining representation, P=P1 and the full
literal source. Work over R=Z[t_i^+-1], s_i=t_i+t_i^-1, or over the
universal spectral coefficient ring Z[s_1,...,s_n].
Write product(s-s_i)=E(s^2)+sO(s^2).
For n=2r+epsilon use

\[
 (p,q)=
 \begin{cases}
 (E,O),&n\text{ even},\\
 (O,E\bmod O),&n\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]

Let H=m_q on R[u]/p. The preceding theorem identifies I_j(H) with
the content ideal (6), generated by at most binomial(r,j) polynomials.
Let A_j denote this ideal, with A_0=R and A_j=0 outside 0<=j<=r.
For k=n-i>=1, the complete Fitting formula becomes

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)=
 2^k\sum_{a+b=k-\epsilon}A_a A_b.} \tag{7}
\]

Here C=K_T(Sp_(2n)/P1)/R[L-L^-1]. This is an exact all-rank
integral generator formula. The API computes only the initial-column
minors of 2H and their products; scaling is performed without dividing
by two. The result is not a global Smith form over R.

## 5. Complete closed formulas in rank five

Put e_j=e_j(s), F=product_(i<j)(s_i+s_j),
A=e_1e_4-e_5, B=e_1e_2-e_3 and J=(A,B). For n=5,

\[
 p=u^2+e_2u+e_4,\quad q=Bu+A,\qquad
 H=\begin{pmatrix}A&-Be_4\\B&A-Be_2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its entry ideal is J and its determinant is -F. As with the previous
rank-four block, F belongs to J^2. The doubled-block theorem and the
extra scalar two give

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Fitt}_0(C)&=(32F^2),\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_1(C)&=16FJ,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_2(C)&=8J^2,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_3(C)&=4J,\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_4(C)&=(2),\\
 \operatorname{Fitt}_i(C)&=R\quad(i\ge5).
 \end{aligned}} \tag{8}
\]

This is an immediate two-by-two corollary, rather than a separate
deep rank-five theorem.

## 6. The universal cubic block and complete ranks six and seven

For any monic cubic p=u^3+P*u^2+Q*u+R0 and q=a*u^2+b*u+c,
put J=(a,b,c) and

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K=(\;&c^2-Qac+R0ab,\quad bc-Pac+R0a^2,\\
     &b^2-Pab-ac+Qa^2\;).
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]

These three expressions are precisely the minors of the first two
columns of H=m_q mod p. Theorem (1) proves
I_1(H)=J and I_2(H)=K, including scheme structure and arithmetic primes.
Write f=det(H). Then I_3(H)=(f).
The minor ideals of H+H, in increasing size, are

\[
 R,\quad J,\quad J^2,\quad JK,\quad K^2,\quad fK,\quad(f^2). \tag{10}
\]

To check the only nontrivial simplifications, K is contained in J^2
because the entries of H are linear in a,b,c, and f belongs to JK
by Laplace expansion. Finally adj(adj(H))=fH for every three-by-three
matrix over a commutative ring. Each entry of adj(H) is a two-minor,
so fJ is contained in K^2. This proves (10) integrally.

For n=6 use

\[
 (P,Q,R0)=(e_2,e_4,e_6),\qquad(a,b,c)=(e_1,e_3,e_5).
\]

The actual O has the opposite overall sign, which does not change
minor ideals or cokernels. For n=7 use

\[
 (P,Q,R0)=(e_2,e_4,e_6),\qquad
 (a,b,c)=(e_1e_2-e_3,\ e_1e_4-e_5,\ e_1e_6-e_7).
\]

This is E reduced modulo the monic cubic O. In both ranks
(f)=(F) by the established even/odd resultant identity. We obtain

| Fitting index i | n=6 | n=7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | (64F^2) | (128F^2) |
| 1 | 32FK | 64FK |
| 2 | 16K^2 | 32K^2 |
| 3 | 8JK | 16JK |
| 4 | 4J^2 | 8J^2 |
| 5 | 2J | 4J |
| 6 | R | (2) |
| i>=7 | R | R |

The ideals J,K mean the explicit lists above, not unspecified
matrix minors. These are complete closed formulas in both ranks,
with no localization assumptions. They do not assert that the
cokernel is a direct sum of cyclic R-modules.

## 7. Independent checks, implementation and remaining scope

[The certificate script](tools/verify_symplectic_minors.py) constructs
generic monic quotients over independent integer polynomial variables.
It builds elementary exterior operators and Giambelli operators, then
checks (5) coefficient by coefficient for every minor in degrees 1-4.
Thus the computational certificates express full minors in the ideal
of initial-column minors over Z; they do not merely compare radicals,
generic ranks or rational Groebner bases.

The same script checks the cubic formulas and integral adjugate
identity, the rank-five resultant identity, and ordinary, singular and
finite-field specializations through degree six.
[Report](symplectic-minors-verification.json).
The existing original 2n projective inclusion remains the independent
reference for numerical API checks.

The API is documented in [the classical guide](CLASSICAL_EQUIVARIANT.md).
The original production program files are retained.

What is now resolved is a uniform integral Schur-coordinate generator
formula in every rank, plus explicit closed ideal lists through n=7.
What remains is a further simplification/classification of those
generators for larger blocks, their arithmetic primary structure and
closed ordinary Smith multiplicities. The first new block size is
four, occurring at n=8,9. Larger isotropic Grassmannians and B/D quadric
backends remain separate projects.

The literature distinction is substantive: integral Schur cyclicity,
monic factorization algebras and resultant theory are known. Formula (1)
is a direct consequence of those inputs, and (7)-(10) are their
application here. These arguments do not establish priority for the
specific image formulation or prove a new ambient Schubert theorem.
