# Degree-one generation for normalized trace Springer sources

For every representation supported by `SpringerCharacterMap`, the full
integral **torus** source is generated in degree one:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_\rho=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(\Lambda_\rho).}
\]

This includes every nontrivial highest-weight representation of a simply
connected simple complex group, and direct sums with faithful differential.
The Springer map is the normalized trace projection. The result includes
inhomogeneous polynomials. It does not assert that the Levi-invariant ring
is generated by its degree-one invariants.

The proof below is an elementary deduction from the trace formula and
saturation. It replaces the previous need to find a separate all-primes
leading-term basis for each representation. The old certificates remain
valid independent checks. No assertion of literature priority is made.
The [2026-10-20 proof and literature audit](TRACE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) checks
each step, its hypotheses, and the published inputs. No gap was found;
the exact integral statement was not located in the reviewed sources,
so its literature priority remains unresolved.

## The maps and the integral question

Let X=X(T), let r be its rank, and identify X with Z^r using fundamental
weights. Write S_Q=Sym_Q(X tensor Q) and Z[X] for the Laurent character
ring. If theta_rho:T -> h is the normalized Springer map, set

\[
\mathcal C_\rho=\{f\in S_{\mathbb Q}:\theta_\rho^*f\in\mathbb Z[X]\},
\qquad \Lambda_\rho=\mathcal C_\rho\cap(X\otimes\mathbb Q).
\]

For a standard Levi L, the polynomial-character construction in
[Kumar, Part I, Definition 4 and Theorem 5](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar76.pdf)
is the composite (theta_rho^*)^(-1), followed by the Borel map beta^P.
The present result identifies which polynomials in that composite come
from integral virtual characters. Restricting to rational coefficients
loses no integral characters: in each finite degree bound, the injective
pullback has a rational coefficient matrix, so an integral output in
its complex image has rational input coordinates.

Let m_mu be the weight multiplicities. In fundamental-weight coordinates,
the trace formula is

\[
M=\sum_\mu m_\mu\mu\mu^t,\qquad
\theta_\rho^*(\omega)=M^{-1}\sum_\mu m_\mu\mu e^\mu.
\]

M is invertible under the stated hypotheses. This normalized trace
construction is published in
[Kostant--Michor, Sections 2.1 and 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109066).
In particular d(theta_rho)_1=id, so the pullback is injective.
The weight-moment matrix formula above is also explicitly derived in
[Rogers, Theorem 1 and Section 3, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01538v1).
It is a published input, not a new formula of this note.

The rational linear coefficient matrix A has rank r. Clearing its
denominators gives a full sublattice of Lambda_rho, and any nonsingular
r-row submatrix of A bounds Lambda_rho inside a rational lattice.
Thus Lambda_rho is a full, finitely generated lattice.

Choose a Z-basis ell_1,...,ell_r of Lambda_rho, with coefficient columns
in a matrix B, and put b_i=theta_rho^*(ell_i). Let C be the integer
coefficient matrix of these Laurent polynomials, with rows indexed by
their nonzero support weights mu. Then

\[
C_{\mu,*}=m_\mu\mu^t K^t,\qquad K=B^tM^{-1}\in GL_r(\mathbb Q).
\tag{1}
\]

The zero weight contributes no trace moment. All support multiplicities
in (1) are nonzero. Thus a square coefficient minor is nonzero over Q
exactly when the corresponding weight exponents are independent over Q.

## Lemma 1: the coefficient columns are primitive

Let A be the rational coefficient matrix of theta_rho^* on linear
polynomials. By definition Lambda_rho={v in Q^r : Av in Z^N}, and C=AB.
Consequently

\[
C\mathbb Z^r=(C\mathbb Q^r)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
\]

Indeed, if Av is integral, v belongs to Lambda_rho and hence v=Bz with
z integral. This proves that C Z^r is saturated in Z^N. Its nonzero
Smith factors are therefore all one; equivalently its maximal minors
have gcd one. In particular C has column rank r modulo every prime p.

It is essential to use the **full** linear lattice, rather than a
convenient denominator-cleared sublattice.

## Lemma 2: independence in every characteristic

Fix any prime p and order the finite Laurent support by descending
lexicographic order on its integer exponents. Row-reduce C^t over F_p.
Since its rank is r, there is U in GL_r(F_p) such that the Laurent
polynomials U(b_1,...,b_r)^t have leading exponents mu_1,...,mu_r at
the r pivot columns.

Those pivot columns form a nonsingular minor modulo p. The corresponding
integer minor of C is therefore nonzero over Q. By (1),

\[
0\ne\det C_{\{\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r\},*}
=\left(\prod_jm_{\mu_j}\right)
  \det\big((\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r)^t\big)\det K.
\]

Hence the exponent vectors mu_j are linearly independent over Q.
The factorization is used only over Q; neither K nor M^(-1) is reduced
modulo p. The change U may depend on p and need not lift to a unimodular
integer matrix.
Distinct monomials in the transformed b_i have distinct leading Laurent
exponents: equality would give an integer linear relation among the mu_j.
Their leading coefficients are nonzero in F_p. The transformed b_i,
and therefore the original b_i, are algebraically independent over F_p.

The exponent determinant need not be invertible modulo p. For example,
t1^2 and t2^2 remain independent in characteristic two. Exponents are
integer vectors, not vectors reduced modulo p. This is why a Jacobian
test alone would be insufficient.

Only finite supports are used. Lexicographic order on Z^r is additive;
no well-order on the whole Laurent group is required.

## Proof of the theorem

Every f in C_rho has a unique expression f=F(ell_1,...,ell_r)/D, where
F has integer coefficients and D>0 is a least common denominator.
If a prime p divides D, not all coefficients of F are divisible by p.
But integrality of theta_rho^*f forces F(b_1,...,b_r)=0 modulo p,
contradicting Lemma 2. Therefore D=1. The reverse inclusion follows
because the b_i are integral Laurent polynomials.

The argument permits arbitrary F, including mixed degrees and constants.
Thus it proves the full polynomial intersection and, in particular,
that C_rho is graded.

## Levi characters, the Borel map and previous calculations

The degree-one lattice is Weyl-stable, and theta_rho^* is equivariant
and injective. Taking W_L invariants gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A_{\rho,L,\mathbb Z}
       =\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb Z}(\Lambda_\rho)^{W_L}.}
\]

These are the polynomials whose pullbacks are integral polynomial
characters of the actual Levi in the simply connected group. Integral
virtual characters, rather than only characters with positive
multiplicities, are intended. No division by the order of W_L is used.

Moreover Lambda_rho is contained in X. Differentiate the integral Laurent
pullback of a linear ell at 1; all its torus derivatives are integers,
and d(theta_rho)_1=id identifies these with the weight coefficients of ell.
Thus the integral weight-polynomial Borel map and the primitive Schubert
pullback from G/P to G/B give

\[
\beta^P(\mathcal A_{\rho,L,\mathbb Z})\subset H^{2*}(G/P;\mathbb Z).
\]

The theorem proves degree-one generation before taking invariants.
For example, when L=G=SL2 the invariant source contains a quadratic
generator and has no nonzero linear invariant. This is compatible with
the torus theorem. Computing the full Levi invariant lattice and its
Borel image still requires further work.

The earlier G2/F4 and E6/E7 lattice identifications and all finite image
tables remain valid. Their special all-primes leading certificates are
additional verifications. The general theorem now proves that the
linear lattice suffices for every representation under these hypotheses.
The finite formula for that lattice remains

\[
\Lambda_\rho=M D_\rho^\#,
\qquad D_\rho=\sum_\mu\mathbb Z(m_\mu\mu).
\]

For one nontrivial irreducible representation the subsequent
[closed lattice classification](IRREDUCIBLE_CHARACTER_LATTICES.md) proves
D_lambda=Q+Z*lambda and computes Lambda_lambda from its highest weight
and Dynkin index. The weighted formula above remains necessary for
arbitrary direct sums; the irreducible shortcut cannot be used there.

## API and independent checks

Version 0.15 adds the trace-specific certificate:

```python
from sage_borel import springer_character_map

s = springer_character_map("G2", (2, 0))  # dimension 27
certificate = s.certify_trace_source()
assert certificate.certified
assert certificate.primitive_coefficients
assert certificate.trace_factorization
```

`IntegralCharacterSource(s, algebra)` now uses this theorem by default.
An explicit `basis_change` still requests the earlier leading-term test.
The supplied rational algebra must have independent invariant inputs;
the theorem does not infer that a chosen list generates all Levi invariants.
`certify_polynomial_source()` retains its old behavior for the generic
leading test. A false result there can coexist with a true trace certificate.

The [survey](experiments/trace_source_survey.py) checks 59 representation
cases, including direct sums, higher exceptional representations and E8.
It saves exact weights, trace and linear lattices, primitive-column minor
certificates, and adapted leading bases at six primes per case. Eighteen
bounded searches also test actual polynomial expansions through degree three.
The [dataset](experiments/trace-source-survey-results.json) preserves all data.

The [Python-only audit](experiments/audit_trace_source_survey.py) uses no Sage
or runtime-library imports. It reconstructs the weight sets and
multiplicities from the Cartan data by weight strings and Freudenthal's
recurrence, checks Weyl dimensions, rebuilds trace/Laurent coefficients,
verifies saturation by small maximal minors, and checks adapted leading
exponents using a different coordinate order. It also repeats the bounded
Laurent expansion searches. The weight recurrence is a standard shared
mathematical formula; its implementation and arithmetic are separate from
Sage's character implementation. Its [saved result](experiments/trace-source-survey-audit.json)
binds the exact files checked.

## Published inputs and literature limits

Kostant--Michor supply the trace projection; Kumar supplies the
polynomial-character subring and its transported Borel map. Those
constructions are published results, not consequences of this note.

The trace moments are logarithmic derivatives of the representation's
character. Related characteristic-zero geometry is studied in
[Fassarella, Logarithmic Hesse's Problem, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.1659).
That theorem concerns complex Laurent polynomials and their logarithmic
polar maps; it is not cited here as the integral all-primes statement.
The proof above supplies the precise saturation and reduction steps used
in this repository. Focused searches do not establish literature priority,
and no claim that this formulation is new or that its question was posed
in the cited papers is made.
