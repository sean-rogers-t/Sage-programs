# The largest type-B Smith factor: a closed formula in every rank

This note determines the multiplicity of the largest possible normalized
factor `2^min(a,k)`. It also proves an integral statement about a filtered
symplectic coefficient lattice. It does not assume that every intermediate
filtered lattice is primitive. The proof uses classical Schubert cells,
incidence resolutions, quaternionic Grassmann bundles and the Schur Pieri
rule; no novelty claim is made.

## Formula

Use the normalized generators and degree conventions of
[TYPE_B_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_SMITH.md). Set

$$
\ell=\min(a,k),\qquad A=\max(a,k),\qquad n=A+\ell,
\qquad T=\left\lfloor\frac{A+\ell-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
$$

By integral block duality we may put the head block in size `ell` and the
tail block in size `A`. Write `d=2m` for polynomial degree, or `4m` for
cohomological degree. The selected degree lattice has rank equal to the
number of partitions `lambda` of `m` in the `ell`-by-`A` rectangle.

The multiplicity `M_ell(m)` of the Smith factor `2^ell` is

$$
\boxed{M_\ell(m)=
\#\{\lambda\subseteq A^\ell:
          |\lambda|=m,\ \lambda_\ell>T\}.}\tag{1}
$$

Zero parts are included when `lambda` has fewer than `ell` rows. Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{\sum_{m\ge0}M_\ell(m)q^m
=q^{\ell(T+1)}
 {A-T-1+\ell\brack\ell}_q.}\tag{2}
$$

Since $T+1=\lceil n/2\rceil$, the same polynomial is
$q^{\ell\lceil n/2\rceil}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor\brack\ell}_q$.

The Gaussian coefficient counts partitions in the
`ell`-by-`(A-T-1)` rectangle. Because `A>=ell`, that width is nonnegative.
The largest factor first occurs in polynomial degree `2ell(T+1)` and
always occurs once at the selected top degree `2Aell`.

For example, when `(A,ell)=(5,3)`, we have `T=3` and the multiplicity of
the factor `8` is one in each of `m=12,13,14,15`, and zero elsewhere.
When `(A,ell)=(4,4)`, the factor `16` occurs only at `m=16`.

For the defining/vector Springer integral form, every degree-`d` lattice
is multiplied by `2^d`; the corresponding largest factor is therefore
`2^(d+ell)`, with the same multiplicity. See
[INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md](INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md) for that separate
normalization calculation.

## 1. A stronger integral coefficient statement

Work first in

$$
X_C=\operatorname{IG}(\ell,2n).
$$

Use the head Schur inputs

$$
f_\lambda=s_\lambda(y_1^2,\ldots,y_\ell^2),
\qquad \lambda\subseteq A^\ell,\quad |\lambda|=m,
$$

where the `y_i` are the Chern roots of the dual tautological bundle.
These are an integral source basis for the selected tail image: the total
squared-root relation interchanges head and tail elementary/complete
series, sending the tail Schur basis to the conjugate head Schur basis
up to degree signs. This is a unimodular source change.

Let `C` be their integral symplectic Schubert coefficient matrix. Its
columns are primitive, so its row lattice is the full source coordinate
lattice `Z^r`. This is proved through the quaternionic Grassmann
projection in [TYPE_B_SMITH.md, Section 1](TYPE_B_SMITH.md#1-a-primitive-symplectic-comparison-lattice).
For a minimal right signed-permutation representative `w`, put
`nu(w)=#{negative entries of w}`. Let `P_(ell-1)` be the row lattice
generated by the rows of `C` with `nu(w)<=ell-1`.

The integral result needed below is

$$
\boxed{P_{\ell-1}
=\bigoplus_{\substack{\lambda\subseteq A^\ell,\ |\lambda|=m\\
                       \lambda_\ell\le T}}
                   \mathbb Z\,e_\lambda.}\tag{3}
$$

Here `e_lambda` is the coordinate functional in the specified head Schur
source basis. In particular, this filtered row lattice is primitive, not
merely of full rank modulo two in its rational span.

## 2. The Schubert union selected by `nu <= ell-1`

Fix a compatible Hermitian metric and quaternionic structure `J` on
`C^(2n)`. Choose a complex Lagrangian `L_0` and a symplectic coordinate
basis whose first half spans `L_0`. Take the Borel subgroup preserving the
associated full isotropic flag. Define

$$
Z=\{\Sigma\in X_C:\dim_\mathbb C(\Sigma\cap L_0)\ge1\}.
$$

This closed incidence locus is exactly the union of the Schubert cells
with `nu(w)<=ell-1`. To check the convention explicitly, the coordinate
isotropic plane at the cell `w` is spanned by the `ell` signed coordinate
vectors selected by its head block. The positive ones lie in `L_0` and
the negative ones in `J L_0`. Minimality on the tail Weyl subgroup makes
every tail entry positive. Hence at this coordinate plane, and therefore
throughout its Borel orbit,

$$
\dim(\Sigma\cap L_0)=\ell-\nu(w).
\tag{4}
$$

The same fact follows from the pivot columns of its row echelon matrix:
precisely the positive pivots give the subspace in `L_0`. In particular
the identity cell is contained in `L_0` and has `nu=0`; this specifies the
cell orientation. The signed-permutation/Schubert conventions and their
partition form are given in
[Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, Section 6.1, PDF pp.35--36](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/isogiamfinal.pdf).

Restriction from `X_C` to `Z` records exactly the coefficient rows in
(3). Indeed, the Schubert cell closures in `Z` give an integral homology
basis, and the ambient Schubert cohomology basis is dual to these cycles.

## 3. The incidence resolution preserves the row lattice integrally

Consider the smooth projective incidence variety

$$
Y=\{(L,\Sigma): L\subseteq\Sigma\cap L_0,\ \dim_\mathbb C L=1\}
\xrightarrow{\ f\ } Z.
$$

Its projection to `P(L_0)=CP^(n-1)` has fiber
`IG(ell-1,2n-2)`. Over a point `Sigma` the resolution fiber is
`P(Sigma cap L_0)`.

The pullback `f^*:H^*(Z;Z)->H^*(Y;Z)` is a primitive injection. Here is
an integral proof, which avoids assuming this from birationality. On
each Schubert cell of `Z`, choose the first positive pivot vector in
its canonical row echelon representative. Its span is a regular choice
of line in `Sigma cap L_0`, and so gives a section of `f` over that
cell. The closure of this section in `Y` maps with degree one onto the
corresponding Schubert cell closure. Consequently `f_*` is surjective
on integral homology, since those closures form a homology basis of
`Z`. The even-cell decompositions give free integral homology, and the
universal coefficient theorem identifies `f^*` with the dual of this
surjection. It is therefore split injective degreewise. The homology of
`Y` is free in even degrees as well, by its isotropic Grassmann bundle
over complex projective space.

A primitive injection of target lattices does not change the row lattice
of a source-to-target matrix: an integral left inverse gives the reverse
row containment. Thus the coefficient row lattice of the source classes
on `Y` is precisely `P_(ell-1)`.

## 4. A quaternionic Grassmann bundle gives ordinary Pieri rows

Over `CP^(n-1)`, let `H(L)=L direct-sum J L` be the quaternionic line
generated by the tautological complex line, and let `E=H(L)^perp`, of
quaternionic rank `n-1`. Put

$$
Q=\operatorname{HGr}(\ell-1,E).
$$

There is a continuous fiber-bundle map `Y -> Q`: remove `L` from
`Sigma` orthogonally and take the quaternionic span of the residual
isotropic `(ell-1)`-plane. Its fiber is the complex Lagrangian
Grassmannian `LG(ell-1,2ell-2)` in that quaternionic span.

The quaternionic Grassmann bundle `Q` is simply connected, and base and
fiber have free integral cohomology in even degrees only. The integral
Serre spectral sequences, or Leray--Hirsch, show that
`H^*(Q;Z)->H^*(Y;Z)` is a primitive injection. Thus computing the source
row lattice on `Q` gives exactly the same lattice as on `Y` and `Z`.

Write `x=c_1(L^*)`. If `U` is the quaternionic tautological bundle on
`Q`, its Borel Schur classes give the integral Leray--Hirsch basis

$$
x^j s_\mu(U),\qquad
0\le j\le n-1,\qquad \mu\subseteq A^{\ell-1}.
\tag{5}
$$

The quaternionic Grassmann Schur basis and its integral presentation are
recorded in
[Panin--Walter, Theorem 11.2, equations (11.7)--(11.8), PDF pp.33--34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.0649).
Apply the usual bundle version of Leray--Hirsch using these globally
defined characteristic classes. The case `ell=1` means `Q=CP^(n-1)`
and `mu` is the empty partition.

The quaternionic span of `Sigma` splits on `Y` as `H(L) direct-sum U`.
The first Borel class of `H(L)` is `x^2`. The Schur addition rule gives

$$
f_\lambda\longmapsto
\sum_{\substack{\mu\subseteq\lambda\\
                 \lambda/\mu\text{ horizontal strip}}}
x^{2r}s_\mu(U),\qquad r=|\lambda|-|\mu|.
\tag{6}
$$

Every `mu` in this formula already lies in the rectangle in (5), so no
Grassmann bundle relation is needed to reduce a fiber Schur class.
The base relation `x^n=0` removes precisely the terms with `r>T`.
It follows that the row lattice is spanned by the ordinary Schur
coefficient vectors of

$$
h_r s_\mu,\qquad 0\le r\le T,\quad
\mu\subseteq A^{\ell-1},\quad r+|\mu|=m,
\tag{7}
$$

restricted to the source rectangle `A^ell`.

## 5. Integral triangularity of the Pieri rows

Every partition `lambda` occurring in (7) satisfies `lambda_ell<=r`:
the entire bottom row must be supplied by the horizontal strip, which
has only `r` boxes. Thus the row span is contained in the right side of
(3).

Conversely take a rectangle partition `lambda` with bottom part `r<=T`
and set `mu=(lambda_1,...,lambda_(ell-1))`. The Pieri row `h_r s_mu`
contains `s_lambda` with coefficient one. Any other partition `eta` in
that row has `eta_ell<r`: if its bottom row used all `r` boxes, the
remaining rows would have to be exactly `mu`, giving `eta=lambda`.
Induction on `r`, starting with `r=0`, subtracts the already obtained
coordinate rows with smaller bottom part. It produces `e_lambda`
integrally. This proves (3).

## 6. From the filtered symplectic lattice to the largest type-B factor

For the same coordinate inputs the type-B and type-C matrices satisfy

$$
B_w=2^{\nu(w)}C_w,
$$

as proved in [TYPE_B_SMITH.md, Section 2](TYPE_B_SMITH.md#2-the-exact-bc-row-rescaling).
Let `R` be the type-B row lattice in `Z^r`, and set
`R_0=P_(ell-1)`. The full type-C row lattice is `Z^r`, so

$$
2^\ell\mathbb Z^r\subseteq R,\qquad
2^{\ell-1}R_0\subseteq R\cap R_0,
\qquad R\subseteq R_0+2^\ell\mathbb Z^r.
$$

By (3), choose the coordinate complement
`Z^r=R_0 direct-sum R_1`. The preceding containments give

$$
R=(R\cap R_0)\ \oplus\ 2^\ell R_1.
$$

All factors on `R_0` divide `2^(ell-1)`, while the complementary factors
are exactly `2^ell`. Their number is the rank of `R_1`, namely the count
in (1). This argument proves the largest-factor multiplicity without
requiring any unproved statement about the other `nu` prefixes.

Finally subtract `T+1` from each of the `ell` parts counted in (1).
The resulting partitions fill the `ell`-by-`(A-T-1)` rectangle and lose
weight `ell(T+1)`. This proves (2).
