# Smith factors for normalized type-B tail images

**Status: audited repository deductions (2026-10-19).** The cited
Schubert and quaternionic facts are published inputs; the selected-lattice
consequences are derived here. The
[proof/source audit](PROOF_STATUS.md) gives dependencies and limitations.
No originality, priority, or independent external certification is claimed.

This note gives proofs for the natural normalized tail generators. It proves
the possible primes and an exponent bound, a duality in the two block sizes,
stable saturation, a complete answer when one block has size one, and an
exact finite determinantal formula. It does **not** prove a closed partition
statistic for arbitrary block sizes. Subsequent notes give complete closed
formulas when one block has size at most three, and closed formulas for
both unit factors and the largest possible factor in every rank.
The [all-rank partition candidate](TYPE_B_CLOSED_CONJECTURE.md) gives a
single conjectural formula for the remaining intermediate multiplicities.

The arguments assemble classical characteristic-map, quaternionic and
Grassmannian facts. No novelty claim is made. The character-lattice
normalizations are discussed in [INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md](INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md).

## Setup and results

Let `a,k >= 1`, `n=a+k`, and put

$$
X_B=\operatorname{OG}(a,2n+1),\qquad
q_i=e_i(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2),\quad 1\le i\le k.
$$

Each `q_i` has polynomial degree `2i`, or cohomological degree `4i`. We use
the Borel normalization `beta(omega_i)=sigma_(s_i)`, minimal **right**
parabolic coset representatives, and rightmost-first divided differences.
Let `L_B(a,k;d)` be the degree-`2d` integral cohomology lattice generated by
degree-`d` polynomials over Z in the images of the `q_i`.

All such lattices are zero in odd polynomial degree. Write `d=2m`, and let

$$
r_{a,k}(m)=\#\{\lambda:\lambda_1\le a,\ \ell(\lambda)\le k,\ |\lambda|=m\}.
$$

The following statements hold for the normalized inputs:

1. The image has rank `r_(a,k)(m)`, with Z-basis given by the images of
   `s_lambda(x_(a+1)^2,...,x_n^2)` for these rectangle partitions.
2. Every nonzero Smith factor is `2^e`, where `0 <= e <= min(a,k)`.
3. The Smith multiset is unchanged when `a` and `k` are interchanged.
4. The stable image, with `k` fixed and `a` tending to infinity degreewise,
   is saturated. In particular, all finite factors are one when `d <= a`.
   By block duality, `d <= max(a,k)` is another sufficient finite condition.
5. If `a=1`, the only nonzero factor in polynomial degree `2m`, `0<=m<=k`, is

   $$
   s_m=\begin{cases}1&2m\le k,\\2&k<2m\le2k.\end{cases}
   $$

   The case `k=1` follows by interchanging the blocks.
6. If `min(a,k)=2`, every multiplicity is given by a two-row partition
   statistic in [TYPE_B_TWO_ROW_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_TWO_ROW_SMITH.md).
   If `min(a,k)=3`, the complete multiplicities are the closed partition
   counts in [TYPE_B_THREE_ROW_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_THREE_ROW_SMITH.md).
7. For arbitrary `l=min(a,k)`, `A=max(a,k)`, and
   `T=floor((A+l-1)/2)`, the multiplicity of the largest factor `2^l`
   is the number of size-`m` partitions in `A^l` whose last part exceeds
   `T`. The [general proof](TYPE_B_HIGHEST_SMITH.md) uses an integral
   incidence resolution and Pieri straightening.
8. The [unit-factor theorem](TYPE_B_UNIT_SMITH.md) gives, in every rank,
   the positive closed polynomial

   $$
   \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}
   q^{j(c-l+j)}{r\brack j}_q{c-j\brack l-2j}_q,
   \qquad r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\quad c=\lceil n/2\rceil.
   $$

   Its coefficient of $q^m$ is the number of unit factors. The proof
   also establishes primitivity of the zero-negative-entry prefix.
9. For $l=4$, all prefixes are primitive, and the factor-eight
   multiplicity polynomial is $q^{3c}{r\brack3}_q[c-3]$, where
   $[v]=1+q+\cdots+q^{v-1}$. The
   [four-row appendix](TYPE_B_FILTRATION.md) proves these additional
   partial results. The factor-two/factor-four split remains open here.

The index always means the index in the saturation of the rational image.
The selected image usually has smaller rank than the full cohomology group.

## 1. A primitive symplectic comparison lattice

Use the same coordinate polynomial inputs on
`X_C=IG(a,2n)`. The compact homogeneous projection

$$
X_C=Sp(n)/(U(a)\times Sp(k))
\longrightarrow Sp(n)/(Sp(a)\times Sp(k))=\operatorname{HGr}(k,n)
$$

has fiber `LG(a,2a)`. The fibration is stated explicitly by
[Pragacz--Ratajski, Appendix A, §10.5, pp.81--82](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1000182205320).
The base is simply connected; base and fiber have free integral cohomology
only in even degrees. Thus the integral Serre spectral sequence collapses,
and the image of the base pullback is primitive: its cokernel has a filtration
with free successive quotients.

The pullbacks of the symplectic characteristic classes of the quaternionic
bundle are the `q_i`, up to harmless sign conventions. The integral quaternionic
Grassmannian presentation is

$$
\mathbb Z[q_1,\ldots,q_k]/(h_{a+1},\ldots,h_n),
$$

with Schur basis indexed by the `k`-by-`a` rectangle. See
[Panin--Walter, Theorem 11.2, equations (11.7)--(11.8), pp.33--34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.0649)
and the integral topological discussion in Pragacz--Ratajski, Appendix A,
§10.1--10.3. Therefore the symplectic image lattice `L_C(a,k;d)` is primitive.

In degree `2m`, form its matrix `C` with columns indexed by those Schur
polynomials and rows by all length-`2m` minimal right parabolic representatives.
The columns form a Z-basis of the selected image. Consequently `C` is injective
as a map from `Z^r`, its column lattice is primitive, and every nonzero Smith
factor of `C` is one.

## 2. The exact B/C row rescaling

The Weyl groups of B and C are the same signed permutation group, with the
same Coxeter generators and parabolic representatives. In standard
orthonormal coordinates,

$$
\alpha_i=x_i-x_{i+1}\ (i<n),\qquad
\alpha_n^B=x_n,\qquad\alpha_n^C=2x_n.
$$

Their reflection substitutions agree. Their divided differences therefore obey

$$
\partial_i^B=\partial_i^C\ (i<n),\qquad
\partial_n^B=2\partial_n^C.
$$

For a signed permutation `w`, let `nu(w)` be its number of negative entries.
It equals the number of occurrences of the end generator `s_n` in any
reduced word: starting at the identity, each length-increasing end reflection
changes a positive entry to a negative one, while the other generators only
permute entries. Equivalently this count is unchanged by the braid relations.
Thus for every homogeneous coordinate polynomial `f` of degree `length(w)`,

$$
(\partial_w^B f)(0)=2^{\nu(w)}(\partial_w^C f)(0).
$$

If `B` denotes the Schur-column matrix for the type-B image, then exactly

$$
B=D C,\qquad D_{w,w}=2^{\nu(w)}.\tag{1}
$$

This agrees with the classical partition version of the comparison:
the Chern-class-preserving rational ring isomorphism sends the symplectic
Schubert class `sigma_mu` to `2^(ell_k(mu))*tau_mu`. See
[Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, §2.1, p.8 and equation (12)](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/isogiamfinal.pdf).
The signed-permutation description above fixes the convention independently
of partition-to-Weyl indexing choices.

Since the row diagonal is injective over Z, the integral polynomial kernels
in B and C coincide. Hence the rectangle Schur classes really give an
integral basis of the B image as well, not just a rational basis. This proves
the rank statement and justifies replacing all monomial columns by `B`.

## 3. Only the prime two, and a bound on each exponent

For an odd prime `p`, reduction of `D` is invertible. Primitivity of `C`
means its reduction has full column rank modulo every prime. Equation (1)
therefore shows that `B` has full column rank modulo every odd prime. None
of its nonzero Smith factors is divisible by an odd prime.

For a minimal right representative for the parabolic with retained Levi
`S_a x W_(B_k)`, the last block is positive. Thus `nu(w)<=a`, so all diagonal
weights in `D` divide `2^a`. Write `V` for the rational column span of `C`.
If `x` belongs to the saturation of `B*Z^r`, then `D^-1*x` lies in `V`, and
`2^a*D^-1*x` is an integral vector. Primitivity of `C` gives

$$
2^aD^{-1}x\in V\cap\mathbb Z^h=C\mathbb Z^r.
$$

Multiplying by `D` proves

$$
2^a\operatorname{sat}(L_B)\subset L_B.\tag{2}
$$

Therefore every Smith exponent is at most `a`. The next symmetry improves
the bound to `min(a,k)`.

## 4. Block duality, including integral lattices

First, computing the Smith factors in the partial flag or after pullback to
the full flag gives the same answer. For any complex flag projection
`G/B -> G/P`, the base and fiber have free even Schubert cohomology and the
base is simply connected. The same integral spectral-sequence argument shows
that its pullback is a primitive embedding in each degree. Composing a
lattice embedding with a primitive embedding leaves its nonzero Smith factors
unchanged.

Now work in integral full-flag cohomology. The Weyl group acts by integral
ring automorphisms, induced on the compact model `K/T` by right multiplication
by representatives in the torus normalizer. It permutes the coordinate
weights equivariantly with the characteristic map. A coordinate permutation
therefore carries the tail alphabet of size `k` to a head alphabet of size
`k`, preserving its image-lattice Smith factors.

For the resulting head and complementary tail alphabets, all the global
positive-degree invariants vanish integrally. Their rational vanishing
implies integral vanishing because flag cohomology is torsion free. Hence

$$
E_{\rm head^2}(t)E_{\rm tail^2}(t)=1.
$$

Inverting either series expresses every elementary generator of one block
as an integral complete-symmetric polynomial in those of the other. Their
generated integral subrings in full-flag cohomology coincide. The complement
now has size `a`, and lies in the parabolic family with head size `k`.
Pulling back from that partial flag is again primitive. This proves

$$
\operatorname{Smith}(a,k;d)=\operatorname{Smith}(k,a;d).\tag{3}
$$

Applying (2) after the interchange proves that every nonzero Smith factor
divides `2^min(a,k)`. No averaging with a denominator is used in this argument.

## 5. Stable saturation at every prime

The stable odd-orthogonal ring has special Schubert generators `tau_j` and
relations

$$
\tau_s^2+\sum_{i=1}^s(-1)^i\tau_{s+i}c_{s-i}=0\quad(s>k),\qquad
c_j=\begin{cases}\tau_j&j\le k,\\2\tau_j&j>k.\end{cases}
$$

Here `c_0=1`. This integral presentation and the generator image formula are
given after [Kumar--Rogers, Definition 28 and in Theorem 29, equations (24)--(25)](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar81.pdf).
Let `A=Z[q_1,...,q_k]` be the selected normalized source and let `xi` be
its stable map.

Define an integral ring homomorphism to the symmetric polynomial ring in
`k` independent variables by

$$
\rho(\tau_j)=\begin{cases}e_j(y_1,\ldots,y_k)&j\le k,\\0&j>k.\end{cases}
$$

Every stable relation vanishes, since all its leading special-class indices
are greater than `k`. The Springer-generator formula and the identity
`E_y(t)E_y(-t)=E_(-y^2)(t^2)` give

$$
(\rho\circ\xi)(q_i)=
e_i(y)^2+2\sum_{j=1}^i(-1)^j e_{i+j}(y)e_{i-j}(y)
=e_i(y_1^2,\ldots,y_k^2).
$$

This composite is injective and has saturated image. Indeed, its image is
the signed-permutation invariant subring

$$
\mathbb Z[y_1^2,\ldots,y_k^2]^{S_k}
\subset\mathbb Z[y_1,\ldots,y_k]^{S_k}.
$$

If a nonzero integer multiple of an integral symmetric polynomial is also
invariant under coordinate sign changes, torsion freeness forces the
polynomial itself to have that invariance. The elementary symmetric
description of the invariant ring is valid over Z.

To conclude, suppose `N*h=xi(f)` in stable integral cohomology. Applying
`rho` and using saturation of the composite gives
`rho(h)=(rho*xi)(g)` for some integral `g` in `A`. Injectivity of the composite
then implies `f=N*g`. Stable cohomology is torsion free, so
`N*(h-xi(g))=0` implies `h=xi(g)`. This proves stable saturation at every
prime, not merely stable injectivity.

In polynomial degree `d`, stable Schubert classes have partition weight `d`.
If `a>=d`, all such partitions fit in the finite rectangle, and stable-to-finite
specialization is an integral isomorphism in that degree. It respects the
selected generators. All finite factors in that range are therefore one.
Equation (3) gives the symmetric sufficient range `max(a,k)>=d`.

## 6. A complete closed formula for the one-block cases

For `a=1`, the flag variety is the odd-dimensional quadric

$$
X_B=Q^{2k+1}\subset\mathbb P^{2k+2}.
$$

Let `h` be its primitive hyperplane class. Inverting the total squared
elementary series gives `beta(q_i)=(-1)^i*h^(2i)`. Thus the selected image
has one basis vector in degree `2m`, represented by `h^(2m)`, for
`0<=m<=k`, and is zero in other positive degrees.

The integral quadric Schubert basis has `h^j` primitive for `0<=j<=k`
and `h^j=2*alpha_j` with `alpha_j` primitive for `k<j<=2k+1`.
These are also the `a=1` specializations of the integral Pieri/presentation
formulas in [Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, §2, Theorems 2.1--2.2](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/qprig3.pdf).
This gives the piecewise formula stated at the beginning. Block duality gives
the answer for every `k=1` family: replace the other block size by `a`.

## 7. An exact formula for every finite Smith factor

Retain the integral symplectic rectangle Schur matrix `C` from Section 1.
For `1<=j<=r`, let `I` range over sets of `j` Schubert rows and `J` over
sets of `j` rectangle Schur columns, with nonzero determinant. Put

$$
\delta_0=0,\qquad
\delta_j=\min_{I,J}
\left\{\sum_{w\in I}\nu(w)+v_2\!\left(\det C_{I,J}\right)\right\}.
\tag{4}
$$

Then the nonzero Smith factors, in divisibility order, are exactly

$$
\boxed{s_j=2^{\delta_j-\delta_{j-1}}\quad(1\le j\le r).}\tag{5}
$$

**Proof.** The determinant of the corresponding minor of `B=D*C` is
`2^(sum_(w in I) nu(w))*det(C_(I,J))`. The gcd of the `j`-by-`j` minors of
an integer matrix is the product of its first `j` nonzero Smith factors.
Section 3 proves that this gcd has no odd prime divisors. Taking its
two-adic valuation gives (4), and successive quotients give (5).

The entries of `C` have the exact formula

$$
C_{w,\lambda}=
\left(\partial_w^C s_\lambda(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2)\right)(0).
$$

They can alternatively be obtained in the known integral theta basis. Thus
(4)--(5) determine every rank and factor without a guessed degree bound or
a conjectural fit to the examples. They still require minors or an equivalent
two-adic matrix calculation; they are not a closed partition-statistic
classification of the exponents.

## 8. The unit factors have a Grassmannian Frobenius formula

There is a smaller exact description of the first layer. Let

$$
R=H^{2*}(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n);\mathbb F_2)
$$

with ordinary polynomial degree, so the Schur basis of `R_m` is indexed by
the same rectangle partitions of `m`. The number of type-B Smith factors
equal to one in degree `d=2m` is

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}
\bigl(R_m\longrightarrow R_{2m},\ f\longmapsto f^2\bigr).}\tag{6}
$$

**Proof.** In (1), reduction modulo two removes every row with a negative
signed-permutation entry. The remaining representatives are precisely the
ordinary type-A Grassmannian representatives; their divided differences use
only the roots `x_i-x_(i+1)`. On those rows the map sends the ordinary Schur
polynomial `s_lambda(x_tail)` to `s_lambda(x_tail^2)`, which modulo two is
its square. The source rectangle Schur classes give the integral basis used
for `B`. Consequently the rank of `B` modulo two is the rank in (6), and
the former counts exactly its odd, hence unit, Smith factors.

This is often convenient for deciding saturation or counting the factors
greater than one. It does not by itself distinguish factors two from four
or higher powers.

## Normalization and scope

For the defining/vector Springer polynomial-character intersection discussed
in [INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md](INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md), every degree-`d` lattice
is scaled by `2^d`. Its nonzero Smith factors are therefore `2^d*s_j`;
the exponents in (5) are shifted by `d`. The normalization is part of the
statement and cannot be changed silently.

The finite grid in
[orthogonal_smith_search_results.json](experiments/orthogonal_smith_search_results.json)
supports the results above but is not their proof. It also shows why stable
injectivity alone is insufficient for finite saturation: for `a=k=2`, the
normalized factors in polynomial degrees `0,2,4,6,8` are respectively
`(1)`, `(1)`, `(1,2)`, `(2)`, `(4)`.

The independent [verification script](experiments/verify_type_b_frobenius.py)
checks (6) with Schur Littlewood--Richardson multiplication over `GF(2)`,
without importing the Borel engine. It also checks the rectangle ranks,
exponent bounds, block symmetry and sufficient stable range against the
recorded grid. Its [verification report](experiments/type_b_frobenius_verification.json)
binds the input dataset by SHA-256. Reproduce it from `borel/` with
`sage -python experiments/verify_type_b_frobenius.py`.

The [Schur-product filtration](TYPE_B_FILTRATION.md) explains the subsequent
closed formulas and reduces the general problem to explicitly bounded
ordinary Schur-product spaces. The
[closed partition candidate](TYPE_B_CLOSED_CONJECTURE.md) now proposes a
rule for every factor. Its unit and highest layers are proved in every
rank. The remaining issue is proving its intermediate layers when
`min(a,k)>=4`, including the required integral prefix comparisons.
