# Every unit Smith factor in type B

**Audited deduction (2026-10-19).** The argument below is a repository
derivation from stated published inputs, checked again during the proof audit.
The exact Smith claim is not attributed here to a published theorem, has
not received independent external mathematical review, and establishes no
novelty or priority claim. The counting polynomial itself is classical
Birkhoff/Hall-type enumeration; its identification with this normalized
Springer-image problem is the deduction made here. See
[PROOF_STATUS.md](PROOF_STATUS.md) for the distinction between published
results, audited deductions, finite computations, and conjectures.

This note derives a closed formula, in every rank and degree, for the number
of Smith factors equal to **1** in the normalized type-B Springer image. It
also derives that the zero-negative-entry symplectic comparison matrix has
a primitive integral image. The complete formula for all powers of 2 is now established separately
in [TYPE_B_GENERAL_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_GENERAL_SMITH.md); this note retains
its independent unit-layer argument.

The argument identifies that matrix with restriction to the variety of invariant
subspaces of a square-zero operator of largest possible rank. An embedding of
this variety in an affine Grassmannian supplies the integral Chern-generation
statement needed for the proof. The variety here parametrizes subspaces
preserved by the operator; it is not the partial-flag variety defined by the
stronger condition that the operator annihilates successive quotients.

## 1. Normalization and statement

Use the notation of [TYPE_B_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_SMITH.md). For
\(a+k=n\), the selected normalized generators on
\(\operatorname{OG}(a,2n+1)\) are

\[
q_i=e_i(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2).
\]

Their polynomial degree is \(2i\). The degree-\(2m\) image lies in
cohomological degree \(4m\); odd polynomial degrees have zero image. Integral
block duality permits us to put

\[
\ell=\min(a,k),\qquad A=\max(a,k),\qquad n=A+\ell,
\qquad r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\quad c=\lceil n/2\rceil.
\]

After an integral change of source basis, the degree-\(2m\) source basis is
\(s_\lambda(y_1^2,\ldots,y_\ell^2)\), for
\(\lambda\subset A^\ell\) and \(|\lambda|=m\). In particular, the source
rank is \([q^m]{n\brack\ell}_q\). Gaussian binomials are zero outside their
usual range.

**Deduction 1 (unit factors; proved from the cited inputs).**
Let \(u_{n,\ell}(m)\) be the number of
nonzero Smith factors equal to 1 in this degree-\(2m\) normalized type-B image.
Then

\[
\boxed{\quad
\sum_{m\ge0}u_{n,\ell}(m)q^m
=U_{n,\ell}(q)
=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor}
q^{j(c-\ell+j)}
{r\brack j}_q
{c-j\brack\ell-2j}_q.
\quad} \tag{1}
\]

Thus this part of the Smith problem has a positive finite partition formula,
subject to the normalization and B/C comparison stated below.
The exponent of \(q\) records half the ordinary cohomological degree on the
auxiliary variety used below; it records one quarter of cohomological degree in
the original orthogonal Grassmannian image.

The deduction concerns these normalized generators. For the literal defining-
representation integral form discussed in [INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md](INTEGRAL_LITERATURE.md),
every degree-\(d\) image is multiplied by \(2^d\). In degree \(d=2m\), the
factors counted by (1) consequently become factors \(2^{2m}\), rather than
remaining unit factors. Changing the integral form changes the arithmetic.

## 2. The zero-negative-entry comparison matrix

Let \(C\) be the symplectic coefficient matrix on the head Schur basis just
specified. If \(\nu(w)\) counts negative entries of the signed Schubert index,
the corresponding odd-orthogonal matrix has rows

\[
B_w=2^{\nu(w)}C_w.
\]

Consequently only rows with \(\nu(w)=0\) survive modulo 2, and elementary Smith
theory gives

\[
u_{n,\ell}(m)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}
\bigl(C_{\nu=0}\bmod2\bigr). \tag{2}
\]

Write \(P_0(m)\) for the integral row lattice of this submatrix in the source
coefficient lattice. At \(\nu=0\), the symplectic restriction is the ordinary
Grassmannian restriction of \(s_\lambda(y^2)\). Littlewood's 2-quotient
identity says that the coefficient of a target Schur function \(s_\gamma\)
is zero unless \(\gamma\) has empty 2-core; otherwise its row, up to one
overall sign, is the coefficient vector of
\(s_{\gamma^{(0)}}s_{\gamma^{(1)}}\).
Consequently \(P_0(m)\) is generated by the Schur coefficient vectors of products

\[
s_\alpha s_\beta\quad\text{in}\quad
H^{2*}(\operatorname{Gr}(\ell,n);\mathbb Z), \tag{3}
\]

where, on putting \(s=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor\) and
\(p=\lceil\ell/2\rceil\), the two rectangles may be written

\[
\alpha\subset(r-s)^s,\qquad
\beta\subset(c-p)^p,
\qquad |\alpha|+|\beta|=m. \tag{4}
\]

Here the rectangle bounds can be checked directly, independently of the
general filtration construction. Represent
\(\gamma\subset A^\ell\) by its \(\ell\)-bead beta set
\(\{\gamma_i+\ell-i:1\le i\le\ell\}\subset\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\).
An empty 2-core requires \(s\) beads in odd positions and \(p\) in even
positions, as in the beta set of the empty partition. There are \(r\)
available odd positions and \(c\) available even positions. Subtracting the
packed bead positions gives respectively the rectangles
\((r-s)^s\) and \((c-p)^p\), and every such pair occurs. Runner interchange
or the overall row signs do not change the integral row lattice.
This is also the \(t=0\) case of
[TYPE_B_FILTRATION.md](TYPE_B_FILTRATION.md).
We now derive its primitivity and rank over the integers.

## 3. The invariant-subspace variety and its affine Schubert cells

Let \(V=\mathbb C^n\) and choose \(N:V\to V\) with
\(N^2=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}N=r\). Put

\[
I=\operatorname{im}N,\qquad K=\ker N,
\qquad \dim I=r,\quad\dim K=c,
\]

and consider the closed projective variety

\[
W=\{U\in\operatorname{Gr}(\ell,V):N(U)\subset U\}. \tag{5}
\]

We need an integral statement about its cohomology. Here is a lattice model
which supplies it. Let \(R=\mathbb C[[t]]\), \(\epsilon=c-r\in\{0,1\}\), and

\[
L_0=R^c,\qquad L_b=t^2R^r\oplus tR^\epsilon.
\]

Then \(L_0/L_b\cong V\), with multiplication by \(t\) inducing \(N\).
Taking inverse images identifies (5) with the lattices

\[
L_b\subset L\subset L_0,\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb C}(L/L_b)=\ell. \tag{6}
\]

For even \(n\), this rectangular-Jordan-type affine-lattice model is an
established construction: see Proposition 14 and Section 4 of
[Hahn--Nebe--Stanojkovski--Sturmfels, *Subspaces Fixed by a Nilpotent Matrix*](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/shuffle0702final.pdf).
The following direct argument also treats the extra length-1 Jordan block
when \(n\) is odd.

All such lattices belong to one component of the affine Grassmannian of
\(\operatorname{GL}_c\). Choose the Iwahori subgroup of
\(\operatorname{GL}_c(R)\) whose reduction modulo \(t\) is lower triangular,
ordering the exponent-2 coordinates before the optional exponent-1 coordinate.
It preserves both bounds in (6): the only entry which needs an extra factor
\(t\) is an entry from an exponent-1 column to an exponent-2 row, and it is
above the diagonal. Hence \(W\) is Iwahori invariant.

An Iwahori orbit in the affine Grassmannian is an affine Schubert cell. The
bounded closed locus (6) is therefore a finite closed union of such cells,
with their Schubert closures contained in \(W\). In particular, its integral
homology and cohomology are free, vanish in odd degrees, and have Schubert-cycle
bases. The even-cell decomposition also gives an integral surjection

\[
H^*(\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathrm{aff},c};\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H^*(W;\mathbb Z). \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, restriction to a closed union of even Schubert cells is surjective on
cellular cochains, whose differentials are zero. One can work inside finite
Schubert unions in every fixed degree.

The standard affine Bruhat input being used is that these orbits are affine
spaces, their closures are lower unions of orbits, and a cell has regular
representatives given by an ordered product of affine root subgroups.
For the affine flag version, see Remark 2.1.22 of
[Zhu, *An introduction to affine Grassmannians and the geometric Satake equivalence*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.05593).
Projection from the affine flag variety to the affine Grassmannian identifies
the cell of a minimal coset representative with its Iwahori orbit. Its root
subgroup coordinates therefore give a regular map \(z\mapsto g(z)\) into
the Iwahori itself, with \(g(z)L_{\mathrm{coord}}\) equal to the corresponding
point of the cell. In type A the root factors are elementary matrices
\(1+zt^dE_{ij}\); only finitely many factors occur in each cell. This is the
representative property used again in Section 5.

## 4. Integral Chern generation of \(H^*(W)\)

The external input is the main, unnumbered theorem on journal page 728 of
David Anderson's
[Integral equivariant cohomology of affine Grassmannians](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C937C1630E014A51A881243392219146/S0008439524000122a.pdf/integral-equivariant-cohomology-of-affine-grassmannians.pdf),
*Canadian Mathematical Bulletin* **67** (2024), 727–741,
[doi:10.4153/S0008439524000122](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439524000122).
It states that the Sato-Grassmannian embedding induces a surjection on integral
torus-equivariant cohomology. The generators are Chern classes of its universal
index bundle. Section 2.2, page 730, specifies that bundle; page 729 explains
ordinary specialization. Thus ordinary integral affine-Grassmannian cohomology
is generated by these same Chern classes. Only this surjectivity and geometric
identification of the generators are used here.

Choose the Sato indexing so that the reference subspace \(V_{\le0}\) in
Anderson's notation is \(L_0\). On the bounded locus (6), cancellation of
the common subspace \(L_b\) identifies the universal index class exactly as

\[
[L_0]-[L]
=[L_0/L_b]-[L/L_b]
=[V]-[U]=[V/U].
\]

This is an equality of finite virtual bundle classes after the displayed
cancellation. Its Chern classes are integral polynomials in those of \(U\),
since

\[
c(V/U)c(U)=1.
\]

Combining this observation with (7) proves the needed stronger finite statement:

**Lemma 2 (integral ambient restriction).** The ordinary restriction map

\[
H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(\ell,n);\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H^*(W;\mathbb Z) \tag{8}
\]

is surjective.

This argument uses integral cohomology throughout. A rational Chern-generation
statement would not suffice for the Smith calculation.

## 5. A Grassmann-bundle resolution and primitive pullback

Keep \(s=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor\), \(p=\lceil\ell/2\rceil\). Define

\[
Y=\{(F,U):F\subset I,\ \dim F=s,\quad
F\subset U\subset N^{-1}F,\ \dim U=\ell\},
\qquad f:Y\longrightarrow W. \tag{9}
\]

The map is proper and surjective. To see the latter, let
\(j=\operatorname{rank}(N|_U)\le s\). If \(j=s\), take \(F=N(U)\).
If \(j<s\), then

\[
\dim(U\cap I)\ge\ell-j-\epsilon\ge s,
\]

and one can extend \(N(U)\) to an \(s\)-plane \(F\subset U\cap I\).

Over \(\operatorname{Gr}(s,I)=\operatorname{Gr}(s,r)\), let \(F\) also denote
the tautological bundle. The bundle \(E=N^{-1}F/F\) has rank \(c\) and fits into

\[
0\longrightarrow K/F\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow0.
\]

Because \(K\) is trivial, \(c(E)=c(K/F)c(F)=1\). Thus \(Y\) is the relative
Grassmannian \(\operatorname{Gr}(p,E)\). Its integral Grassmann-bundle
presentation gives a ring isomorphism

\[
H^*(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(s,r);\mathbb Z)
\otimes H^*(\operatorname{Gr}(p,c);\mathbb Z), \tag{10}
\]

with the second factor represented by the Chern classes of \(U/F\). In
particular, these groups are free and even. Since the pulled-back universal
\(\ell\)-plane has subbundle \(F\) and quotient \(U/F\), Schur branching reads

\[
s_\lambda(U)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
c_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}s_\alpha(F)s_\beta(U/F). \tag{11}
\]

Consequently the rows of the map from the ambient Grassmannian to (10) are
exactly the Schur-product vectors (3)–(4). Using dual bundles throughout merely
changes uniform degree signs.

The following integral point rules out a hidden index in this identification.

**Lemma 3 (primitive pullback).** The map
\(f^*:H^*(W;\mathbb Z)\to H^*(Y;\mathbb Z)\) is a split injection of abelian
groups in every degree.

For each Iwahori Schubert cell of \(W\), choose its coordinate lattice
representative \(U_0\). Choose a coordinate \(s\)-plane
\(N(U_0)\subset F_0\subset U_0\cap I\), which exists by the preceding
surjectivity argument. Standard root-subgroup coordinates on this cell give a
regular representative \(g(z)\) in the Iwahori subgroup sending \(U_0\) to its
point with coordinates \(z\). The group preserves the two lattice bounds,
commutes with multiplication by \(t\), and preserves \(I\). Therefore

\[
z\longmapsto\bigl(g(z)F_0,g(z)U_0\bigr)
\]

is a regular section of \(f\) over the whole cell. The closure of this section
in the projective variety \(Y\) maps with degree 1 onto the Schubert closure
of the cell. Such closures form an integral homology basis of \(W\), so
\(f_*:H_*(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H_*(W;\mathbb Z)\) is surjective. Dualizing between
free integral groups proves the lemma.

By Lemmas 2 and 3, the image of ambient restriction to \(H^*(Y;\mathbb Z)\)
is exactly \(f^*H^*(W;\mathbb Z)\) and is primitive. A matrix and its transpose
have the same nonzero Smith factors. Equations (10)–(11) therefore prove:

**Corollary 4.** Every nonzero Smith factor of the matrix \(C_{\nu=0}\) is 1,
and

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(C_{\nu=0}\bmod2)
=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}C_{\nu=0}
=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}H^{2m}(W;\mathbb Z). \tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, \(P_0(m)\) is primitive in every rank and degree.

## 6. Counting the cells

Stratify \(W\) by \(j=\operatorname{rank}(N|_U)\). For fixed \(j\), first
choose

\[
D=N(U)\in\operatorname{Gr}(j,I),\qquad
B=U\cap K,\quad D\subset B\subset K,\quad\dim B=\ell-j.
\]

For fixed \(D\), the second choice is
\(B/D\in\operatorname{Gr}(\ell-2j,K/D)\). Once \(D,B\) are fixed, the
possible lifts \(U\) form an affine space modeled on
\(\operatorname{Hom}(D,K/B)\), of dimension \(j(c-\ell+j)\). Thus the stratum
is an affine bundle over a Grassmann bundle, and its compactly supported
Poincare polynomial is

\[
q^{j(c-\ell+j)}{r\brack j}_q{c-j\brack\ell-2j}_q. \tag{13}
\]

Ordinary Grassmannian cells trivialize these bundles, so each stratum has
free compactly supported cohomology concentrated in even degrees, with
polynomial (13). The rank condition gives a closed filtration by
\(\operatorname{rank}(N|_U)\le j\). The compact-support exact sequence for
each successive stratum then splits into short exact sequences in even
degrees; the groups are free and their ranks add. Since \(W\) is projective,
adding (13) therefore computes its ordinary Betti polynomial. Equations
(2) and (12) complete the proof of Deduction 1.

The polynomial in (13) has a classical counting interpretation and should
not be attributed as a new formula here. The Birkhoff subgroup-count
polynomial for ambient type \(\lambda\) and subgroup type \(\mu\) is

\[
\prod_{i\ge1}
q^{\mu'_{i+1}(\lambda'_i-\mu'_i)}
{\lambda'_i-\mu'_{i+1}\brack
 \mu'_i-\mu'_{i+1}}_q.
\]

Substituting
\(\lambda=(2^r,1^\epsilon)\) and
\(\mu=(2^j,1^{\ell-2j})\), whose conjugates are
\((c,r)\) and \((\ell-j,j)\), gives exactly (13).
The same submodule count applies to the nilpotent operator in (5);
the geometric derivation above supplies the needed Betti interpretation.
The exact general formula is displayed in the
[official Sage documentation for `q_subgroups_of_abelian_group`](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/q_analogues.html#sage.combinat.q_analogues.q_subgroups_of_abelian_group),
which cites Butler's 1987 treatment.
The original bibliographic source is
[Birkhoff, *Subgroups of Abelian Groups*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-38.1.385),
*Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* **38** (1935), 385--401.
The audit verified that publication's metadata and the displayed formula in
Sage's documentation; Birkhoff's original full text was inaccessible.
The proof in this note does not depend on an uninspected statement there.

At \(q=1\), formula (1) simplifies to

\[
\sum_m u_{n,\ell}(m)
=\operatorname{coeff}_{x^\ell}\bigl((1+x)^\epsilon(1+x+x^2)^r\bigr). \tag{14}
\]

This is the elementary count in which each length-2 Jordan chain contributes
zero, its bottom vector, or both vectors, and an optional length-1 chain
contributes zero or one vector. It counts the coordinate invariant subspaces,
equivalently the affine Schubert cells of \(W\). Equation (1), rather than a
naive weighted version of this product, supplies their cohomological degrees.

## 7. Scope of the result

The argument above establishes primitivity of the zero-negative-entry lattice
from the stated inputs, without a bound on
\(A\) or \(\ell\). Combined with
[TYPE_B_HIGHEST_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_HIGHEST_SMITH.md), this determines both the
unit multiplicities and the multiplicities of the largest possible factor
\(2^\ell\) in all ranks. Complete formulas for \(\ell\le2\) and
\(\ell=3\) are proved in
[TYPE_B_TWO_ROW_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_TWO_ROW_SMITH.md) and
[TYPE_B_THREE_ROW_SMITH.md](TYPE_B_THREE_ROW_SMITH.md).

The unrestricted formula, now proved separately, is

\[
\sum_m M_e(m)q^m
=
q^{ec}{r\brack e}_q U_{n-2e,\ell-e}(q).
\]

The present deduction proves its \(e=0\) case. It does not establish integral
Chern generation for the larger defect loci, nor primitivity of all the
intermediate lattices \(P_t\). The [general proof](TYPE_B_GENERAL_SMITH.md)
supplies those comparisons by a different integral triangular argument.

The affine-Grassmannian input is a published theorem. Its application to the
specific lattice (3), the primitive-section argument, and the resulting
normalized Springer-image formula are deductions presented here; no priority
claim follows from this computation or from the literature checked so far.

Independent finite corroboration, outside the recorded BGG range, is in
[the unit-frontier report](experiments/type_b_unit_frontier_verification.json).
It computes all 70 degree components for $(A,\ell)=(6,6),(8,4)$
by exact integer Littlewood--Richardson matrices: every nonzero prefix
Smith factor is one, and every rank agrees with (1). Reproduce it from
the package directory with
`sage -python experiments/probe_type_b_qtrinomial.py --unit-frontier`.
These checks support the unit proof; the unrestricted intermediate-layer
statement is now derived in the separate general proof.
