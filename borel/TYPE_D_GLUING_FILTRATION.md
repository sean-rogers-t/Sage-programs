# A filtered row-lattice criterion for full type-D gluing

The general normalized Smith formula and unit-gluing exhaustion are now
proved in [TYPE_D_EVEN_GLUING_THEOREM.md](TYPE_D_EVEN_GLUING_THEOREM.md)
by the relocated-incidence argument. This note retains a separate,
stronger sufficient criterion whose strictness hypotheses remain
conditional; they are not asserted as consequences of that theorem.

**Status: proved conditional lattice theorem, 19 October 2026.**
The linear algebra below is complete. The strictness hypotheses in
Section 4 are not proved for arbitrary type-D parameters. Finite checks
are identified separately and do not establish those hypotheses in
unexamined ranks.

## 1. The unweighted typed rows

Fix an even tail \(k\), a head \(a\), and a polynomial degree. Use
integral Schur source coordinates for the square and Euler sectors, and
write their direct sum as
\[
                         V=V_+\oplus V_-.
\]
Let \(\Gamma_\lambda\) be the unnormalized type-D plus Giambelli
polynomial and \(\Theta_\mu\) the unnormalized symplectic polynomial
for the smaller Euler comparison. If
\(\nu(\lambda)=\#\{i:\lambda_i>k\}\), the actual plus class is
\(2^{-\nu(\lambda)}\Gamma_\lambda\). For a positive-type shape,
removing one part \(k\) gives \(\mu\), and
\(\nu(\lambda)=\nu(\mu)\).

Write \(A_\lambda\in V_+\) and \(B_\mu\in V_-\) for the
unweighted coefficient rows. The unweighted full rows are
\[
 (A_\lambda,0)\quad\text{for an untyped shape},\qquad
 (A_\lambda,B_\mu),\ (A_\lambda,-B_\mu)
 \quad\text{for a typed pair}.
\tag{1}
\]
The corresponding actual Schubert rows are (1) multiplied by
\(2^{\nu(\lambda)}\). This follows from the two typed classes being
the half-sum and half-difference of the plus and minus classes; no
additional factor of two is inserted in (1).

Let \(M_t\subset V\) be the span of the rows (1) with
\(\nu\le t\). Let \(P_t^\pm\) be their sector projection lattices,
and put
\[
                       N_t=P_t^+\oplus P_t^-.
\tag{2}
\]
Zero sectors and repeated steps in these chains are allowed. By adding
and subtracting the two typed rows,
\[
                   2N_t\subset M_t\subset N_t,
\qquad M_t\otimes\mathbb Q=N_t\otimes\mathbb Q.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, when \(N_t\) is primitive in \(V\),
\(N_t=\operatorname{Sat}(M_t)\), and every nonzero unweighted
prefix Smith factor is either \(1\) or \(2\).

The actual full row lattice is exactly
\[
                         R=\sum_{t=0}^a2^tM_t.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed a row of level \(j\le t\) multiplied by \(2^t\) is an
integer multiple of its actual row of weight \(2^j\).

## 2. Conditional filtered lattice theorem

**Theorem.** Suppose
\[
       0\subset N_0\subset N_1\subset\cdots\subset N_a=V
\]
is a chain of primitive sublattices. Suppose \(M_t\subset N_t\) is
a nested chain satisfying (3), and assume:

1. \(M_t=N_t\cap M_a\) for every \(t\).
2. \(N_0+M_a=V\).

Put \(I=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(V/M_a)\), which is defined by (3).
Then the Smith exponents of (4) are the exponents of
\(\sum_t2^tN_t\), except that exactly \(I\) zero exponents are
replaced by ones.

Equivalently, if
\(d_t=\operatorname{rank}N_t-\operatorname{rank}N_{t-1}\),
where \(N_{-1}=0\), the exponent multiplicities of \(R\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\#(0)&=d_0-I,\\
\#(1)&=d_1+I,\\
\#(t)&=d_t\quad(t\ge2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Because every \(N_t\) is primitive, choose an integral basis of
\(V\) adapted to this chain. The quotient map
\(\chi:V\to V/M_a\cong\mathbb F_2^I\) is surjective already
on \(N_0\), by hypothesis 2. Change the integral basis of \(N_0\)
so its first \(I\) basis vectors map to a basis of the quotient and
all its other basis vectors map to zero. Such a change exists because
\(\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_2)\)
is surjective.

Subtract integral combinations of those first \(I\) vectors from
every basis vector outside \(N_0\) to make its quotient image zero.
These changes preserve the adapted chain. In the resulting basis,
\(M_a\) is generated by twice the first \(I\) vectors and by all
the remaining vectors. Hypothesis 1 gives exactly the same description
inside each \(N_t\).

A basis vector first entering the chain at level \(t>0\) therefore
occurs in (4) with coefficient lattice \(2^t\mathbb Z\).
The ordinary vectors in \(N_0\) occur with coefficient
\(\mathbb Z\), while its first \(I\) vectors occur with coefficient
\(2\mathbb Z\). This proves (5). The comparison lattice
\(\sum_t2^tN_t\) has exponent \(t\) on every level-\(t\) vector,
so the asserted replacement rule follows.

For the type-D application, the comparison lattice is the direct sum
of the two separately weighted sector row lattices. Consequently (5)
is precisely the correction that replaces \(I\) pairs \((1,1)\)
of sector factors by \((1,2)\).

## 3. What must be checked integrally

Hypothesis 1 is a strictness assertion, not a consequence of equal
rational ranks. Hypothesis 2 says that every congruence defining the
full unweighted row lattice is detected already on the level-zero
sector prefix.

One useful equivalent test is as follows. Suppose the common index is
\([N_t:M_t]=2^I\) for all \(t\), and \([V:M_a]=2^I\).
If \(N_0+M_a=V\), then \(N_t+M_a=V\) for every \(t\), so
\([N_t:N_t\cap M_a]=2^I\). The containment
\(M_t\subset N_t\cap M_a\) and equality of indices prove strictness.

Equal prefix Smith lists by themselves are insufficient. For example,
take \(V=\mathbb Z e_1\oplus\mathbb Z e_2\),
\(N_0=\mathbb Z e_1\), \(N_1=V\),
\(M_0=2\mathbb Z e_1\), and
\(M_1=\mathbb Z e_1\oplus2\mathbb Z e_2\).
Both quotients \(N_t/M_t\) have dimension one, but
\(M_0\ne N_0\cap M_1\), and level zero detects none of the
quotient \(V/M_1\). Here the weighted lattice has exponents
\((1,2)\), rather than the \((1,1)\) predicted by (5).

## 4. The remaining type-D hypotheses

For an all-rank application, one must prove all of the following for
the actual unweighted typed rows:

- The sector prefixes in (2) are primitive with the rank increments
  that yield their known sector Smith multiplicities.
- Their full unweighted row quotient has dimension equal to the proved
  unit-overlap coefficient
  \[
  [q^m]\;q^{h(\lfloor a/2\rfloor+1)}
       {h+\lceil a/2\rceil-1\brack h}_q,
  \qquad k=2h.
  \]
- The strictness and level-zero detection conditions of Section 2 hold.

These are sufficient conditions. They have not been proved in this
note for arbitrary head and tail sizes. In particular, the known
equality of the separately weighted B/D square row lattices does not
alone establish an equality of every unweighted \(\nu\)-prefix.
One must also respect the actual head in the Euler sector; full images
do not have the square-sector block duality.

The additive scaled-eta lattice should not be treated as a subring.
For example, when \(a=k=2\), put
\(\tau_2=(c_2+p)/2\) and
\(\Gamma_{(3,1)}=c_3c_1-c_4\). Then
\[
             \tau_2^2=c_2\tau_2-\Gamma_{(3,1)}/2.
\]
The scaled additive lattice contains \(\tau_2\), but its pure plus
part contains \(\Gamma_{(3,1)}\) and excludes
\(\Gamma_{(3,1)}/2\). Thus adjoining a central half-sum as a ring
already introduces further denominators.

## 5. A proved insertion identity, and its limit

There is a simple exact identity behind the typed insertion map. Let
\(\mu\) be \(k\)-strict, let \(\nu\) count its parts greater than
\(k\), and insert one part \(k\) at position \(c=\nu+1\) to obtain
\(\lambda\). Embed the old raising-operator indices around this new
position. Then every old pair has the same critical status for the
type-C numerator of \(\mu\) and the type-D numerator of \(\lambda\).

Indeed, for a pair straddling the insertion, its index gap increases
by one, and the type-D weak inequality becomes the type-C strict
inequality. For two parts at most \(k\), both conditions fail. For
two parts greater than \(k\), strictness of those parts gives
\(\mu_i+\mu_j\ge2k+j-i+2\), so both conditions hold.

Every pair between an old high part and the inserted part is critical:
\(\mu_i\ge k+\nu-i+1=k+c-i\). Every pair between the inserted
part and an old low part is noncritical. Consequently, with tagged
Chern factors so the raising operators retain their indices,
\[
\Gamma^D_\lambda=
\left(\prod_{i\le\nu}\frac{1-R_{ic}}{1+R_{ic}}\right)
\left(\prod_{j>\nu}(1-R_{c,j+1})\right)
R^C_\mu\,c_\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(R^C_\mu\) acts on the old positions only. This is an identity
of universal Chern polynomials, before imposing finite-head relations.
Modulo two it simplifies to
\[
\Gamma^D_\lambda\equiv
\left(\prod_{j>\nu}(1+R_{c,j+1})\right)
R^C_\mu\,c_\lambda\pmod2,
\tag{7}
\]
because each high-central factor in (6) is one modulo two.

Thus typed insertion is an ordinary Bernstein insertion among the low
parts, with no high-central correction modulo two. This identifies an
operator to study; it does not prove the strictness in Section 2.
Finite-head relations can mix the high-part filtration. For example,
with one head root and \(k=2\), \(c_4=c_2^2\), although the left
side has a high Chern index and the right side does not. A proof must
control such finite relations rather than assume the stable
high-part filtration survives unchanged.

## 6. Finite exact checks

The separate verifier
[verify_type_d_gluing_filtration.py](experiments/verify_type_d_gluing_filtration.py)
and its
[report](experiments/type_d_gluing_filtration_verification.json)
check the actual unweighted typed prefix modules over \(\mathbb Z\).

criterion. Those calculations corroborate the proposed all-rank
strictness statement; they do not prove it.
