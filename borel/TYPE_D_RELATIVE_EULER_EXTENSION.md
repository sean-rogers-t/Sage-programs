# A primitive relative algebra with the head Euler class

**Status: internally audited complete repository deduction, 19 October 2026.**
This note proves an integral algebra lemma for the relative orthogonal
incidences used in [TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md](TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md).
The [general full-image theorem](TYPE_D_EVEN_GLUING_THEOREM.md)
combines this lemma with the separate B comparison and index argument.
The argument received a fresh internal adversarial review in this session.
It is unpublished, with no originality claim or external certification.

## 1. Geometry and the precise claim

Let \(H,K\geq1\), \(0\leq t\leq H\), \(b=H-t\), \(n=H+K\), and
\(N=K+t=n-b\). Over
\[
T=\operatorname{Gr}(b,\mathbb C^n)
\]
write \(S\) for the tautological bundle and \(W=\mathbb C^n/S\), of rank
\(N\). Let \(F=\operatorname{Fl}(W)\), and put
\[
B=H^*(F;\mathbb Z).
\]
All rings are graded; polynomial degree is half the cohomological degree.
The ring \(B\) is free over \(\mathbb Z\), by integral flag-bundle
Leray--Hirsch.

The oriented orthogonal bundle is the hyperbolic bundle
\(E=W\oplus W^*\), with its orthogonal hyperbolic form. Its compact
oriented real form has Euler class \(\pm c_N(W)\). We distinguish this
orthogonal form from the symplectic form on the quaternionization
\(\mathbb H(W)\). Their characteristic polynomials in squared Chern
roots agree. Write
\[
Q(s)=\prod_{j=1}^N(1+w_j^2s)
      =\sum_{j=0}^N Q_js^j,\qquad Z=\pm c_N(W).
\]
Thus \(Q_N=Z^2\). Choose the Euler orientation signs consistently below.

Consider the relative orthogonal Grassmann bundle
\[
Y=\operatorname{OG}(t,E)\longrightarrow F.
\]
Its tautological \(t\)-plane is \(U\), and its orthogonal residual bundle
has rank \(2K\). Let
\[
e=c_t(U^*),\qquad p=e(U^\perp/U),\qquad
u_i=e_i(z_1^2,\ldots,z_t^2),\qquad
v_j=e_j(y_1^2,\ldots,y_K^2),
\]
where \(z_i\) are the dual head roots and \(y_j\) are orthogonal tail
roots. Normalize signs so that
\[
e^2=u_t,\qquad p^2=v_K,\qquad ep=Z.
\tag{1}
\]
The sign chosen for \(Z\) does not affect any quotient or primitivity
claim.

Define the relative quaternionic ring
\[
R=B[u_1,\ldots,u_t,v_1,\ldots,v_K]\Big/
\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^t u_is^i\right)
      \left(\sum_{j=0}^K v_js^j\right)=Q(s)\right],
\quad u_0=v_0=1.
\tag{2}
\]
This is the usual integral characteristic presentation of
\(H^*(\operatorname{HGr}(t,\mathbb H(W));\mathbb Z)\).
Integral Schur Leray--Hirsch gives a free \(B\)-module with fiber basis
\(\{s_\lambda(u):\lambda\subseteq K^t\}\).
The quaternionic presentation and its topological interpretation are
the published inputs recorded in
[TYPE_B_SMITH.md, Section 1](TYPE_B_SMITH.md#1-a-primitive-symplectic-comparison-lattice).

Let
\[
\mathcal C=R[e,p]/(e^2-u_t,\ p^2-v_K,\ ep-Z),
\qquad
\mathcal A=\operatorname{im}(R+eR\longrightarrow\mathcal C).
\tag{3}
\]
The notation \(R+eR\) denotes an additive image, and does not assert that
its displayed summands are independent. It is also a subring, since
\(e^2=u_t\).

**Lemma.** For \(t\geq1\):

1. The formal ring \(\mathcal C\) injects into integral cohomology of
   \(Y\) as the subring generated by \(B,u_i,v_j,e,p\). In particular it
   is torsion free over \(\mathbb Z\).
2. There is an exact sequence of additive \(R\)-modules
   \[
   0\longrightarrow\mathcal A\longrightarrow\mathcal C
    \longrightarrow R/(u_t,Z)\longrightarrow0.
   \tag{4}
   \]
3. The right hand quotient is free over \(\mathbb Z\). Consequently
   \(\mathcal A\) is primitive in \(\mathcal C\).

For \(t=0\), use \(e=1\), \(R=B\), and \(p=Z\); then
\(\mathcal A=\mathcal C=B\), so the primitivity conclusion is immediate.

Only the stated subring primitivity is proved. It does not assert
primitivity of all rationally invariant integral cohomology classes.

## 2. An integral polynomial freeness lemma

For \(r\geq1\), put
\[
P_r=\mathbb Z[x_1,\ldots,x_r],\qquad
D_r=\mathbb Z[e_1(x^2),\ldots,e_{r-1}(x^2),x_1\cdots x_r].
\tag{5}
\]
When \(r=1\), this means \(D_1=\mathbb Z[x_1]\).

**Polynomial lemma.** The polynomial ring \(P_r\) is a free
\(D_r\)-module with a homogeneous basis including \(1\).

Here is an elementary integral proof. Denote the displayed \(r\)
positive-degree generators by \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\). Over every field
\(\mathbb F\), their only common zero over an algebraic closure is the
origin. Indeed \(f_r=0\) also gives
\(e_r(x_1^2,\ldots,x_r^2)=f_r^2=0\), so all elementary polynomials in
the squared coordinates vanish. Hence every squared coordinate, and
then every coordinate, is zero. This argument also applies in
characteristic two.

It follows that the \(f_i\) are a homogeneous system of parameters in
\(\mathbb F[x]\). A polynomial ring over a field is Cohen--Macaulay,
so they form a regular sequence. The quotient therefore has Hilbert
series
\[
\prod_{i=1}^r(1-q^{\deg f_i})/(1-q)^r
\tag{6}
\]
over every field, including \(\mathbb Q\), independently of the
characteristic. The integral quotient \(P_r/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\) is
finitely generated over \(\mathbb Z\): in this quotient the elementary
polynomials in \(x^2\) all vanish, and the characteristic polynomial
identity gives \(x_j^{2r}=0\) for every \(j\). Each graded piece has
the same dimension after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\) and with every
\(\mathbb F_\ell\). The structure theorem for finitely generated
abelian groups then excludes all prime torsion. The quotient is thus
a finite free graded abelian group.

Choose a homogeneous integral basis of this quotient, with \(1\) as
its degree-zero member, and lift it to \(P_r\). The resulting map from
the free \(D_r\)-module on these lifts onto \(P_r\) is surjective:
reduce a homogeneous polynomial modulo \((f_1,\ldots,f_r)\), then
express the remainder as \(\sum f_i g_i\), and induct on degree.
The generators \(f_i\) are algebraically independent, since they form
a system of parameters over \(\mathbb Q\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), (6)
therefore gives equal Hilbert series for source and target of this
surjection. Its kernel becomes zero over \(\mathbb Q\); the source is
torsion free over \(\mathbb Z\), so its kernel was zero. This proves
the polynomial lemma integrally.

The same statement for the two blocks follows by tensoring their
bases. In particular
\[
P_N=\mathbb Z[z_1,\ldots,z_t,y_1,\ldots,y_K]
\]
is free over the block polynomial ring
\[
D_{t,K}=\mathbb Z[u_1,\ldots,u_{t-1},e,
                         v_1,\ldots,v_{K-1},p],
\tag{7}
\]
with a basis including \(1\). It is also free, with a basis including
\(1\), over the global ring
\[
D_N=\mathbb Z[Q_1,\ldots,Q_{N-1},Z],
\tag{8}
\]
where global \(Q_j=e_j(z^2,y^2)\) and \(Z=(\prod z_i)(\prod y_j)\).
All these statements are about explicit polynomial modules; no
exactness of taking integral Weyl invariants is used.

## 3. The formal ring has no hidden torsion

Use the ambient characteristic classes to make \(B\) a \(D_N\)-algebra.
Eliminating \(u_t=e^2\) and \(v_K=p^2\) from (3) gives
\[
\mathcal C\cong B\otimes_{D_N}D_{t,K}.
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficient relation at degree \(N\) is automatic, since
\(u_tv_K=e^2p^2=Z^2=Q_N\); the remaining coefficient relations and
the relation \(ep=Z\) are precisely the base-change relations in (9).

The block polynomial freeness lemma gives a direct-sum decomposition
\[
P_N=D_{t,K}\cdot1\ \oplus\
       \bigoplus_{\alpha\ne1}D_{t,K}\cdot m_\alpha.
\]
This is also a decomposition of \(D_N\)-modules. After base change it
makes (9) an additive \(B\)-module direct summand of
\[
B\otimes_{D_N}P_N.
\tag{10}
\]
Global polynomial freeness makes (10) a finite free \(B\)-module.
Since \(B\) is free over \(\mathbb Z\), its direct summand
\(\mathcal C\) is torsion free over \(\mathbb Z\).

To connect this formal statement to geometry, pull \(Y\) back to the
relative complete orthogonal flag bundle of \(E\). The coordinate
line classes give a characteristic map from (10) into its integral
cohomology. Rationally this is the usual Borel presentation: the
coordinate classes restrict to generators of the rational fiber
cohomology, and rational Leray--Hirsch gives the presentation with
the ambient invariant classes substituted. Thus the map is an
isomorphism over \(\mathbb Q\).

The target integral cohomology is free over \(\mathbb Z\), by the
even Schubert Serre spectral sequence over \(F\). The torsion-free
source (10) consequently maps injectively. Its subring (9) therefore
maps injectively as well. Its generators are exactly the pullbacks
of the geometric classes \(B,u_i,v_j,e,p\) on \(Y\). Their map on
\(Y\) is injective because its composite with complete-flag pullback
is injective. This proves the first assertion of the main lemma.
The characteristic map need not be an integral isomorphism.

## 4. The exact additive quotient

Every monomial in \(e,p\) reduces using their squares and \(ep=Z\),
so every element of \(\mathcal C\) has a representative
\[
A+eB'+pC',\qquad A,B',C'\in R.
\]
Define
\[
\psi:\mathcal C\longrightarrow R/(u_t,Z),\qquad
A+eB'+pC'\longmapsto C'\bmod(u_t,Z).
\tag{11}
\]
To verify well-definedness without assuming uniqueness of this normal
form, define the map first on polynomial monomials. A monomial with an
even exponent of \(p\) has image zero. A monomial
\(e^{2i}p^{2j+1}\) has image \(u_t^i v_K^j\) modulo \((u_t,Z)\);
a monomial with an odd exponent of \(e\) has image zero.
This is an additive \(R\)-linear map on \(R[e,p]\).
It kills every multiple of \(e^2-u_t\) and \(p^2-v_K\).
It also kills every multiple of \(ep-Z\): the second term has a
factor \(Z\), while a potentially surviving first term has a positive
even power of \(e\), hence a factor \(u_t\). It therefore descends
to (11).

It is surjective and kills \(\mathcal A\). Conversely, if
\(C'\in(u_t,Z)\), then
\[
u_tp=Ze,\qquad Zp=v_Ke
\tag{12}
\]
show \(pC'\in eR\). Reduction to the displayed three-term normal
form now proves \(\ker\psi=\mathcal A\). This proves (4), including
its reverse kernel containment.

## 5. Why the quotient is integrally free

Let \(T'=\operatorname{Gr}(b,\mathbb C^{n-1})\subset T\), using a
fixed hyperplane. Restriction of ordinary Grassmannian cohomology
is surjective with kernel generated by \(c_N(W)\):
\[
H^*(T;\mathbb Z)/(c_N(W))\cong H^*(T';\mathbb Z).
\tag{13}
\]
For example, in the complete-symmetric presentation, adding
\(h_N(S^*)=0\) changes the Grassmann relations
\(h_{N+1},\ldots,h_n\) to \(h_N,\ldots,h_{n-1}\); the remaining last
relation follows by the recurrence. This also covers \(b=0\), when
both sides are \(\mathbb Z\) and \(c_N(W)=0\).

Put \(F'=F\times_TT'\). The integral flag-bundle presentation and its
free Leray--Hirsch basis give
\[
B/(Z)\cong H^*(F';\mathbb Z).
\tag{14}
\]
Over \(T'\), the bundle \(W\) splits as
\[
W|_{T'}=W'\oplus\mathbb C,\qquad W'=\mathbb C^{n-1}/S.
\]
Thus its quaternionization has a specified trivial quaternionic line.
The inclusion of relative quaternionic \(t\)-planes containing that
line is the relative Grassmann bundle
\[
\operatorname{HGr}(t-1,\mathbb H(W'))
\longrightarrow\operatorname{HGr}(t,\mathbb H(W|_{T'})).
\]
Its integral characteristic restriction sets \(u_t=0\) and leaves
the other head and tail classes unchanged. The presentations identify
its cohomology exactly with
\[
R/(Z,u_t).
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed after setting \(u_t=0\) the head has rank \(t-1\), the ambient
bundle has rank \(N-1\), and the last coefficient relation vanishes
automatically; the tail rank stays \(K\). Integral quaternionic
Schur Leray--Hirsch gives a free abelian cohomology group, because
\(F'\) has free even integral cohomology. This proves that (15) is
torsion free, and hence proves the primitivity assertion of the lemma.


## 6. The smaller Euler module and a stronger primitive subalgebra

Write \(c=c_b(S^*)\), pulled back to \(F\), and put \(c=1\) when \(b=0\).
The following stronger statement is useful for an original head Euler
input, whose restriction is \(ce\).

**Stronger lemma.** The additive subalgebra
\[
\mathcal D=R+ceR\subset\mathcal C
\tag{16}
\]
is primitive in \(\mathcal C\). Its two displayed summands are
independent. Moreover multiplication by \(ce\) identifies
\[
R/(Z,v_K)\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ ceR.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the second summand, with its degree shifted by \(b+t\), is the
integral quaternionic Grassmann ring of head rank \(t\) and tail rank
\(K-1\) over \(F'\).

We supply all annihilator and flatness details. In ordinary
Grassmannian cohomology the two top Chern classes satisfy
\[
\operatorname{ann}(Z)=(c),\qquad
\operatorname{ann}(c)=(Z).
\tag{18}
\]
Multiplication by \(c\) adds a full column \((1^b)\) to a Schur
partition. Its image has the Schur basis of partitions with exactly
\(b\) nonzero parts, and its kernel is spanned by partitions whose
first part is \(N\). The latter is precisely the ideal generated by
\(Z=h_N(S^*)\): multiplication by \(h_N\) sends
\(s_\mu\), for \(\mu\) of length at most \(b-1\), to
\(s_{(N,\mu)}\) inside the \(N\)-by-\(b\) Grassmann rectangle.
This proves both statements of (18), integrally. For \(b=0\), (18)
means \(\operatorname{ann}(0)=B\) and
\(\operatorname{ann}(1)=0\).

The free flag-bundle Leray--Hirsch basis extends (18) to \(B\).
It also shows that \(B/(c)\) is free over \(\mathbb Z\):
for \(b\geq1\), it is the cohomology of the restricted flag bundle
over \(\operatorname{Gr}(b-1,\mathbb C^{n-1})\), where the
tautological \(b\)-plane is required to contain a fixed line.
For \(b=0\) the quotient is zero.

The ring \(R\) is free over \(B\). The proof in Section 3 makes
\(\mathcal C\) a direct summand of a finite free \(B\)-module, so
\(\mathcal C\) is projective, and in particular flat, over \(B\).
Tensoring the annihilator exact sequences in (18) with these flat
modules gives
\[
\operatorname{ann}_R(Z)=cR,\qquad
\operatorname{ann}_{\mathcal C}(c)=Z\mathcal C.
\tag{19}
\]
In particular multiplication by \(c\) induces an isomorphism
\[
\mathcal C/Z\mathcal C\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ c\mathcal C.
\tag{20}
\]

Set \(R_0=R/ZR\). Since \(u_tv_K=Z^2=0\) in \(R_0\), the
base change of (3) at \(Z=0\) has the exact additive decomposition
\[
\mathcal C/Z\mathcal C
 =R_0\ \oplus\ e(R_0/(v_K))\ \oplus\ p(R_0/(u_t)).
\tag{21}
\]
This is an integral normal-form statement. To check it directly,
put the multiplication \(e^2=u_t\), \(p^2=v_K\), and \(ep=0\)
on the displayed module. It is well defined because \(u_tv_K=0\).
The natural maps to and from the polynomial quotient are inverse:
they reduce every monomial to one of the three summands and impose
exactly \(v_Ke=0\) and \(u_tp=0\), with no other relation.
Associativity follows from these same relations, or by the two
inverse maps from the associative polynomial quotient.

Equations (20)--(21) immediately give
\[
\ker\bigl(R\xrightarrow{\ r\mapsto cer\ }\mathcal C\bigr)
                         =(Z,v_K),
\]
which proves (17).

For completeness, the square characteristic map \(R\to\mathcal C\)
is injective. Rationally the global Schur classes of the squared head
roots restrict to the independent rectangle Schur classes on each
orthogonal fiber. This is the rational square-sector kernel calculation
used in [TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md](TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md).
Extend that independent fiber set to a rational fiber cohomology
basis, using global rational head Chern and tail Euler polynomials.
Rational Leray--Hirsch then makes their span free over \(B_\mathbb Q\),
so \(R_\mathbb Q\to H^*(Y;\mathbb Q)\) is injective.
Since \(R\) is free over \(\mathbb Z\), the integral map is injective.

We can now identify one more annihilator:
\[
\ker\bigl(R\xrightarrow{\ r\mapsto er\ }\mathcal C\bigr)
                                  =cv_KR.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(er=0\), multiplication by \(p\) gives \(Zr=0\) in the embedded
ring \(R\). By (19), write \(r=cs\). Equation (17) says
\(s\in(Z,v_K)\), and \(cZ=0\), so \(r\in cv_KR\).
Conversely \(ecv_K=cZp=0\), proving the reverse inclusion.

There is an integral involution of \(\mathcal C\) that fixes \(R\)
and sends \(e,p\) to \(-e,-p\). It preserves every defining relation.
The intersection \(R\cap eR\) is therefore zero: its elements would
be both fixed and negated, while \(\mathcal C\) is torsion free.
Using (22), we obtain
\[
\mathcal A=R\oplus eR,\qquad
\mathcal A/\mathcal D
 \cong eR/ceR
 \cong R/(c).
\tag{23}
\]
The right hand quotient is free over \(\mathbb Z\), since \(R\) is
free over \(B\) and \(B/(c)\) is free. Thus \(\mathcal D\) is
primitive in \(\mathcal A\). Section 5 proved that \(\mathcal A\)
is primitive in \(\mathcal C\); their successive torsion-free
quotients prove that \(\mathcal D\) is primitive in
\(\mathcal C\). This completes the stronger lemma.

Finally, (17) has the claimed geometric interpretation. First set
\(Z=0\), restricting to \(F'\) and \(W=W'\oplus\mathbb C\) as in
Section 5. Then \(v_K=0\) is restriction to quaternionic planes
whose complementary \(K\)-plane contains the specified trivial
quaternionic line. This leaves a head \(t\)-plane in
\(\mathbb H(W')\), of ambient rank \(N-1\) and tail rank \(K-1\).
The characteristic presentations agree integrally.


## 7. Primitivity of the specified source for a positive incidence level

Assume \(t>0\), and use integral source symbols for
\[
s_\lambda(x_1^2,\ldots,x_H^2),\quad \lambda\subseteq K^H,
\qquad
e_Hs_\mu(x_1^2,\ldots,x_H^2),\quad \mu\subseteq(K-1)^H.
\tag{24}
\]
The head splits into the \(b\) base roots of \(S^*\) and the \(t\)
residual roots of \(U^*\). Therefore the restrictions of (24) are
\[
s_\lambda(S^{*2}+u),\qquad
ce\,s_\mu(S^{*2}+u).
\tag{25}
\]
Here the first expression lies in \(R\), and the second lies in \(ceR\).

The actual-head prefix primitivity proved in
[TYPE_D_HEAD_EULER_B_COMPARISON.md, Section 2](TYPE_D_HEAD_EULER_B_COMPARISON.md#2-primitivity-for-the-actual-head)
implies that the image of the first family is primitive in the
quaternionic incidence ring before flag-base pullback.
The flag-base pullback is itself primitive: its integral flag
Leray--Hirsch basis contains \(1\). Thus this family remains primitive
in \(R\).

Under (17), the second family becomes the square family
\(s_\mu(S^{*2}+u)\) on the quaternionic incidence ring over
\(\operatorname{Gr}(b,n-1)\), of head rank \(H\), tail rank \(K-1\),
and the same positive level \(t\). Its image is primitive by the
same actual-head prefix theorem, including its \(K-1=0\) point
convention. The additional flag-base pullback again preserves this
primitivity.

The summands in (16) are independent and that ambient subalgebra is
primitive in \(\mathcal C\). Consequently the combined source image
(25) is primitive in \(\mathcal C\).

We record why the source coefficient rows match those used in the
symplectic comparison, integrally. On the symplectic incidence, the
classes \(1,e=c_t(U^*)\) can be included in a global integral
Leray--Hirsch basis for the fiber
\(\operatorname{LG}(t,2t)\): \(e\) restricts to its primitive special
Schubert class, and all fiber Schubert classes have integral Chern
polynomial representatives. The two source families therefore occupy
independent coefficient components \(1\) and \(e\).
Multiplication by \(c\) identifies the second coefficient module
with \(R/ZR\), since \(\operatorname{ann}_R(c)=ZR\).

In that latter module the quaternionic ambient bundle is
\(\mathbb H(W')\oplus\mathbb H\), and restriction to the quotient
by \(v_K\) sends the Schur classes of width at most \(K-1\)
basis-by-basis to the target Schur basis. Classes of greater width
can reduce to base-dependent classes, so no vanishing assertion is
made for those classes.
For every source \(\mu\subseteq(K-1)^H\), branching has the form
\[
s_\mu(S^{*2}+u)
 =\sum_{\eta\subseteq\mu}
       s_{\mu/\eta}(S^{*2})s_\eta(u).
\tag{26}
\]
Every fiber partition \(\eta\) has first part at most \(K-1\).
Thus the restriction \(R/ZR\to R/(Z,v_K)\) is an isomorphism on
the entire free Schur-coordinate submodule containing these source
images. It preserves their integral coefficient matrices, not just
their rational kernels.

It follows that the square and Euler coefficient rows of (25), in
their primitive target sublattices, are exactly
\[
P_t(H,K)\oplus P_t(H,K-1),
\tag{27}
\]
with the Euler degree shifted by \(H\). Equation (27) refers to the
unweighted source coefficient lattice, using the source coordinates
(24). This is the same direct sum as in
[the symplectic calculation, Section 3](TYPE_D_HEAD_EULER_B_COMPARISON.md#3-every-positive-prefix-splits-into-the-two-sectors).
For \(t=0\), the two families lie together in ordinary Grassmannian
cohomology; their overlap must instead be treated by that note's
separate signed SXP argument.

## 8. Integral coefficient projection and the eta denominator

Let \(\mathcal P\subset H^*(Y;\mathbb Z)\) be the subring generated
by \(B\), the Chern classes of \(U^*\), and the tail Euler class \(p\).
There is an integral additive retraction
\[
\rho:\mathcal P\longrightarrow\mathcal C.
\tag{28}
\]
To define it, represent a class by a polynomial in head roots,
symmetric under their permutations, with coefficients in \(B[p]\).
Delete every head monomial except those whose exponent parities are
all even or all odd. This coefficient operation is integral and
commutes with head permutations.

Over \(\mathbb Q\), this operation is the average over the group of
even head sign changes. On the relative complete-flag Borel
presentation, that group fixes all ambient invariant relations:
it fixes the squared-root invariants and also the global Euler
relation \((\prod z_i)(\prod y_j)=Z\). Thus the rational kernel is
stable. If two integral polynomial representatives define the same
class, their projected difference is rationally zero; torsion-free
integral cohomology makes it integrally zero. This proves that the
coefficient operation descends to \(\mathcal P\).

Its image is exactly \(\mathcal C\). The retained symmetric
polynomials have the form
\[
f(u)+e\,g(u)+p\,h(u)+ep\,j(u),
\]
with coefficients in \(B\); powers of \(p\) and \(e\) reduce by
(1). The tail squared classes are integral polynomials in the head
squared classes and ambient classes, by inversion of the two total
squared-root series. Every generator of \(\mathcal C\) is therefore
retained, and \(\rho\) is the identity on it. For \(t=1\) the even
head sign-change group is trivial and every parity pattern is
retained; this agrees with (28). For \(t=0\) both rings are \(B\).

The relative integral eta basis also gives
\[
                        2^tH^*(Y;\mathbb Z)\subseteq\mathcal P.
\tag{29}
\]
Here one must use the full typed basis, rather than assume an integral
outer-involution splitting over the base. After flagging \(W\), the
orthogonal structure bundle reduces to a torus, so the equivariant
typed eta classes give global integral Schubert lifts. They restrict
to the integral fiber Schubert basis, hence form an integral
Leray--Hirsch basis over \(B\).

The explicit denominator bound is the one verified in
[TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md](TYPE_D_SQUARE_THEOREM.md).
In Tamvakis's
[*Double eta polynomials and equivariant Giambelli formulas*](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/dbleta.pdf),
Definition 1 on PDF pp.2--3 and Theorem 1 on PDF p.5 give the factor
\(2^{-\nu}\), where \(\nu\) counts parts greater than \(K\).
For a typed class there is at most one additional central half;
the modified hatted Chern expressions are integral Chern--Euler
polynomials because \(2b_K-c_K=\pm p\).
There are at most \(t\) parts, and a positive type uses a part \(K\),
so \(\nu+1\leq t\) in that case; an untyped class has \(\nu\leq t\).
The geometrization of Section 4.1, PDF pp.19--20, substitutes the
base flag classes and the geometric Chern--Euler classes.
Consequently every global typed class becomes a polynomial in
\(\mathcal P\) after multiplication by \(2^t\), which proves (29).

Together, (25), (28), (29), and the source primitivity in Section 7
have the following precise consequence. If an integral class \(z\)
on \(Y\) lies in the rational span of the specified source, then
even fiber-head sign changes fix it: the square summand is all-even
and the head Euler summand is all-odd. Hence
\[
2^tz=\rho(2^tz)\in\mathcal C.
\]
The source image is primitive in \(\mathcal C\), so \(2^tz\)
belongs to that source image. This controls the full source,
including its specified head Euler component. It does not require
an actual geometric map from \(Y\) to a quaternionic Grassmannian.

## 9. Scope

The primitive relative algebra and specified-source comparison
above supply an integral incidence lemma. A global Smith theorem
must still combine its rows with the level-zero signed overlap
and the restriction comparison between the ambient B and D
varieties. Those steps are separate from this note.

No statement here treats taking invariants on all integral
cohomology as exact. The coefficient projection is defined only
on the indicated polynomial subring; integral descent follows
from rational relation stability and torsion freeness.
