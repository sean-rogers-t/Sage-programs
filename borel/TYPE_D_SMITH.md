# Type D integral tail images: general results and a quadric formula

**Status: Audited deduction (2026-10-19).** The statements and proofs
below are deductions in this repository from the identified published
eta, Schubert, characteristic-map, and quadric results. The audit found
no gap in these arguments under their stated hypotheses; it does not
establish novelty or provide external mathematical certification.
The selected-image conclusions are not attributed to those sources as
published Smith theorems. See [PROOF_STATUS.md](PROOF_STATUS.md) for the
claim-by-claim assessment.

This note proves stable integral primitivity, an explicit finite stable range,
and the absence of odd-prime Smith factors in every rank. It also gives a
complete formula for the one-dimensional-head family.
The subsequent [unit theorem](TYPE_D_FROBENIUS.md) determines every
normalized unit multiplicity in every rank. The
[square comparison](TYPE_D_SQUARE_COMPARISON.md) adds block duality,
an improved square-only stable range and a one-sided B bound. The
[full closed formula](TYPE_D_CANDIDATE.md) remains conjectural in its
higher Smith layers. It does **not** claim
an explicit partition formula for all finite head dimensions. All degrees
below are polynomial degrees, half the cohomological degrees.

## Normalization and scope

Let

\[
X=\operatorname{OG}(a,2n),\qquad k=n-a,
\]

and use the standard coordinate weights \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\). The normalized
tail algebra is generated by

\[
q_i=e_i(x_{a+1}^2,\ldots,x_n^2)\quad(1\leq i<k),\qquad
p=x_{a+1}\cdots x_n.
\]

Here \(\deg q_i=2i\), \(\deg p=k\), and \(p^2=q_k\). Including \(q_k\)
as a redundant input generator gives the same image lattice. Let \(L_d\)
be the integer span of the images of all weighted-degree-\(d\) monomials in
these fixed generators. The Smith factors describe

\[
\operatorname{Sat}(L_d)/L_d,\qquad
\operatorname{Sat}(L_d)=(L_d\otimes\mathbb Q)\cap H^{2d}(X;\mathbb Z).
\]

Only positive nonzero Smith factors are listed, including 1. An empty list
means rank zero. Saturation in the rational image is distinct from spanning
all the ambient cohomology.

## General deduction: stable primitivity and a finite stable range

Fix \(k\geq2\). The normalized map

\[
A_k=\mathbb Z[q_1,\ldots,q_{k-1},p]
\longrightarrow\varprojlim_a
H^*(\operatorname{OG}(a,2(a+k));\mathbb Z)
\]

is injective and has saturated image as an additive subgroup. In finite rank,
every degree-\(d\) normalized Smith factor is **1** whenever

\[
\boxed{a=n-k\geq d.}
\]

In this range the number of factors is

\[
r_{k,d}=[t^d]\frac{1}{(1-t^k)\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(1-t^{2i})}.
\]

This is a sufficient range, not a claim of the least possible rank.
The inverse limit is taken in graded rings, so each assertion concerns an
ordinary finite-dimensional homogeneous component.

### Proof

Use the integral eta-polynomial model \(B^{(k)}\) of stable cohomology.
Its generators satisfy

\[
\eta_r=\vartheta_r\ (r<k),\quad
\eta_k+\eta'_k=\vartheta_k,\quad
\eta_k-\eta'_k=e_k(y),\quad
2\eta_r=\vartheta_r\ (r>k).
\]

The model is an actual subring of the integral symmetric-function ring in an
auxiliary alphabet \(z\), with polynomial coefficients in
\(y_1,\ldots,y_k\). The displayed halves are integral there: the
positive-degree Schur-Q functions have even ordinary monomial coefficients.
Evaluation at \(z=0\) therefore defines an integral ring homomorphism

\[
\rho:B^{(k)}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[y_1,\ldots,y_k]^{S_k},
\qquad
\rho(\eta_r)=e_r(y)\ (r\leq k),\quad
\rho(\eta'_k)=0,\quad
\rho(\eta_r)=0\ (r>k).
\]

The Chern generating series has the form

\[
\vartheta(t)=Q_z(t)E_y(t),\qquad Q_z(t)Q_z(-t)=1.
\]

The stable image of a normalized squared-tail generator is explicitly

\[
f(q_i)=(-1)^i[t^{2i}]\,\vartheta(t)\vartheta(-t)
=e_i(y^2),\qquad
f(p)=\eta_k-\eta'_k=e_k(y),
\]

up to an immaterial overall Euler sign. These are integral polynomials in
the eta generators by the displayed relations. Thus the composite
\(g=\rho\circ f\), where \(f:A_k\to B^{(k)}\), identifies \(A_k\) with

\[
S_k=\mathbb Z[y]^{W(D_k)}
=\mathbb Z[e_1(y^2),\ldots,e_{k-1}(y^2),e_k(y)].
\]

This invariant subring is saturated in \(\mathbb Z[y]^{S_k}\). Indeed,
if \(m v\) is invariant and \(v\) has integral coefficients, then
\(m(wv-v)=0\) for every signed permutation \(w\), so \(wv=v\).
The displayed description over \(\mathbb Z\) follows by separating
all-even and all-odd exponent patterns, then using integral symmetric
polynomials; it requires no averaging by a group order.

Now suppose \(m h=f(u)\) in the stable ring, for a nonzero integer \(m\).
Applying \(\rho\) and using saturation gives \(\rho(h)=g(v)\) for some
\(v\in A_k\). Since \(g\) is injective, \(u=mv\). The stable ring is
torsion-free, whence \(h=f(v)\). This proves stable saturation as well as
injectivity.

The integral typed Schubert basis and its finite specialization are supplied
by [Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, *A Giambelli formula for even orthogonal
Grassmannians*, Theorem 4 and Section 5.2](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/oggiam.pdf).
In our notation specialization retains precisely the typed partitions in the
\(a\times(a+2k-1)\) rectangle. Every partition of size \(d\) fits when
\(a\geq d\). Specialization is therefore an integral isomorphism in that
degree, and the finite statement follows. Counting the free weighted
monomials of \(A_k\) gives the stated coefficient formula.

## General deduction: every finite Smith factor is a power of 2

For every \(a\geq1\), \(k\geq2\), and every degree, all positive normalized Smith
factors belong to \(\{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}\). The same assertion holds for
the \(SO(2)\) tail \(k=1\) if \(W(D_1)\) is interpreted as trivial.
The stable primitivity proof also applies then, with \(A_1=\mathbb Z[p]\).
This boundary Grassmannian requires omitting both fork nodes; it is not a
single-node maximal parabolic.

### Proof

Work over \(R=\mathbb Z[1/2]\), and put

\[
P=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]^{S_a\times W(D_k)},\qquad
I=(e_1(x^2),\ldots,e_{n-1}(x^2),x_1\cdots x_n).
\]

The characteristic-map presentation after inverting the orthogonal torsion
prime is

\[
H^*(\operatorname{OG}(a,2n);R)=P/(I\cap P).
\]

The complete-flag presentation follows from the additive specialization of
[Calmes--Petrov--Zainoulline, Theorems 6.4 and 6.9,
Sections 13.3 and 13.6--13.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.1341).
The latter sections identify the abstract characteristic-map model with
the Chow ring: Section 13.3 verifies the required Schubert-basis hypothesis,
and Sections 13.6--13.7 give geometric surjectivity and the invariant
kernel. Over the complex numbers the cycle map identifies this cellular
Chow ring with integral even cohomology, and hence with its localization
over `R`. Here the torsion index is invertible and `R` has no 2-torsion,
as required by the cited results.
No odd torsion prime occurs; see also
[Totaro, *The torsion index of the spin groups*, Theorem 3.2](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~totaro/papers/public_html/spin.pdf)
for the special orthogonal torsion indices. Here is an explicit justification
of the parabolic step. Let \(\pi:G/B\to G/P\), and choose a polynomial
\(u\) restricting to the normalized point class of its Levi flag fiber, so
\(\partial_{w_{0,P}}u=1\). Such a polynomial exists over \(R\): the
\(GL(a)\) flag has an integral point-class monomial, and the orthogonal
factor has torsion index a power of 2. For \(v\in H^*(G/P;R)\), full-flag
surjectivity supplies \(g\) with \(\beta(g)=\pi^*v\). Then

\[
f=\partial_{w_{0,P}}(ug)\in P,\qquad
\beta(f)=\pi^*\pi_*(\beta(u)\pi^*v)=\pi^*v.
\]

The projection formula therefore gives an invariant lift of every class.
The \(SO\) coordinate character lattice and its simply connected weight
lattice differ only by an index that is a power of 2, which is already a
unit in \(R\). Thus no inversion of the odd factors of \(a!\) or \(k!\)
is involved.

Let \(E=(\mathbb Z/2)^a\) act by independent sign changes on the head
coordinates. Its averaging operator

\[
\mathcal R=2^{-a}\sum_{\epsilon\in E}\epsilon
\]

is defined over \(R\). Although a single head sign change need not preserve
the head-symmetric ring \(P\), the group \(S_a\) normalizes \(E\).
Hence \(\mathcal R\) commutes with \(S_a\), maps \(P\) into itself, and
is an \(R\)-linear retraction onto

\[
P_0=R[e_1(x_{\rm head}^2),\ldots,e_a(x_{\rm head}^2),q_1,\ldots,q_{k-1},p].
\]

The full polynomial ideal \(I\) is stable under every head sign change:
its squared generators are fixed and its Euler generator changes by a sign.
Thus \(\mathcal R\) descends to a retraction on \(P/(I\cap P)\) onto
the image of \(P_0\). Finally the total squared elementary relation gives

\[
e_i(x_{\rm head}^2)=(-1)^i h_i(x_{\rm tail}^2)
\]

in the quotient. The image of \(P_0\) is therefore exactly the selected
tail image, rather than a larger algebra. That image is an \(R\)-module
direct summand of the full cohomology, and in particular is saturated over
\(R\). No odd prime can divide an integer Smith factor. This proves the
claim without determining the individual 2-adic exponents.

## Exact Euler-sector comparison with type B

Let \(a\geq2\), \(k\geq2\), and let \(L_d^-\) be the integer lattice
generated by the classes \(p f(q)\) of polynomial degree \(d\), where
\(f\in\mathbb Z[q_1,\ldots,q_k]\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Smith}(L_d^-\subset H^{2d}(\operatorname{OG}(a,2n);\mathbb Z))
=\operatorname{Smith}_B(a-1,k;d-k).}
\]

The type-B lattice on the right uses normalized squared-tail generators on
\(\operatorname{OG}(a-1,2n-1)\); a negative degree means the empty list.
For \(a=1\), interpret the smaller Grassmannian as a point: the Euler
sector is primitive and occurs only in degree \(k\).

### Proof

Let \(\iota\) be the diagram involution. Its integral anti-eigenlattice is
primitive, being the kernel of \(1+\iota\) in a free abelian group. Its
integral basis consists of

\[
\tau_{\lambda+k}-\tau'_{\lambda+k},
\]

where \(\lambda\) ranges over the Schubert partitions of the smaller odd
orthogonal Grassmannian. Removing one part equal to \(k\) gives precisely
that indexing bijection. The basis correspondence

\[
\psi(\sigma_\lambda)=\tau_{\lambda+k}-\tau'_{\lambda+k}
\]

is therefore an isomorphism of integral abelian groups onto the
anti-eigenlattice. Proposition 2 of the
[Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis paper](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/oggiam.pdf)
states that it respects multiplication by the Chern classes over
\(\mathbb Q\). Since the displayed map and both multiplication operations
are integral and the groups are torsion-free, those identities hold over
\(\mathbb Z\) as well.

The normalized \(q_i\) are the same integral polynomials
\((-1)^i[t^{2i}]c(t)c(-t)\) in these common Chern classes, and
\(\psi(1)=p\) after fixing the Euler sign. Hence

\[
\psi(f(q))=p f(q).
\]

Composing an integral lattice inclusion with a primitive embedding does not
change its nonzero Smith factors. The claimed comparison follows. This is
a statement about the Euler sector on its own; when the square and Euler
sectors occur in the same degree their combined saturation can introduce
additional index-two gluing.

## Quadric deduction for \(a=1\)

In this case \(n=k+1\) and \(X=Q^{2k}\) is the smooth even-dimensional
quadric. For \(k\geq2\), its normalized tail Smith-factor list in degree
\(d\geq0\) is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{condition}&\text{Smith factors of }L_d\\ \hline
d=0 &(1)\\
0<d<k,\ d\text{ even}&(1)\\
d=k,\ k\text{ odd}&(1)\\
d=k,\ k\text{ even}&(1,2)\\
k<d\leq2k,\ d\text{ even}&(2)\\
\text{all other cases}&()
\end{array}}
\]

The same table holds for \(k=1\): then the image is generated by \(p\)
alone and its three nonzero degrees have factors \((1),(1),(2)\).

In particular, when \(k\) is odd the image in middle degree has rank one
inside a rank-two ambient group. Its factor \((1)\) means that this line is
primitive, not that the image is all middle cohomology.

## Proof

Write \(h\in H^2(Q^{2k};\mathbb Z)\) for the hyperplane class, and let
\(\alpha,\beta\in H^{2k}(Q^{2k};\mathbb Z)\) be the classes of the two
rulings of maximal linear subspaces. The standard integral quadric basis is

\[
1,h,\ldots,h^{k-1};\quad\alpha,\beta;\quad
h\alpha,h^2\alpha,\ldots,h^k\alpha.
\]

Its relations include

\[
h^k=\alpha+\beta,\quad h\alpha=h\beta,\quad
(\alpha-\beta)^2=(-1)^k h^{2k},\quad
h^{2k}=2[\mathrm{point}].
\]

Thus \(h^d\) is primitive below the middle, while

\[
h^d=2h^{d-k}\alpha\qquad(k<d\leq2k)
\]

is twice a primitive class above the middle. These integral basis and
intersection formulas are stated explicitly in
[Dimitrov--Roth, *Intersection multiplicity one for classical groups*,
Section 2.8, equation (2.8.1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06840).

The total elementary relation in the squared coordinate weights gives

\[
q_i=(-1)^i h^{2i}.
\]

The diagram involution fixes \(h\), interchanges \(\alpha,\beta\), and
negates \(p\). Its anti-invariant middle line is therefore spanned by
\(\alpha-\beta\), so write \(p=c(\alpha-\beta)\) over \(\mathbb Q\).
Since

\[
p^2=q_k=(-1)^k h^{2k}
=(\alpha-\beta)^2\ne0,
\]

we have \(c^2=1\). After interchanging the ruling labels if necessary,

\[
p=\alpha-\beta,\qquad hp=0.
\]

For \(k\geq2\), the image algebra is consequently generated by
\(h^2\) and \(\alpha-\beta\). Every even power of \(p\) is a signed
power of \(h^2\); every odd power beyond the first vanishes because
\(hp=0\). Hence:

- in even degree away from the middle the image is generated by \(h^d\);
- in odd degree only \(d=k\) can occur, where the image is generated by
  \(\alpha-\beta\);
- when \(d=k\) is even, the two generators are
  \(\alpha+\beta\) and \(\alpha-\beta\).

The first two cases give the factors in the table. In the final case the
coefficient matrix on the primitive basis \((\alpha,\beta)\) is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose Smith factors are \((1,2)\). For \(k=1\), the same conclusion follows
directly from \(p=\alpha-\beta\), \(p^2=-2[\mathrm{point}]\), and
\(p^3=0\). This proves every case.

## Literal polynomial-character form

For the defining/vector Springer map on the \(SO(2k)\) tail, the literal
integral polynomial-character intersection uses the different generators

\[
\widetilde q_i=4^i q_i,\qquad \widetilde p=2^k p.
\]

Each is \(2^{\deg}\) times its normalized counterpart. Every monomial of
weighted degree \(d\) therefore has the common factor \(2^d\), giving

\[
L_d^{\mathrm{literal}}=2^d L_d^{\mathrm{normalized}}.
\]

Multiply every nonzero factor in the quadric table by \(2^d\) to obtain the
literal Smith factors. This scaling identity also holds for arbitrary
\(a\), independently of whether an explicit normalized Smith-factor
formula is known. It refers to the fixed defining/vector Springer map,
not to maps constructed from spin representations.

## Why the arbitrary-head case needs more work

For general \(a\), the diagram involution gives a decomposition over
\(\mathbb Q\) into square-tail classes and Euler multiples. The integral
cohomology lattice does not, in general, split as the sum of its integral
eigenlattices: a swapped pair of typed Schubert classes has sum and difference
of index 2. The middle-degree \((1,2)\) in the theorem is the simplest
example of this obstruction.

[Buch--Kresch--Tamvakis, *A Giambelli formula for even orthogonal
Grassmannians*, Proposition 2 and Theorem 2](https://math.umd.edu/~harryt/papers/oggiam.pdf)
provide the relevant rational eigenspace comparison and typed Giambelli
formulas. They do not by themselves justify taking a union of Smith factors
computed independently in the two eigenspaces. A general partition formula
must also prove the integral change of basis and account for this gluing.
Finite computations support further investigation; they are not a proof of
such a formula.

The all-rank lattice reconstruction, including the precise index-two gluing
and its flags over \(\mathbb F_2\), is proved in
[ORTHOGONAL_SMITH.md](ORTHOGONAL_SMITH.md). Together with the exact
Euler-sector comparison above, it determines what further integral data a
general formula must supply.

The [finite sector calculations](experiments/orthogonal_sector_search_results.json)
also give the same Smith lists for the square-only type-D sector and the
type-B image on \(\operatorname{OG}(a,2n+1)\) in every recorded component.
That square-sector identification remains an observed pattern in this work.
Proposition 1 gives its rational setting; proving preservation of the
integral saturation requires an additional argument. The Euler-sector
comparison proved above already has that integral justification.
