# A rational presentation of the joint two-step image

This note derives the relations tested by the two-step experiment. It is a
consequence of standard rational invariant theory and the Borel presentation;
it is not a claim of a new theorem or a completed literature review.

Let `a,b,k >= 1`, `n=a+b+k`, and let `F` be the symplectic partial flag variety

$$
F=\{U\subset V\subset\mathbb C^{2n}:\dim U=a,\ \dim V=a+b,
\ V\text{ isotropic}\}.
$$

Its Levi has factors `GL(a) x GL(b) x Sp(2k)`. Split the standard Cartan
coordinate alphabet into blocks `A`, `M` and `T` of sizes `a`, `b` and `k`.
Write

$$
c_j=e_j(M)\quad(1\leq j\leq b),\qquad
e_i=e_i(T^2)\quad(1\leq i\leq k),\qquad
B=\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_b,e_1,\ldots,e_k].
$$

Here `M^2` and `T^2` mean the squared coordinate alphabets. These formal
generators are algebraically independent before applying the characteristic map,
and have polynomial degrees `j` and `2*i`. Let `Z=M^2 union T^2`.

## Kernel statement

For the restriction of the Borel map to `B`,

$$
\ker(\beta|_B)=\bigl(h_{a+1}(Z),h_{a+2}(Z),\ldots,h_n(Z)\bigr).
$$

The `h_r(Z)` are viewed as polynomials in `c_j,e_i`, using elementary symmetric
functions of the squared middle coordinates. In particular this is a statement
over `QQ`; no assertion about integral lattices is made here.

## Derivation

The rational Levi-invariant ring is

$$
R=\mathbb Q[e_1(A),\ldots,e_a(A),c_1,\ldots,c_b,e_1,\ldots,e_k].
$$

The rational Borel presentation identifies `H*(F;QQ)` with `R/I`, where `I`
is generated by the full symplectic Weyl invariants

$$
F_r=e_r(A^2\cup M^2\cup T^2),\qquad 1\leq r\leq n.
$$

Introduce the subring

$$
R_0=B[p_1,\ldots,p_a],\qquad p_i=e_i(A^2).
$$

In the ambient coordinate polynomial ring, average polynomials in `R` over
signed permutations of the head block `A`. Individual signed permutations need
not preserve `R`; their average lands in the invariant subring below.
This Reynolds operator takes `R` to `R_0`, fixes `R_0` and is `R_0`-linear.
Each `F_r` lies in `R_0`. Consequently

$$
(F_1,\ldots,F_n)R\cap R_0=(F_1,\ldots,F_n)R_0.
$$

Indeed, applying the average to an expression `f=sum F_r*g_r` for `f` in
`R_0` replaces every coefficient `g_r` by one in `R_0`. Thus passing from
unsquared to squared head invariants introduces no extra relations on `B`.

Now put `E_Z(t)=prod_(z in Z)(1+z*t)`. The equations `F_r=0` are exactly
the positive-degree coefficients of

$$
(1+p_1t+\cdots+p_at^a)E_Z(t)=1.
$$

The first `a` equations eliminate the head generators, giving
`p_i=(-1)^i h_i(Z)`. After this substitution, the remaining equations are
triangular combinations of `h_(a+1)(Z),...,h_n(Z)`, with nonzero leading
coefficients. They generate exactly that ideal. Hence

$$
R_0/(F_1,\ldots,F_n)\simeq
B/(h_{a+1}(Z),\ldots,h_n(Z)),
$$

and the contraction calculation proves the kernel statement.

## Hilbert series and sharp degree bound

The quotient above is finite-dimensional: the contraction embeds it in the
finite-dimensional cohomology `R/I`. Its ideal is generated by `b+k`
homogeneous polynomials in `b+k` polynomial variables. They are a homogeneous
system of parameters, hence a regular sequence in the polynomial ring `B`.
Therefore the Hilbert series of the joint image is

$$
\operatorname{Hilb}(\operatorname{im}\beta|_B;t)=
\frac{\prod_{r=a+1}^{n}(1-t^{2r})}
{\prod_{j=1}^{b}(1-t^j)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-t^{2i})}.
$$

The first nonzero kernel degree is exactly `2(a+1)`: the first displayed
generator has that degree, it is nonzero, and every other ideal generator has
higher degree. Nonvanishing can be seen by setting all middle generators and
all tail generators except `e1` to zero; `h_(a+1)(Z)` becomes `e1^(a+1)`.

Thus the finite map is injective through polynomial degree `D` precisely when

$$
D<2(a+1).
$$

Equivalently, for this genuine two-step family and fixed `b,k`, the least
eligible rank giving injectivity through degree `D` is

$$
n=b+k+\max\{1,\lfloor D/2\rfloor\}.
$$

Cohomological degree is twice polynomial degree. The total dimension of the
joint image, obtained by taking `t` to 1, is

$$
2^b\frac{n!}{a!\,b!\,k!}.
$$

The degree bound grows with `a`; it gives degreewise injectivity in the stable
limit whenever the maps are assembled using the natural compatible flag
pullbacks. The tail factor alone is pulled back from the isotropic Grassmannian
obtained by forgetting `U`; the joint image also records mixed middle/tail
relations.

## Small example

For `a=b=k=1`, `n=3`, the kernel may be written either

$$
(h_2(Z),h_3(Z))
=(c_1^4+c_1^2e_1+e_1^2,\ e_1^3).
$$

The image Hilbert series is

$$
1+t+2t^2+2t^3+2t^4+2t^5+t^6+t^7.
$$

The generic calculator independently recovers the homogeneous kernel spaces;
the experiment compares its ranks to this series. Low-rank tests also compute
the entire kernel spaces by Groebner reduction in orthonormal coordinates.

## Context

The general Springer-induced map and its compatibility with flag projections
are in [Kumar, Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06826) and
[Kumar--Rogers, Part II](https://kumar.math.unc.edu/papers/kumar81.pdf).
Classical partial-flag presentations and Giambelli formulas are developed in
[Tamvakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3628). The related complete-symmetric
presentations for quaternionic Grassmannians are in
[Panin--Walter](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/LAG/man/405.pdf).
The derivation here uses the rational Borel presentation directly and does not
depend on the finite experiment establishing a theorem.
